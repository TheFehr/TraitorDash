# RFC 14: Revivals and Role Changes in Rounds, Statistics and Replays

> **Status: draft (2026-10-20), revised the same day after review.** It extends [RFC 12](./RFC-012.md) (the round replay and its track format) and corrects a part of the round report of the stats feature, which no RFC describes (the code is `addon/lua/traitordash/stats.lua` and `POST /api/v1/round` in `backend/src/routes/api.tsx`).

## 1. Abstract
A round is more than "alive until dead". A **defibrillator** brings a player back. In TTT2 a **role can change during the round**: a deputy becomes sheriff, a player revived by a necromancer rises as a zombie, a sidekick joins the jackal. Today the bridge reads each player's role and team **once, at round start**, so the report and the replay show the start state for the whole round, the win check uses the start team, and a revival leaves no trace except a gap in the track. This RFC:

1.  records **who revived whom, and how** (the defibrillator or the role's own power), in the round report and in the replay;
2.  records **role and team changes** with their times;
3.  uses the **final team** for the win check;
4.  adds **statistics by final role** next to the existing ones by starting role;
5.  shows all of it in the replay.

No new service and no setting. The new fields are optional, so old bridges, old backends and old tracks keep working.

## 2. Motivation
*   **The replay is meant to settle "what happened".** A zombie drawn with the innocent colour, or a sheriff still called a deputy, misleads the viewer. A figure that vanishes at a death and reappears elsewhere, with nothing saying who brought it back or with what, leaves the main question open ("who defibbed the traitor?", "was that the necro or a normal defib?").
*   **The win check is wrong for changed teams.** `won` is "the player's start team equals the winning team" (`stats.lua`). A sidekick who started as an innocent and won with the jackal is counted as a loss; a player who started as a traitor and was converted away is counted as a win. This affects the existing statistics, not only the replay.
*   **Role statistics ignore what the role did.** A zombie who started as an innocent is today an innocent in every table, and the zombie role never shows a win rate. Both views are useful: "how do players who start as X do" (the role as dealt) and "how do players who end as X do" (the role as played).
*   **The cost of doing it now is low.** The track format has just shipped and has few stored tracks. Fields added now are optional; later they would need a migration of every stored track.

## 3. Current State (summary, not a change)
*   `Begin` in `stats.lua` stores, for every player alive at the round start, `role` (`GetSubRoleData().name`) and `team` (`GetTeam()`). Nothing updates them.
*   `survived` is "alive when the round ends", so a revived player counts as a survivor. This is already right.
*   `track.lua` samples every tracked player that is alive (`IsTerror()`). When a player dies the segment ends; when the player is alive again, a new segment starts. A revival is recorded as a gap followed by a new segment, **but nothing marks it as a revival** (as opposed to a skipped tick), and nothing says who or what caused it.
*   The viewer colours a figure by the start team (`teamColour`) for the whole round.
*   The role and team tables (`getRoleStats`, `getTeamStats`) group by the stored start `role` and `team`.
*   **How a TTT2 revival works**, read on 2026-10-20 from the public sources of TTT2 (`TTT-2/TTT2`, `master`) and of the defibrillator addon (`TTT-2/ttt2-wep_defi`, `master`), and on 2026-10-15 from the installed necromancer role (that role's code is not in this repository). **The installed files on the real server were checked on 2026-10-20** (read-only, the Workshop archives in the game server's volume): the installed TTT2 core (workshop 1357204556, file dated 2026-04-25) contains `events.Trigger(EVENT_RESPAWN, ply)`, `plymeta:Revive`, and `hook.Run` for `TTT2UpdateSubrole`, `TTT2UpdateTeam` and `TTT2AddedEvent`; the installed defibrillator (2115944312) has the same `TTT2AttemptDefibPlayer(owner, ent, self)` call, `revivalReason = "revived_by_player"` and `ply:Revive(...)`. Line numbers differ from `master`, the calls are the same. Details:
    *   `plymeta:Revive(delay, OnRevive, DoCheck, needsCorpse, blockRound, OnFail, spawnPos, spawnEyeAngle)` starts a timer and, when it ends, calls `SpawnForRound(true)` and then the `OnRevive` callback. It takes **no argument that names the reviver**.
    *   `SpawnForRound` triggers TTT2's own `EVENT_RESPAWN` (an active player) or `EVENT_SPAWN`, and `events.Trigger` runs `hook.Run("TTT2AddedEvent", name, event)` with the new event. The respawn event carries the nick and the SteamID64.
    *   `plymeta:SetRole` runs the hooks `TTT2UpdateSubrole(ply, oldSubrole, newSubrole)` and `TTT2UpdateTeam(ply, oldTeam, newTeam)` (also `TTT2UpdateBaserole`), then `EVENT_ROLECHANGE`.
    *   The defibrillator's `PrimaryAttack` runs `hook.Run("TTT2AttemptDefibPlayer", owner, ragdoll, self)` (**a non-nil answer blocks the revival**), then `BeginRevival` calls `ply:Revive(reviveTime, ...)` on the victim and `SendRevivalReason(self.revivalReason, { name = owner:Nick() })`. After the revive time `FinishRevival` calls `SWEP:OnRevive(ply, owner)`; a failure chance can cancel it. `SWEP.revivalReason` is `revived_by_player`.
    *   The necromancer's weapon (`weapon_ttth_necrodefi`) overrides `OnRevive` and makes the victim a zombie there; per the earlier notes the role is set in the same tick as the respawn.
    *   **Roles that bring themselves back** (installed on the real server, found by `Revive(` in their code): haunter (`ttt2-role_haunt`), sgrunt, cursed, flood, sbarrels, speedrunner, beggar, swapper, and the SG-1 pack. Each has a `Revive(...)` call in its own code (only the call lines were read, not the surrounding logic; the owner reports that some bring the player back when their killer dies); none of them contains the defibrillator hook. Whether any of them respawns a player by another route (`SpawnForRound`) was not searched for in the Workshop archives. They are the "source unknown" case below, and they are the reason it must not be just "unknown".
*   Not yet run: the bridge against a real defibrillator, zombie or deputy. The bot test only produces kills.

## 4. Design

### 4.1 Role changes and revivals, from TTT2's own signals
Three public signals (3), no patching of TTT2:

| Signal | Used for |
|---|---|
| hooks `TTT2UpdateSubrole(ply, old, new)` and `TTT2UpdateTeam(ply, old, new)` | a `role` event, at the exact time |
| hook `TTT2AddedEvent(name, event)` with the respawn event (`event.event.sid64`) | a `revive` event, at the exact time |
| the per-tick comparison "not alive at the previous tick, alive now" | the same `revive`, as the fallback |

*   **A role change** is recorded when either hook fires for a player in the round after the round has begun. The role assignment at the start fires the same hooks and is ignored. A change of role and team happens in one call that fires both hooks, so the handler schedules itself for the next tick (`timer.Simple(0)`), reads the settled role and team once, and records one event when it differs from the last recorded state.
*   **A revival** is a respawn event of a player who died in this round. The per-tick comparison that the sampler already makes covers a revival by a path that fires no event; when both see the same revival it is one event (the hook's), and a revival seen only by the comparison has the time of its tick (0.2 to 1 s late) and no source.
*   A revival and a role change at the same instant (the zombie case) get the same time. The order they happen in differs between role addons, so the viewer does not depend on it.
*   The round report gets `end_role` and `end_team` (4.4): the same role handler keeps each player's last settled state, so a player who dies or leaves keeps theirs.
*   The tracked set stays the players alive at the round start: a player cannot enter a TTT2 round alive in any other way than by being revived, which is covered. If an untracked player is ever seen alive, the bridge writes one debug line per round and does nothing else.
*   Cost: nothing per tick for roles (they are events), a table lookup per respawn event, and the comparison the sampler already does.

### 4.2 Attribution: who revived whom, and how
The defibrillator addon announces every attempt with `hook.Run("TTT2AttemptDefibPlayer", owner, ragdoll, defibrillator)` before it starts the revival (3). That names the **reviver** (`owner`), the **victim** (the player of the ragdoll, `CORPSE.GetPlayer(ragdoll)`), and the **how** (the weapon's class, and its `revivalReason`).

*   The bridge keeps a **note** per victim: reviver, weapon class, reason key, time. A revival of that victim within **30 seconds** takes the note (the defibrillator's default revive time is 3 s; a role may configure more) and uses it once. An attempt that fails leaves a note that expires; a second attempt replaces the first.
*   The necromancer's defibrillator derives from the same weapon and fires the same hook, so it is covered; its `revivalReason` is `revived_by_necromancer`, the normal one's is `revived_by_player`.
*   **The handler must return nothing.** In the defibrillator a non-nil answer from this hook blocks the revival (`~= nil` → error). A test pins this.
*   **A revival with no note** (a role that revives itself, an admin's respawn, a weapon that does not fire the hook) is recorded with the fact and the time and **no `by`**. Its `how` is **`role:<name>`, the role the player had when they died** (the bridge keeps it from the death; for the roles above this is the role that revived them, even when the role changes on revival, as the swapper's does). The one case it would be wrong for is an admin's forced respawn, which has no marker; that is accepted and said in the interface ("by their role, or an admin") rather than pretended away.
*   **"Their killer died":** the round page and the replay add, for any revival, a fact read from the report: the revival came N seconds after the death of the player who last killed the revived player, when that death is in the same round and within 30 seconds before the revival ("came back 2 s after their killer, Name, died"). It is a sequence of recorded events, not a claimed cause, and it needs nothing from the role addon. There is no guessing from who stood nearby; a wrong name in a replay is worse than none.
*   **Fields:** `by` (the reviver's SteamID64 as in the round; absent when unknown), `how` (the weapon class, up to 64 characters), `why` (the reason key). A reviver who is not in the round (a bot excluded by the bot setting) is absent.
*   A revival that fails after the attempt (the defibrillator has a configurable failure chance) produces no respawn and so no event.

### 4.3 Events in the track
The upload (RFC 12, 3.3) gets one optional array, `events`, sorted by time:

```jsonc
"events": [
  { "t": 31000, "p": "7656119...", "k": "role", "role": "sheriff", "team": "innocents" },
  { "t": 94000, "p": "7656119...", "k": "revive", "by": "7656119...", "how": "weapon_ttth_necrodefi", "why": "revived_by_necromancer" },
  { "t": 94000, "p": "7656119...", "k": "role", "role": "zombie", "team": "necromancers" }
]
```
*   `t` is milliseconds since the round began, as in `deaths`. `p` is a player ID that is in the round. `k` is `revive` or `role`. `role` and `team` are present for `role` only. `by`, `how` and `why` are allowed for `revive` only and absent when unknown. Names: up to 64 characters of letters, digits, underscore, dot and hyphen (the filter the weapon name uses). `by` must be a player in the round or absent.
*   **At most 1,024 events** per track. Beyond it the recording stops adding events and sets `truncated`. Revivals and role changes are rare in real rounds; a role that flips every tick would hit the cap by design instead of growing the upload.
*   A death does not need an event: it is in `deaths`. A revival with no earlier death in the track (lost to a skipped tick or the cap) is still recorded.
*   The segments are unchanged. A revival remains a gap and a new segment; the event says why.

### 4.4 The round report: revivals, final role and team, the win check
`POST /api/v1/round` gets three optional additions. They belong to the report, not the track, because the track is opt-in and statistics must work without it.

*   **`revives[]`**, like `deaths[]`: `{ victim, reviver, how, why, at }` with `at` in seconds since the round began, at most 512 per round. Victim and reviver are players in the round (a reviver that is not is stored as `NULL`). Recorded by the bridge whenever the stats are on, with or without tracking.
*   **`end_role` and `end_team`** on each player: the last state the bridge saw. Read at the round end for players in the list, and kept from the last sighting for a player who has gone.
*   **`won` uses the end team.** A player won if their `end_team` is the winning team. When no end team is known the start team is used, as today.
*   `role` and `team` keep **the same meaning as today (the start state)**, so no stored row changes meaning.
*   Old rows are not backfilled: the information was never recorded.
*   Everything is optional on the receiving side: a round without the new fields is accepted exactly as now.

### 4.5 Statistics by final role and team
*   The role and team tables get a switch **"as dealt" / "as played"** (query `?by=start|end`, default `end`). *As dealt* is today's table. *As played* groups by `COALESCE(end_role, role)` and `COALESCE(end_team, team)`, so old rows, which have no end state, count as dealt.
*   **The default is *as played*.** It is the more accurate picture (a converted player is counted where they ended, with the team that won or lost), and nothing is lost by choosing it: there is no history to keep stable, and rows without an end state count as dealt, so the two views are identical until a role changes. *As dealt* stays one click away. The page remembers the choice in the link only.
*   The same switch applies to the roles table on a player's page. Rounds played are equal in the two views (every player appears once in each), so only the grouping changes.
*   A round's page shows, for a player whose role or team changed, "Zombie (was Innocent)", and lists the revivals: "Name was revived by Name with Defibrillator at 4:12".
*   A player's page gets two numbers: **times revived** and **revivals done**.
*   Rounds before the update show neither the changed state nor revivals, and the tables say how many rows have no end state (a count under the switch), so the "as played" numbers are not mistaken for complete.

### 4.6 The viewer
*   **Colour by the current team.** The figure, its pin and trail use the team the player has at the scrubber time (start team, then each `role` event's team). A conversion is a colour change at that moment.
*   **Role in the player panel**: the role at the scrubber time with the earlier ones ("Zombie, was Innocent"), and a list of the selected player's events with times that jump the scrubber.
*   **Markers on the scrubber** for revivals (a mark distinct from the death one) and role changes, with a tooltip ("Name revived by Name, Defibrillator"), next to the death markers.
*   **A revival is drawn**: a short ring at the revival position and, when the reviver is known, a line from the reviver to the revived at that moment. The gap in the trail remains a gap (RFC 12: never a straight line across it). An unknown source is shown as "source unknown".
*   A track without `events` (every track stored before this RFC, or a bridge that sends none) draws as today, with the start colours.
*   The fixed line about the mesh not being a line-of-sight oracle (RFC 12) is unchanged.

### 4.7 What it does not change
*   No change to the sampling controller, the budget, the caps on ticks, players and segments, the retention, or the access tiers (RFC 13). Events travel in the same upload and are covered by the same size limit.
*   No change to what is public: the round page already shows teams and roles after the round; the new rows show the same kind of information. Revivals name two players, as deaths do. **Decided: there is no option to hide a name** in replays or revival lists (this also closes the question left open in RFC 12); access stays as RFC 13 sets it.

## 5. Data Model
*   **Track:** `events` is part of the stored JSON (`round_tracks.data`); no migration. `events` absent is read as empty.
*   **Migration v15:**

```sql
ALTER TABLE round_players ADD COLUMN end_role TEXT;
ALTER TABLE round_players ADD COLUMN end_team TEXT;

CREATE TABLE round_revives (
  server_id  TEXT NOT NULL,
  round_id   TEXT NOT NULL,
  victim     TEXT NOT NULL,              -- SteamID64, as in round_players
  reviver    TEXT,                       -- NULL: unknown
  how        TEXT,                       -- weapon class or source, NULL: unknown
  why        TEXT,                       -- the weapon's revival reason key, if any
  at_seconds INTEGER NOT NULL,
  FOREIGN KEY (server_id, round_id) REFERENCES rounds(server_id, round_id) ON DELETE CASCADE
);
CREATE INDEX idx_round_revives_round ON round_revives (server_id, round_id);
CREATE INDEX idx_round_revives_reviver ON round_revives (server_id, reviver);
```
The two player columns are nullable, with no default and no index (the grouping reads the whole table per server, as the existing queries do). Round pruning and server deletion remove revivals with the round, as they do deaths.

## 6. Compatibility and Rollout
*   **Bridge older than backend:** sends no events, no `revives`, no `end_*`; everything is as today.
*   **Backend older than bridge:** the round validator ignores unknown fields and the track validator builds a new object from the fields it knows, so the new data is dropped. Both are assumptions about current code that a test must pin (section 7).
*   **The corrected win check applies from the day the bridge is updated.** Rounds before it keep their old `won`. The README gets a one-line change note with the date.
*   **Hooks, not patches.** Everything is built on public hooks (`TTT2UpdateSubrole`, `TTT2UpdateTeam`, `TTT2AddedEvent`, `TTT2AttemptDefibPlayer`); nothing replaces a TTT2 function. A missing hook (an older TTT2) leaves the feature working at a lower resolution: the revival is still seen by the per-tick comparison, and `by` and `how` are unknown.
*   Delivery in order: (1) backend: validation and storage of `events`, `revives`, `end_*`, migration v15, the switch on the statistics, tests on synthetic data; (2) bridge: the role and respawn hooks, events, the report fields, the corrected `won`, then the defibrillator note, with the Fengari harness, after checking the installed TTT2 and defibrillator versions on the real server against the sources in 3; (3) viewer and round page; (4) a real round with a defibrillator and a role addon on a real server, as the acceptance test.

## 7. Testing
*   **Backend, track:** a track with valid events is stored and returned unchanged; events out of order, a time beyond the round limit, a player not in the round, an unknown kind, a role or team with other characters or over 64, a missing role on a `role` event, `role` on a `revive` event, `by` not in the round, more than 1,024 events and a non-array `events` are each a `400` that stores nothing; a track without events is still valid.
*   **Backend, report:** a round with `revives` and `end_*` is stored; `revives` over 512 and a malformed victim are a `400` that stores nothing; a victim not in the round is dropped, as a death is; a reviver not in the round, or a `how` or `why` with other characters than a class name has, is stored as unknown (`NULL`), not refused; `end_role` and `end_team` are read like `role` and `team`; a round without the new fields is accepted and stores `NULL`; a repeat of the same round changes nothing; the migration adds the columns and the table to an existing database and a second run changes nothing; revivals are deleted with their round and server.
*   **Statistics:** *as dealt* gives the numbers the start-state grouping gives today on a fixed set of rounds (a regression test with the current expected values); with no end state in any row, both views give the same numbers, and the default is *as played*; *as played* groups by the end state and falls back to the start state for rows without one; the sum of rounds played is equal in both views; a conversion moves one win from one row to another; the switch rejects other values; the count of rows without end state is right; the player page numbers (times revived, revivals done) count only revivals with a known reviver for "done".
*   **Compatibility:** the current validators are fed new and old bodies (tests that fail if an unknown field starts causing a refusal), and a new-format body is stored by the old code path with the new data dropped (a recorded fixture).
*   **Bridge, role changes (Fengari, controllable clock):** a `TTT2UpdateSubrole` and a `TTT2UpdateTeam` call for the same player in the same tick give one `role` event with the settled role and team; a change that ends where it started gives none; a change before the round begins (the role assignment) gives none; a role change of a dead player is recorded; 1,025 changes stop at the cap and set `truncated`; `end_role` and `end_team` are the last state for a player present, kept for one who left, and absent when none was seen; **`won` uses the end team** (a start-innocent converted to the winning team won; a start-traitor converted away from it did not; no end team falls back to the start team); an error in a hook handler does not reach TTT2 or stop the round; with tracking off no track events are collected but `revives`, `end_*` and `won` are still correct.
*   **Bridge, revivals:** a respawn event for a player who died in this round gives a `revive` at its time; the same revival also seen by the per-tick comparison gives one event, not two; a revival seen only by the comparison gives an event at the tick's time with no source; a defibrillator attempt followed by a respawn of the same victim within the window names `by` (the owner) and `how` (the weapon class) and `why` (its `revivalReason`) and uses the note once; an attempt with no respawn leaves no event; a note older than the window is not used; a second attempt replaces the first; a respawn with no note has no `by` and its `how` is `role:` plus the role the player had at the death (also when the role changed on revival); a revival within 30 s after the death of the player who last killed the victim is marked so, one after 31 s or after a different player's death is not; the hook handler returns nothing (a non-nil return would block the revival: a test calls it the way the weapon does and checks the result); a player who is not in the round is ignored; with tracking off the revivals still reach the report.
*   **Mutation checks** (the habit from RFC 12): remove the team settle step, the same-tick merge, the end-team win check, the note window, the single-use of a note, or the `nil` return of the hook, and a test fails for each.
*   **Cost:** the sampling tick cost is measured again with the hooks in place (`traitordash_track_test`, then `traitordash_frametime`); the average and the worst must stay inside the budget. The numbers are added to RFC 12's real-server section.
*   **Viewer and pages:** events decode and sort; the team at a given time is the last `role` event at or before it; a figure's colour changes at the event; the scrubber shows the markers and tooltips; a revival with an unknown source is labelled so; a track without events draws exactly as before (a pixel comparison with a stored render); the player panel lists the events and a click jumps the scrubber; the round page shows "was" and the revivals; the statistics switch changes the table and the link. Rendered in headless Chromium in each camera mode, and the images are looked at.
*   **Real server (the acceptance test):** a staged round with a normal defibrillator, a necromancer and a zombie, a deputy and a sheriff: every revival is in the report with the right reviver and the right `how`, every role change at about the right time, and the replay shows them. Both the necromancer's defibrillator and the normal one, because they are different code (3).

## 8. Alternatives Considered
*   **Only observation (the per-tick alive and role comparison).** Works whatever fires, but times are 0.2 to 1 s late and who and how are lost. It stays as the fallback for the fact of a revival (4.1).
*   **Inferring revivals from gaps in the segments.** No format change, but a skipped tick looks the same as a death-and-revival, and who and how are lost. Rejected.
*   **A guess for the reviver when the hook is missing.** Dropped: a wrong name in a replay is worse than "unknown", and the aim is to settle disputes.
*   **Wrapping `Player:Revive` to learn the caller (`debug.getinfo`) and guess the reviver by nearness.** More reach (it sees every call), but it patches a core function that other addons also replace, and the reviver would be a guess. The defibrillator's own hook already names the reviver exactly. Kept as a later option for role addons that turn out to matter.
*   **Reading each weapon's `OnRevive(ply, owner)` by patching the weapons.** Exact for the weapons we patch and nothing for the others. Rejected.
*   **Send the role with every sample.** Five numbers instead of four, for something that rarely changes. Events cost bytes only when something happens.
*   **Change `role` and `team` in `players[]` to the end state.** Silently changes every statistic by role and team from the update on. Keeping the start state and adding `end_*` preserves meaning and lets both views exist.
*   **Statistics by end role only.** Both are useful (2); a switch costs a query parameter. The default is *as played*, the more accurate view; *as dealt* is one click away.
*   **Backfill old rounds.** There is nothing to backfill from.
*   **Track late arrivals.** Dropped: a TTT2 round has no way to add an alive player after the start other than a revival, which is covered (4.1).

## 9. Open Questions
*   **Is `role:<death role>` right for every role that revives itself?** It is for the nine found (3). A role that revives someone else, or a future one, would be labelled with the revived player's role. The staged round shows it; a hook-based note for such a role is a later change.
