# RFC 27: Voice Chat Through the Dashboard (Living and Dead)

> **Status: stage 1 built (2026-11-10), not yet run on a real server or media server.** Tested with fakes: the rules, the routing, the bridge's messages and the routes (1 970 tests pass); the browser page is bundled but has not been opened in a browser. Asked for by the maintainer: the game's own voice is poor in quality and drops for every player at each map change. Scope decided in the same conversation: **two groups only, the living and the dead. No proximity (never a goal) and no team or traitor channel (not fair, decided 2026-11-10).**

> **Third reason (reported 2026-11-10):** the game's voice has repeatedly failed to unmute a revived player in recent rounds. The cause is not known (not investigated); this design answers it by making the group a function of the current list of the living, repeated every 5 seconds (section 3), so a missed revive cannot last.

## 1. Abstract
The game's voice runs through the game server's connection: the codec is the engine's, and a map change breaks the connection for everyone. This RFC adds voice over WebRTC (Opus) through a page of this dashboard, where the dashboard decides who hears whom from the round's state. Because the dashboard routes the streams itself, a player's group changes without any visible step (nobody is moved between channels, nobody sees an icon), which a chat service made for people cannot do: in TTT a death is secret until the body is found, so anything visible about a player's state is a leak.

## 2. Rules (the whole of stage 1)
| Phase | A living player hears | A dead player hears | Heard by the living | Heard by the dead |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Round in progress | the living | the living and the dead | the living | the living and the dead |
| Not in a round (before, between) | everyone | everyone | everyone | everyone |

*   A player who joined after the round began, or is only spectating, counts as dead for that round.
*   The rules take only **alive or dead** and the round's phase. **No role, team or position goes to the voice side**, so it cannot leak one, and proximity is not possible by construction.
*   Someone who is not on the voice page hears and is heard only through the game's own voice, as today. The two do not mix; the player is told to turn the game's voice down (section 6).

## 3. Design
*   **Who is who.** The voice page is for signed-in users (RFC 13). The Steam account of the session is the SteamID64 of the player in the game; there is nothing to pair. A user who is not a player of the server joins as a listener of the "not in a round" rules only.
*   **Routing is the server's.** The media server is an SFU (for example LiveKit or mediasoup), not a mesh of direct browser connections: with a mesh a listener cannot be stopped from receiving a stream, with an SFU the server forwards a stream to a listener only if the rules allow it. The dashboard hands out join tokens that allow publishing and nothing else; **who is subscribed to whom is set by the dashboard through the SFU's server API** whenever the state changes, never by the browser.
*   **State from the bridge.** One new bridge route (tier `bridge`, RFC 13, 4.4) takes small events: `{ phase: 'prep'|'active'|'over', alive: [steamid…] }`, sent when the phase changes and when a player dies, spawns or leaves, at most a few times a second. The bridge already sees all of these (the hooks of RFC 12 and 26). No new hook of cost, no sampling.
*   **Revivals (a requirement, not a detail).** A player revived by a defibrillator or a role's power is alive again and must at once be heard by the living and stop hearing the dead's chat, and the other way round for a second death. So: (1) the bridge sends the change **when the player is alive again, not when the revive starts**: a player being revived is still dead (and still in the dead's group) until the game says otherwise; the source is the same as the track's (`Track.Revive` from the revive event, RFC 14, and the alive check of the position samples), and a spawn after a death counts as a revive; (2) every event carries the **whole list of the living**, not "X died" or "X revived", so a lost or reordered message cannot leave anyone in the wrong group; (3) the bridge also **repeats the list every 5 seconds** while a round runs, so a missed revive is put right within 5 seconds at most; (4) the SFU's side applies a list by comparing it with the current subscriptions, so repeating the same list changes nothing and is not heard as a click. What a revived player heard while dead (the dead's chat) they keep knowing, as in the game's own voice: that is a rule of the game, not of this system.
*   **If the state goes quiet** (the bridge stopped, the server is changing map): keep the last rules for at most 20 seconds, then fall back to "everyone hears everyone". A map change then costs the dead their separate group for a moment, and nothing else; voice itself does not drop, because the connection is the player's own to the SFU. (Failing the other way, muting everybody, would repeat the very problem this RFC is for.)
*   **Quality.** Opus from the browser, with its own echo cancellation, noise suppression and level control, and **RNNoise** (a small neural noise filter, MIT, `@sapphi-red/web-noise-suppressor` 0.4.1, run in the browser as an audio worklet in WebAssembly) in front of the sending, on by default, a switch on the page (decided 2026-11-10). The filter's files are copied by the build to `/public/rnnoise/` (`bun run copy:rnnoise`), the voice page's policy gets `'wasm-unsafe-eval'` for them (no other page does), and it needs a 48 kHz audio context. If it cannot start (files, WebAssembly, sample rate) the microphone is sent as it is and the page says so. A "Hear myself" switch plays the result back, off at every join, so the filter can be judged alone (one account cannot join twice). **LiveKit's Krisp filter is not an option: its documentation says it is supported only on LiveKit Cloud** (the page of the `useKrispNoiseFilter` hook, found by the maintainer 2026-11-10), and this system runs the SFU itself. **How the microphone opens (the level, the thresholds, a measurement of the room, an automatic mode) is redesigned in [RFC 29](./RFC-029.md).**

## 4. What it does not do
*   It does not stop two players from talking outside the system (a phone call, another chat service). No voice system can.
*   It does not replace the game's voice; it is an addition a player may use. The game's own voice stays as it is and needs no change.
*   No recording of voice. Nothing is stored, nothing is put into a replay. ([RFC 26](./RFC-026.md), stage C names "who talks"; that can be taken from the SFU's speaking events later, and would be a separate decision.)

## 5. Stages
*   **1:** the rules of section 2, the voice page, the bridge route, the SFU hooks. One new server setting (voice on or off), on the shared settings page (RFC 23).
*   **2:** the Tauri wrapper with a global push-to-talk key (section 6).
*   **Later, only if asked for:** per-player volume; a "who is talking" marker on the page (not in the game).

## 6. Open (to decide before building)
*   **Push-to-talk or voice-activity detection: decided 2026-11-10, a choice of the player, voice-activity detection by default.** The page shows both, but **holding a key is switched off until the desktop app exists** (decided 2026-11-10: a key held on the page cannot work during the game, which is when it is needed, so offering it would be a broken control); its code stays in the page and its button is only disabled. Voice-activity detection needs nothing else; its threshold can be set on the page. Push-to-talk on the page works only while the browser tab has the focus (a browser cannot hear a key while the game has it), so for the game's sake it needs a small helper program on the player's machine that sends the key to the page; that helper is **stage 2** (decided 2026-11-10): a Tauri wrapper around the same voice page, with a global hotkey for push-to-talk. The game's own embedded web panel is **not** an option for the voice client (decided 2026-11-10): the panel goes with the map change, which is the very break this RFC is for.
*   **Where the SFU runs, and its ports.** It needs UDP ports open to the players and, for players behind strict networks, a relay (TURN). This is the one part that is not code of this repository.
*   **Echo with speakers, and hearing everyone twice (built 2026-11-10).** A player on speakers with the game's voice also on would hear themselves, and anybody would hear the others twice. The page says to use a headset. On the server, the bridge setting **`traitordash_voice_mute_game`** (RFC 23, group "Voice chat", off by default, applies at once) holds the engine's `sv_voiceenable` at 0 (`sv_voiceenable` is the engine's own switch for in-game voice; not tried on the real server yet). Switching it off puts back what it was if the bridge changed it, and leaves it alone if `server.cfg` already said 0; it is asserted again when a round is prepared, because `server.cfg` runs at every map. **While it is on, a player who is not on the dashboard's voice chat cannot talk at all.** It does not depend on `traitordash_voice`.
*   **A player in the game but not on the voice page** is silent to the others' voice chat. The page lists who is connected to it (not who is alive), see the per-player volume in section 7, so a player can see whether somebody has joined.
*   **Threat model (decided 2026-11-10): a game between friends.** Nobody is assumed to run a modified browser to hear what they should not, so the separation is only as strong as the page, and that is accepted. This is why the SFU is **LiveKit** (the server sets each listener's subscriptions with `updateSubscriptions`, the page does the rest) and not mediasoup, which would give the server full control of what each listener receives but needs far more code. The docs do not say whether a client can override the server's subscriptions, and it does not matter here. The server SDK is `livekit-server-sdk` (Node; Bun not confirmed, to be tried first). Ports for a self-hosted server: TCP 7880 (behind the proxy), UDP 50000-60000 or the single UDP port 7882, TCP 7881, TURN 3478/5349 optional.
*   **Tests for revivals** (stage 1, before anything else): dies, revived, dies again, in one round, in the harness with the SFU's API replaced by a recorder: after each step the recorder holds exactly the groups of section 2; a lost message is put right by the next list; two lists in the wrong order end in the newer one (a sequence number on each).
*   **Trust in the state.** A player who could post to the bridge route could move themselves between groups; the route is bridge-token only, as every other.

## 7. Stage 1 as built (2026-11-10)
*   **Bridge** (`addon/lua/traitordash/voice.lua`, the setting `traitordash_voice`, off by default, RFC 23): a timer twice a second (none when off) looks at TTT's round state and at who `IsTerror`; it sends `POST /api/v1/voice` `{ boot, seq, phase, alive }` when the phase or the list changed, when a game event says so (death, spawn, leaving, TTT2's added-event, which a revival raises), and the same again every 5 seconds. Bots and spectators are never in the list. A failed send is dropped.
*   **Backend** (`services/voice.ts`): `canHear` (the rules of section 2, the 20-second fallback), `parseVoiceState`, a small client of LiveKit's room service (`ListParticipants`, `UpdateSubscriptions`, tokens signed with the API secret; no SDK, so no Bun question), and `VoiceHub`, which keeps each server's latest state and, for a server somebody joined, makes every listener's subscriptions match: at once when a state arrives, every 2 seconds besides, and everything again every 30 seconds. A pass requested during another waits for it and runs after, so a state is never skipped.
*   **Page** (`/servers/:id/voice`, signed-in users; `POST /servers/:id/voice/token`): `voice.ts` joins the room named `traitordash-<server id>` as the player's SteamID64 with **auto-subscribe off**, publishes the microphone (echo cancellation, noise suppression and level control on), and plays what the media server sends. The microphone opens by voice (threshold and a short tail) (a held key is in the code but disabled, see section 6); the choice is remembered in the browser. The page's policy may connect to the media server's address and nothing else besides this site.
*   **Settings page:** a third group "Voice chat" with the one switch.
*   **Deploy:** (1) a LiveKit server reachable by the players: the **egg `pelican/egg-livekit.json`** runs it as a Pelican server of its own on the same node as the dashboard (three allocations: signalling 7880 behind a TLS proxy, TCP 7881, one UDP port 7882 for all media; no TURN, no port range), see `pelican/README.md` (an egg for LiveKit did not exist: none was found in the Pelican egg repositories on 2026-11-10); with an API key and secret; (2) the backend gets `LIVEKIT_URL` (`wss://…`, what browsers use), `LIVEKIT_API_KEY`, `LIVEKIT_API_SECRET`, and `LIVEKIT_API_URL` if it reaches the server by another address; the bundle is built by `bun run build:js` (it makes `src/public/voice.js`); (3) the bridge's `traitordash_voice 1` (or the settings page). Backend before bridge, as always: an old backend answers the bridge's post with 404 and the bridge stops sending on that map.
*   **To try first on the real server:** that TTT's `GetRoundState`, `ROUND_ACTIVE`, `ROUND_POST` and `IsTerror` behave as assumed (a revival by the defibrillator and by the necromancer: the log line `traitordash_voice_status` and the list the backend holds); that LiveKit's JSON answer for `ListParticipants` is read right (an audio track's `type` may be absent); that a player who is dead hears the living and the living do not hear them; the page in the browsers the players use.
*   **Names and who is on (built 2026-11-12).** The bridge's state message also carries `names`: the nick of everyone connected as a person, alive or dead, no bots, cleaned (no control characters, valid UTF-8, at most 32 characters), so it says nothing about who is alive. The backend keeps them with the state (and forgets them when the game server goes quiet) and puts the freshest one in the join token's `name`: the game's nick, else the nick of the last round it stored, else the SteamID64. Before a player has joined, the page asks `GET /servers/:id/voice/people` every 5 seconds (signed-in users; the backend asks the media server, at most once in 3 seconds per server) and shows the count and the names ("On now: Bob, Cara"); it stops when the player joins, because the room tells then, and starts again when they leave. The count showed 0 for somebody who had not joined while another player was on: that was wrong and is the reason. Names for players the game server does not have at that moment (from the Steam Web API) are [RFC 28](./RFC-028.md). A name that becomes known after a player has joined is set in the room for the others (RFC 28, section 3).
*   **Per-player volume (built 2026-11-10, asked for as high priority):** the page lists everyone on the voice chat, by nick where a round knows it (the join token carries it) else by SteamID64, each with a volume slider (0 to 100 in steps of 5) and a mute. They act on that player's audio element, are kept in the browser by SteamID64, and apply whenever that player's track arrives (during a round the game lets a track come and go). The list shows who is connected, not who is alive. No boost above 100: the browser's audio element takes no more, and a boost would need the Web Audio graph, which some browsers do not feed with a remote stream unless an element also plays it.
*   **Not built (stage 2 and later):** the Tauri wrapper with a global push-to-talk key.
