# RFC 33: Drawing the Missing Map Elements (Water, Buttons, Glass, Decoration)

> **Status: proposed (2026-11-15), not built.** The implementation of what the coverage list of [RFC 32](./RFC-032.md) shows as missing, ranked on the maintainer's real server (27 maps, snapshot in `docs/MAP_COVERAGE.md`): 3 269 faces of `gap` classes and 670 faces of `unknown` ones, against 28 392 brush faces drawn. Decided with the maintainer on 2026-11-15, including that `func_illusionary` goes in. Extends RFC 18 (4.2 brush entities, 4.4 parts, 4.9 glass) and RFC 12 (the replay page).

## 1. Abstract
The pipeline draws brush entities from two class lists (`FIXTURE_CLASSES`, standing; `MOVABLE_CLASSES`, at their starting place) and drops everything else. RFC 32's measurement ranks what players see and the pipeline does not draw:

| Rank | Class | Maps | Faces | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `func_illusionary` | 10 | 2 076 | decoration with no collision (torches, flowers, rails, fake blocks) |
| 2 | `func_button`, `func_rot_button`, `momentary_rot_button` | 23 + 3 + 1 | 622 + 95 + 0 | buttons and levers players press |
| 3 | `func_water_analog` | 7 | 319 | the water brush: the pool of `ttt_67thway_rework_b1` (section 2), `ttt_cquestt` |
| 4 | `func_breakable_surf` | 10 | 252 | breakable glass panes |
| 5 | `func_tanktrain`, `func_monitor`, `func_conveyor`, `func_reflective_glass` | 3 / 5 / 1 / 1 | 45 / 22 / 4 / 2 | a moving part, screens, a belt, a mirror |
| - | `func_dustcloud`, `func_buyzone`, `func_bomb_target`, `game_zone_player`, `ttt_traitor_check`, `env_embers` | 4 / 2 / 2 / 1 / 7 / 1 | 365 / 36 / 24 / 12 / 65 / 6 | volumes and zones: **invisible**, only the registry changes |

This RFC draws each of the first five rows, and classifies the last as `invisible` in the registry (RFC 32, 3), so that the coverage page ends with no `unknown` for them. The order of the stages is the order of the table, except that the cheap ones come first (section 5).

## 2. What the map shows (measured 2026-11-15)
The map is Workshop item `577197148` (`ttt_67thway_rework_b1`, by iluka), downloaded anonymously with the pinned DepotDownloader (RFC 11, 3.2; the archive is LZMA-compressed, which the backend's own code already reads) and read with the project's extractor; the file is not kept in the repository.

*   **The world model has water.** The statistics say `waterFaces: 18`, which become **54 water triangles** (in the extractor's `water` list), in a box of x -2064 to 864, y -904 to 1828, z -2560 to 64: low-lying coast water, inside the box of the floors, so the "within 512 units of the floors and walls" rule of `mapgeometry.ts` keeps it. Nine materials carry `SURF_WARP` (`nature/water_coast01_beneath` and eight `maps/ttt_67thway_rework_b1/nature/water_coast01_<x>_<y>_<z>`).
*   **The pool is a brush entity.** The entity lump has one `func_water_analog` (`hammerid 173539`, `targetname "water"`, model `*37`) with `origin "-768 40 -528"`, `startposition 0`, `movedistance 140`, `movedir "-90 0 0"`, `speed 20`, `WaveHeight 3.0`, `startsound "water_flood_in"`, and a `water_lod_control`. Model 37 is a box of 256 x 368 x 144 units (mins -128, -184, -72, maxs 128, 184, 72, around its origin) with **two faces**: its surface (`maps/ttt_67thway_rework_b1/nature/water_coast01_-767_32_-399`) and its underside (`nature/water_coast01_beneath`), both flagged `SURF_WARP` (flags `0x3408`). The extractor's rule "only upward faces (normal z of 0.5 or more) are water" keeps the surface and drops the underside, which is right.
*   **Why it is missing.** `extractMapEntities` (`bsp.ts`) turns an entity into a drawn model only when its class matches `FIXTURE_CLASSES` or `MOVABLE_CLASSES`. `func_water_analog` matches neither, so its two faces fall into `otherModelFaces`. The pool in the screenshot is the floor beneath the water brush.
*   **What it means in the game.** In Source the `movedir` angles of `-90 0 0` point **up** (pitch -90 is up, +90 is down), `startposition 0` is "at the place the mapper put it", and the sound is a flood: the water is low at the start of the round and rises by `movedistance` (140 units) when the map triggers it (`speed 20` units a second). That reading is Source's convention as I know it; it is not verified on a running server (section 7).

## 3. The common rule
A brush entity is read like a fixture already is: the faces of its model `*N`, around the model's own origin, placed at the entity's `origin` and angles (RFC 18, 4.2), **at the start of the round**. An entity with a `parentname` is not drawn (the extractor's existing rule: where a parent moves it is unknown). Nothing is drawn where it is hidden at the start: a `startdisabled` or `rendermode 10` (`kRenderNone`) entity is skipped (so that a hidden trap door does not appear), a `rendermode 1/2/5` ("color"/"texture"/"additive") entity is drawn as it is. Every change below adds a class to a list, or a group to the GLB; none changes how the floors, walls and storeys are computed.

## 4. The elements
### 4.1 Buttons (`func_button`, `func_rot_button`, `momentary_rot_button`)
Added to `MOVABLE_CLASSES`: drawn in the group of parts that can move (RFC 18, 4.4), at their resting place, as doors are. A button is solid and pressed by players; it is also usually small and sits on a wall, so it never forms a floor or a storey (the movable group is outside the level model already). This is the widest gap (23 of 27 maps).

### 4.2 The water brush (`func_water_analog`)
The pool: a new class set `WATER_CLASSES = /^func_water_analog$/` next to the two lists. Its faces that have `SURF_WARP` and an upward normal (z of the normal at least 0.5) go to the `water` list (the translucent blue of RFC 18, 4.1) and every other face of that model (the underside, `nature/water_coast01_beneath`, a side) is dropped, so a water volume never becomes a floor, a wall or a storey. They are placed at the entity's `origin`, **moved along `movedir` by `startposition` x `movedistance`**; `movedir` is turned into a direction with the fixtures' `angleMatrix`; `startposition` is limited to 0 to 1 and `movedistance` to 0 to 16 384, anything malformed counts as 0. The result is the water at the start of the round; the flood (the map raises it during the round) is not shown, and would need an event from the game (RFC 20's part tracking). The replay page gets the **Water** checkbox of the map page (`data-water`, on by default, shown when the map has water, in the controls row beside Walls and Leaves, in the page's style), because it already draws the water and has no switch for it.

### 4.3 Breakable glass (`func_breakable_surf`)
Added to `FIXTURE_CLASSES`: drawn standing, with the glass look the pipeline already gives glass materials (RFC 18, 4.9: translucent where the material is `$translucent`/`$alpha`, the game's own glass texture otherwise). On `ttt_diescraper` this is 96 panes of the 252. The glass breaks in the game; the view shows it whole, as the world is at the start of the round.

### 4.4 Decoration (`func_illusionary`)
The biggest gap by faces and the only one that is not solid: players walk through it. So it must **not** be a floor or a wall, and must not change the levels or storeys, or give a dead body something to lie on in the replay (RFC 22, 4.x: "what a body can lie on is the floors and the solid props"). It goes into a **group of its own in the GLB, `decoration`** (next to `props`, `water` and the movable group), drawn with the textures of its materials like the rest (RFC 18, 5), and a **Decoration** checkbox (on by default) on the map page and the replay page, beside Walls, Leaves and Water, in the page's style.
Measured on `ttt_minecraft_b7_summer` (2026-11-15: 120 entities, 663 faces, 1 329 triangles; by material: `minecraft/torch` 210, `planks` 192, `trap_lava` 51, `stone` 43, `yellowrose` 40, `redrose` 28, `glowstone` 20, `woolwhite` 16, `track_s` 16, `woolred` 8): the torches, flowers, rails and glowstone are small decoration; one large slab (about 500 x 1000 units, `trap_lava`) lies **below the map floor** in the lava-trap area, which no player sees; it is dropped by a rule below. The planks and stone faces could be fake blocks or decoration; they are drawn the same way, in the decoration group, which is what they are in the game (visible, not solid).
*   **Below the map.** A decoration face whose highest point lies below the lowest floor of the map (minus a margin of 64 units) is not drawn: it is hidden by the world. (The same rule would apply to a buried water volume; it is stated once, here.)
*   **Cost.** The group is bounded by the same face and vertex caps as everything else; a map with thousands of decoration faces gets the cap's note, as a map with too many props does.

### 4.5 Smaller classes
`func_tanktrain` (a moving part: added to `MOVABLE_CLASSES` with `func_tracktrain`), `func_monitor` (the screens of a security-camera room: a standing fixture, drawn with its material as a plain surface), `func_conveyor` (a belt: a standing fixture; the belt's motion is a texture scroll, not geometry), `func_reflective_glass` (a mirror: a standing fixture with the glass look of 4.3). Each is a one-line addition to a list.

### 4.6 The invisible ones
`func_dustcloud`, `func_buyzone`, `func_bomb_target`, `game_zone_player`, `ttt_traitor_check`, `env_embers`: volumes that a player cannot see (a particle region, a buy zone, a trigger zone). They become `invisible` entries of the registry (RFC 32) with a note each; nothing is drawn. (That they are invisible is Source's documented use of these classes, not something measured; the first replay of a map with each of them is the check; one that turns out visible moves to a list above.)

## 5. Stages
Each stage changes the extractor's output, so **one** mesh-version bump at the end (both numbers up by one) rebuilds every stored map once; the coverage page then shows the effect.
*   **A: lists.** Buttons (4.1), glass (4.3), the smaller classes (4.5) and the registry statuses (4.6): additions to `FIXTURE_CLASSES`, `MOVABLE_CLASSES` and `COVERAGE`; the registry test fails if a class is drawn in code and not `drawn` in the registry.
*   **B: water** (4.2): the class set, the placement, the face rule, the replay's Water checkbox.
*   **C: decoration** (4.4): the group, the below-the-map rule, the checkbox on both pages, the replay's lying-on-things rule left as it is (the decoration is not in the list of floors).
*   **D: the check.** After the deployment and the rebuild, the coverage page's totals are copied into `docs/MAP_COVERAGE.md` as a second snapshot: **the success measure is that the `gap` row goes from 3 269 faces to 0 and `unknown` from 670 to 0 on these 27 maps**, except what new classes the maintainer's later maps bring.

## 6. Limits, safety and cost
*   The caps of the extractor (`SurfaceLimits`, entity counts) apply to every class added; the names and numbers read from a map (`startposition`, `movedistance`, `movedir`, `rendermode`) are parsed as finite numbers within the ranges above, anything else counts as the default.
*   The glass and decoration add triangles: on the measured maps 252 and 2 076 faces over 27 maps, against 249 087 world faces and 28 392 brush faces drawn: under 1 % of the work of drawing a map; the biggest single map (`ttt_minecraft_b7_summer`, 663 decoration faces) adds about 1 300 triangles.
*   The coverage record (RFC 32) counts them as drawn after the change, so the page shows the effect without any further work.

## 7. Tests (what must fail on a broken build)
*   Per class, on a small constructed BSP (the tests of RFC 18 and 32 build one): the class's faces appear in the intended list and group (buttons in `movable`, glass standing, decoration in `decoration` and **not** in `floors`, `walls`, levels or storeys); a `parentname` entity is not drawn; a hidden one (`startdisabled`, `rendermode 10`) is not drawn; the registry says `drawn` for each class that is drawn.
*   Water: a two-face model (an upward and a downward `SURF_WARP` face) gives one water quad at the entity's origin and nothing in `floors` or `walls`; `startposition 0.5`, `movedistance 100`, `movedir "-90 0 0"` moves it up 50 units and `"90 0 0"` down; malformed or out-of-range values behave as 0; a `func_brush` with a water texture is still water and a `func_door` with one is not (no regression).
*   Decoration: a face below the lowest floor is dropped, one above is kept; the group is bounded by the caps; decoration is not offered as a lying place in the replay.
*   The mesh version numbers are bumped (both, and still different) and a stored mesh of the old version is rebuilt.
*   The pages: the Water and Decoration checkboxes appear when the map has the group and not otherwise, toggling hides the group, no layout jump, contrast (the shared toggle style of RFC 31).
*   The registry: the classes of 4.6 are `invisible`; none of the classes of this RFC is `gap` or `unknown` any more; the coverage page's totals for a constructed map move accordingly.
*   The previous behaviour is unchanged for everything else: the mesh bytes of a map with none of these classes are identical to before (a pinned hash, as RFC 32 does).

## 8. What cannot be verified here
*   Source's reading of `startposition` and `movedir` for the water (section 2): the first replay of `ttt_67thway_rework_b1` after the build shows whether the water sits at the right height; if it is off, the sign of the move is the thing to change.
*   That the six classes of 4.6 are invisible in every map (documented Source behaviour, not measured on a running server).
*   Whether the decoration, buttons and glass look right in the replay's three.js scene (translucent glass over the world, many small torches): the first replay of `ttt_minecraft_b7_summer` and `ttt_diescraper` is the check, and the Decoration checkbox is there to switch it off if it clutters.
*   `func_illusionary` entities that carry a `renderamt`/`rendercolor` effect (a fade, a tint) are drawn without the effect.
