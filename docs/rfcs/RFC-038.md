# RFC 38: Proximity Voice, as a Choice of the Listener

> **Status: proposed (2026-11-20), not built. Revised the same day: the computation is in the backend, not in the game ("I don't like the game doing the tracing").** Asked for by the maintainer as a "wild spin": with the 3D maps and the voice chat under our control, could proximity voice be done? Decided in the conversation: yes, **as an option each listener chooses**, because (1) the game's own locational voice is poor (audio quality; vertical distance is ignored, so a player a floor away comes through while one a door away is silent) and (2) proximity audio makes the best part of the game, talking with friends, harder (feedback from a player). Builds on RFC 27 (the voice chat and its rules), RFC 23 (the shared settings page) and RFC 11 and 18 (the 3D map mesh, used by the fade map; **not** the floor-plan scan of RFC 10: the plan exists for few maps and is the legacy route, the mesh is the way forward and exists for 27 of the maintainer's 28 maps).

## 1. Abstract
Today every living player hears every other living player at full volume in the dashboard's voice chat (RFC 27, 2): the rules are global and know nothing but the phase and the list of the living. This RFC adds **proximity**: the volume, the muffling and the left-right position of each voice follow where the speaker is relative to the listener, with distance, floors and walls counted. It is **off for everyone by default**; a server turns it on as *available*, and **each listener chooses** Off, Soft or Full on their own device, and can **pin** friends who are then always heard in full. **The game server does no maths and no tracing**: it only reports where the living are; the backend computes what each listener hears.

## 2. Rules
1.  **Available by a server setting, never forced.** `traitordash_voice_proximity` (bool, default 0, group "Voice chat", applied at once, RFC 23). With it off nothing in the voice chat changes and the bridge sends nothing new.
2.  **The listener chooses, per device:** **Off** (today's behaviour, the default for everyone), **Soft** or **Full**; remembered on the device like the volumes. A listener who switches proximity on gets Soft first.
    *   **Soft:** voices get quieter and muffled with distance and walls but **never below a floor** (35 % of their volume; the muffling capped at one step). Friends across the map stay audible, and there is still a sense of who is near.
    *   **Full:** the distance and walls apply properly, down to silence.
3.  **Pinned players are always heard in full.** Next to each player's volume and mute on the voice page (kept on the device by SteamID64, as those are): "Always audible". A pinned player skips proximity (gain 1, no muffling, centred) in every mode. A friend group plays on Full and still hears itself.
4.  **The existing rules stay:** only the living in a round are affected. The dead hear everyone at full volume, and outside a round everyone hears everyone, as RFC 27 says. Proximity only scales voices that the rules already allow.
5.  **Positions go from the game to the backend and no further.** While proximity is available the bridge sends the **eye position and view yaw of the living** (rounded; nothing else: no role, no team). The backend keeps them **in memory for one update only**: never stored, never logged, never forwarded. Browsers receive only **gain, pan and muffling per speaker**, which is what the listener hears anyway: they never see a position, a floor or a coordinate. (The backend already receives players' positions for the replay at the end of a round, RFC 12; this is the same trusted place, live.) The sentence in `voice.lua` that says "No role, team or position is sent" is amended to say that positions are sent only while proximity is available, and only to the backend.

## 3. Design: the pipeline
*   **The game server (bridge, `voice.lua`) only reports.** In its existing round message (`POST /api/v1/voice`, RFC 27) it adds, while the setting is on, a `pos` list for the living: `[sid, x, y, z, yaw]` (units and degrees rounded to whole numbers), at about 5 times a second and only when a position moved beyond a small threshold or on a slow refresh. Reading eye position and angles of at most ten players is the whole cost: **no traces, no distance maths, no model in Lua** (a test guards that `voice.lua` calls no trace function). The bridge also passes the server's proximity settings (section 3, last point) in the same message, so the one settings page of RFC 23 stays the only place they are set.
*   **The backend (`VoiceHub`, `voice.ts`) computes**, for each living listener and each living speaker that the rules let them hear, at the rate of the updates:
    *   **Distance:** `d = sqrt(dx^2 + dy^2 + (v * dz)^2)` with `v` the **vertical factor** (default 2: a floor above counts as far, which fixes "above and below are heard, one door away is not").
    *   **Gain:** a smooth curve, 1 up to `d0`, falling to 0 at `d1` (a squared ease: `g = (1 - (d - d0) / (d1 - d0))^2`, clamped), so there is no hard cutoff. Defaults to tune by ear: `d0` 300, `d1` 1 500 units.
    *   **Pan:** the horizontal angle of the speaker relative to the **listener's yaw**, from -1 (left) to 1 (right), 0 straight ahead or behind (a stereo pan, not head-related audio).
    *   **Muffling:** from the fade map (section 4); 0 (clear) for a map without a mesh.
*   **The row to the listener:** the backend sends each listener **only their own** row, a list of `[speaker, gain 0..100, pan -100..100, muffle 0..3]`, as a LiveKit data message to their identity (`SendData`, lossy: a few hundred bytes at 5 Hz for ten players; the Twirp client the hub already uses; verify the call exists in the media server version in use: if not, the voice page polls a route instead). The row is dropped when the rules say the listener hears nobody, when the state is stale (the 20 s rule of RFC 27) or when the round is over. An old bridge sends no `pos`, so the page stays Off.
*   **The browser** (`src/voice.ts`): each remote voice goes through a Web Audio chain: source from the track, a gain (proximity gain, then the player's volume, mute and the Soft floor), a low-pass filter whose cut-off follows the muffling step (clear 20 kHz, then about 3 kHz, 1.2 kHz and 600 Hz), a stereo panner, the output; every change is **smoothed** (about 80 ms) so a 5 Hz update never clicks. Pinned players bypass the proximity values; Off bypasses everything for that listener and the page behaves as today (the per-player audio element volume of RFC 27). Chrome needs the remote stream to be attached to an audio element as well for Web Audio to run on it (a known browser behaviour; the voice library has a mode for it): the implementer reads the library and verifies.
*   **The page:** a control "Proximity" with Off / Soft / Full (shown only when the server has proximity available: the join answer says so), and the "Always audible" toggle for each player in the list; the voice page's layout rules apply (fixed sizes, no layout jump, `--space-N` variables, contrast); a short muted line says what the modes do.
*   **Settings** (shared settings page, group "Voice chat", declared in `settings.lua` and `bridgeSettings.ts`, with their test of the two lists in sync; forwarded to the backend in the round message): `traitordash_voice_proximity` (bool, default 0), `traitordash_voice_prox_near` (units, `d0`), `traitordash_voice_prox_far` (units, `d1`), `traitordash_voice_prox_vertical` (the factor `v`, 1 to 5), `traitordash_voice_prox_leak` (how far sound leaks **through** walls: a multiplier on the straight distance used when it is shorter than the path, the fade map; default 3). Applied at once.

## 4. The model: the fade map, from the 3D mesh (no traces anywhere)
A straight line between two players says nothing about how sound **travels**: around a corner through an open door, up a stairwell. The **fade map** is the map's **air**, voxelised once from the stored 3D mesh, so the backend can measure the **sound-path distance** between two places through open space: a room above is far by the stairs, a room round a corner through a doorway is near. It is built from the **mesh** (RFC 11 and 18: the GLB with the floors, walls and storeys, and the movable parts at their start), which exists for every map the dashboard has built; it does **not** need the floor-plan scan of RFC 10 and no map has to be scanned.
*   **Built by the backend** from the mesh when a map's mesh is built or first needed: every triangle of the world surfaces (floors, walls, ceilings: all level nodes) and the **closed** movable parts at their start pose (a door, RFC 35's start states: a sliding door that starts open is not in the mesh at all) are rasterised into a 3D occupancy grid of cubic voxels (64 units to start; finer is measured) with **conservative** triangle-box overlap, so that a thin wall never leaks diagonally. Water, decoration, props and leaves do not block sound. Then the **air is flood-filled** from the places a player can stand (a point 48 units above each floor triangle's centre), so the skybox, solid interiors and voids are not air. The result is an occupancy bitset with an origin and a size: tens to a few hundred KB per map (measured on the maintainer's meshes before B is accepted), held in memory and cached with the mesh version so a rebuilt mesh rebuilds it.
*   **Used by the backend at every update, no table of pairs:** for each living listener one **range-bounded Dijkstra** over the air voxels from the listener's voxel, stopping at the far distance `d1` (26 neighbours, diagonal steps cost more), a few thousand voxels at most; at most ten of them per update. The path length `p` to each speaker's voxel is read from it (absent = inaudible). The distance used for the gain becomes `min(p, leak * straight distance)`: sound takes the shorter of "around" and "through".
*   **Muffling without a trace:** the **ratio** of the path to the straight line says how much solid is in between: `r = p / straight`: up to 1.3 clear (0), up to 2 (1), up to 3 (2), more (3). A speaker right behind a wall has a long path and a short straight line, so he is muffled; one through an open doorway has a short path and is clear.
*   **Doors:** a closed door of the map's own start pose blocks sound in the model for the whole round; **doors opened or closed during a round are not followed**, so a door the players open stays "closed" for the model (the grid is static). Door states reported by the bridge (RFC 20 already records parts and their inputs) would fix that and are a later step, not part of this RFC.
*   **Fallback:** only for a map **without a mesh** (one of the maintainer's 28) or a position outside the grid: the straight distance of section 3 with the vertical factor and no muffling. Nothing breaks.
*   **No floor plan:** no map has to be scanned, no plan is read, no RFC 10 data is used.
*   **One design, built in two steps that are not separate releases:** first the pipeline of section 3 (the bridge's positions, the backend's rows, the browser chain, the settings, the page), then the model of this section on top of it; the straight-distance formula of section 3 is only the fallback for a map without a mesh, so it is not a stage of its own. The listening session of section 7 judges the whole.

## 5. What this does not do
*   No head-related (HRTF) or height cues, no reverb or room acoustics, no front/back distinction: stereo pan, volume and a low-pass only.
*   No change to who may hear whom (RFC 27 stays the authority), no team or role channels (the voice side knows no teams, by design), and no change to the game's own voice (it stays off while this chat is used).
*   No positions to browsers (rule 5), nothing stored.

## 6. Tests (what must fail on a broken build)
*   **Bridge** (Fengari harness, `voice.lua`'s tests): `pos` appears only with the setting on, only for the living, rounded, at the rate and threshold; the dead and the preparation phase send none; the setting off sends nothing and costs nothing; **no trace or distance function is called by `voice.lua`** (a guard); the settings travel with the message; a missing eye position is skipped.
*   **Backend model** (pure functions, `services/voiceprox.ts` or similar): the gain is 1 up to `d0`, falls monotonically and smoothly to 0 at `d1`, never outside 0..1; the vertical factor (a speaker one floor up at the same horizontal distance is quieter than one on the same floor); the pan sign follows the listener's yaw (right positive, left negative, ahead or behind 0, wrap-around at 180 degrees); only pairs the rules allow get a row; rows go only to their own listener; stale and round-over rows are dropped; positions are never in any outgoing message, log line or stored row (a test that scans for the coordinates used); an old bridge without `pos` and a new backend work.
*   **Fade map:** the voxelisation and the search on **constructed meshes** (a straight corridor, a room round a corner, a wall with a doorway, two storeys joined by a ramp, a closed room, a thin wall at 45 degrees that must not leak, a skybox shell that must not count as air): conservative rasterisation, the air flood fill, path lengths are right, a room a floor above is far, the range bound stops the search, deterministic; the muffling steps from the ratio; `min(p, leak * straight)`; the fallback without a mesh; the grid is cached with the mesh version and rebuilt with it; a build failure never fails the mesh build.
*   **Browser** (page code tests with stubs): a gain row sets the gain, filter and pan values as the tables say, smoothed; the Soft floor (35 %) and the muffling cap; Full reaches silence; pinned players ignore the row; Off ignores rows; the mode and pins are remembered per device; proximity unavailable hides the control; the audio-element fallback for Web Audio is in place.
*   **Layout:** the new control and the pin toggles keep the page's layout rules (no jump between modes, contrast, 390 px).
*   **Mutation checks** on the key rules (the vertical factor, the pan sign, the soft floor, the pin bypass, rows to the wrong listener, the ratio steps, the `min`).

## 7. Verification on the real server (required before it is called done)
A listening session with friends, which no test can replace: stand a floor above each other (a voice should be faint and muffled, not clear), one door away (clear through an open door), behind a wall, at the far end of the map (Soft: faint but there; Full: silent), and check the pan against where people stand. Tune `d0`, `d1`, the vertical factor and the leak by ear and write the final defaults into this RFC. Also render the air voxels of a few real maps (a slice per storey, and the sound distance from a chosen cell) to see that walls, doorways and stairs behave. Unverified until then: the size and build time of the fade maps on the maintainer's 27 meshes, how well a 64-unit voxel keeps thin walls and doorways (finer if not), the availability of `SendData` in the media server's API version, Web Audio on remote WebRTC streams in the browsers the players use, and that the eye position and yaw of a player read as assumed on TTT2.

## 8. Open
*   The defaults of the distances and factors (by ear, section 7).
*   Whether Soft should be the default for every listener once the server has it available (the proposal is Off for everyone until they choose).
*   Closed doors: reporting door states from the bridge (section 4, last point) if the listening session shows they matter.
*   Whether to show a small "near you" indicator in the player list: not planned.
