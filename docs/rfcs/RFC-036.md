# RFC 36: Every Material Gets Its Texture (no Quality Budget; Levels of Detail Later, If Needed)

> **Status: built (2026-11-17).** See section 9 for what differs from the design and what was measured. Originally proposed the same day. Asked for by the maintainer after the furnace, the diamond block and the sign of `ttt_minecraft_b7_summer` showed flat colours in the 3D view ("these two ... are the wrong texture"), and decided with them: **"I don't like the budgets at all; if we start having issues, we encode LOD material variants and load them in gradually."** Extends RFC 18, section 5 (textured floors and walls). Found by reading the map (2026-11-17).

## 1. Abstract
`buildSurfaceTextures` (`backend/src/maps/surfacetex.ts`) ranks the materials of a map by the ground they cover and gives a texture only to the largest **40** (`SURFACE_LIMITS.textured`; RFC 18 5 says 24, which is stale). The next materials, up to 160 looked at (`materials`), get the **average colour** of their texture (taken to 8 shades per channel), and a translucent or cut-out material in that tail gets **nothing** (`if (v.kind !== 'solid') continue`), so its faces keep the plain floor, wall or decoration colour. On `ttt_minecraft_b7_summer` (76 materials) the objects a player looks at are exactly the small ones: `furnace-off` is rank 55, `diamond-block` 58, `sign-count` 71, and they show as flat grey, flat cyan and flat tan. The textures themselves are fine: nothing is missing from the map's pack and nothing fails to decode.

This RFC removes the quality budget. **Every material the map names gets its own texture**, translucent and cut-out ones included, with their alpha. Quality limits are not replaced by other quality limits: how a map is kept light is a question for **levels of detail**, loaded gradually, which is built only if real maps show a problem (section 5).

## 2. What the maps show (measured 2026-11-17)
*   **`ttt_minecraft_b7_summer`:** 76 materials; 51 343 triangles textured, 960 with an average colour (32 materials: furnaces, diamond and gold blocks, the crafting table, chests, minecart parts, the torch, farmland and about 15 signs), 218 plain (4 materials: `redrose`, `hatch`, `door-iron`, `sign-count`). With the budget raised to 100 in a test harness all 76 materials are textured: the furnace, the diamond cube and the sign with its text are right; the PNG data grows from 33 628 bytes (40 images) to 100 486 bytes (76 images) and the GLB from 2 186 368 to 2 308 460 bytes (+122 KB, 5.6 %).
*   **`ttt_mc_skyislands`:** 79 materials, the same cap: ranks 43 and below are flat colours (`furnace`, `furnace_side`, `furnace_top`, `painting1` to `painting6`, `workbench1` to `workbench3`); its plain tail (2 564 triangles) is mostly the helper material `halflife/black` (probably the void, intended) plus cut-outs (`item_*`, `lillypad`, `bed_front`, `hatch`, `cauldren_top`). The same cost estimate applies.
*   Any other map with more than 40 materials has the symptom; the census of section 4 measures them all.

## 3. Design
*   **Texture every material.** In `buildSurfaceTextures` the ranking by area stops deciding *whether* a material is textured: all materials the map names (those with at least one face in the floors, walls, parts or decoration) are looked at, resolved (the pack first, then the game's own files where that is switched on, as now), and given a texture. The `textured` and `materials` counts are removed from `SURFACE_LIMITS`. The area ranking stays only as the **order** in which materials are processed (so that a safety ceiling, below, cuts the least important first).
*   **Translucent and cut-out materials get their texture too.** The loop's `v.kind !== 'solid'` skip is gone: a `blend`, `mask` or additive material becomes a textured material with the right alpha mode and alpha (the code that already handles these kinds for the textured set, `mode = v.kind`, `alpha`, `explicitAlpha`, the 0.4 default for a blend texture without an alpha channel). `sign-count` (`$translucent 1`) therefore shows its text.
*   **The flat colour stays only as the fallback** for a material whose texture truly cannot be found or decoded (a missing VTF, an unreadable format): the average colour if the texture decodes at all, else the plain colour as now. It is no longer where a material ends up for being small.
*   **The picture size stays** (`side`, the longer side of a texture, 128 pixels): that is the resolution of the texture, not a count of textures, and it is the axis along which a level of detail would later be added (section 5). Nothing else about the textures changes.
*   **Safety ceilings, not quality budgets.** The pipeline reads untrusted map files: a map that names thousands of materials or a texture pack of hundreds of megabytes must not blow up the build or produce a GLB that no browser can load. The existing guards stay and are made generous so that no real map reaches them: the total PNG bytes (`pngBytes`: from 3 MB to 24 MB), the pack member size (`memberBytes`, unchanged) and a ceiling on the number of materials looked at (2 000). When a ceiling is reached the remaining materials fall back to the flat colour **and the build says so** (the coverage record counts them), so a ceiling that bites is visible and is a reason for section 5, not a silent quality cut. The previous texture-count and 160-material limits are not safety measures and are removed.
*   **Mesh version.** The output changes: `MESH_VERSION` and `MESH_VERSION_GAME` each go up by one; the stored meshes are rebuilt once.
*   **Docs.** RFC 18, 5: the stale "24 largest" text is replaced by a pointer here.

## 4. The census (so the cost is measured on all maps, not guessed)
The coverage record (RFC 32) already carries the surface report (materials, resolved, textured, coloured, from the game). It gets: the **PNG bytes** of the surface textures, the **number of materials left to a fallback and why** (not found, not decodable, ceiling reached), and the **GLB size**. The coverage page gets a short card "Textures": totals over the maps with a record and, per map, materials, textured, fallback, PNG KB and GLB MB, ranked by GLB size, with the largest maps first. After the deployment and the rebuild this shows what "every material" costs on the maintainer's 28 maps, and whether a map is heavy enough for section 5. The counts only count: they do not change the drawing.

## 5. Not planned: levels of detail (decided, to be built only if needed)
If the census shows a map whose textures make the mesh too heavy for a phone or a slow link, the answer chosen by the maintainer is **levels of detail**, not a budget: each material is encoded in several resolutions (for example 16, 32, 64 and 128 pixels), the mesh ships with the smallest and the viewer **loads the larger variants gradually** (nearest or most visible materials first, a few at a time), the way textures of a streaming scene arrive. That needs a format for the variants (separate small files next to the GLB, or extra images in it), a loader in the viewers (map page and replay), a priority rule and tests; it is its own RFC and is **not** part of this one. This RFC only makes sure the cost is measured, so that the decision to build it is made on numbers.

## 6. Tests (what must fail on a broken build)
*   `buildSurfaceTextures` on a constructed pack with more than 40 materials (a hundred small ones, one of them translucent with a texture that has an alpha channel, one cut-out, one whose texture is missing): every material that has a texture gets it, whatever its area rank; the translucent one has the blend alpha mode with its alpha, the cut-out the mask mode; the missing one falls back to the plain colour and is counted as "not found"; the area order only decides the processing order (a test with a ceiling set to 5 shows the five largest textured, the rest flat **and counted as ceiling reached**).
*   The ceilings: the 24 MB PNG guard and the 2 000-material guard cut as described and are reported; a pack member over `memberBytes` is skipped as before.
*   The picture size is unchanged (128), and a texture's bytes are the same as before for a material that was textured before (the pinned-hash style of RFC 32/33 for a map with at most 40 materials: unchanged apart from the version number).
*   The record and the card: the new counts on the constructed maps, the card's totals and ranking, an old record without them loads, no layout jump, contrast, the page head.
*   The mesh version numbers are bumped and still differ; a stored mesh of the old version is rebuilt.

## 7. Check on the real map (required before it is called done)
Build `ttt_minecraft_b7_summer` and `ttt_mc_skyislands` (the downloaded GMAs of the earlier investigations) with the new code and render, before and after, from the same cameras: the furnace block of `b7_summer` (the furnace, the diamond cube, the sign, the gold block), and on `skyislands` the furnace, the paintings and the workbenches: every one textured, signs with their text; list the GLB sizes before and after and the number of textured materials. A material that is still flat must be explained (not decodable, missing) with its VMT/VTF facts.

## 8. What cannot be verified here
*   The cost on the other 26 maps: measured by the census after the deployment, not here.
*   The load on a phone of a larger GLB (a few hundred KB more on the two maps measured; the largest maps are unknown): this is what section 5 is for.

## 9. As built (2026-11-17)
**What was built.**
*   `buildSurfaceTextures` (`backend/src/maps/surfacetex.ts`): `SURFACE_LIMITS.textured` and the 160-material limit are gone. Every material with a face in the floors, walls, parts or decoration gets a texture; the area ranking only orders the work (largest first). The `v.kind !== 'solid'` skip is gone for the textured path, so blend, cut-out (mask) and additive materials become textured materials with their alpha mode, `alpha`/`explicitAlpha` and the 0.4 default as before. The ceilings are `pngBytes` 24 MB (was 3 MB), `materials` 2 000 and `memberBytes` unchanged; the requests to the game's own files (`stockMaterials`, `stockTextures`) went from 400 and 200 to 4 000 each, because at 400 and 200 they would have been a silent quality cut with 2 000 materials. `side` stays 128.
*   `TextureBook` (`texturebook.ts`) knows why a texture is missing (`whyNot`: not found, not decodable, ceiling) and the PNG bytes it holds. Its `textures` limit is now optional: the surfaces pass none, the leaves of the plants (`LEAF_LIMITS`, RFC 18 4.6) keep their 48.
*   `SurfaceReport` gained `pngBytes`, `notFound`, `notDecodable`, `ceiling`; each material is in exactly one of textured, notFound, notDecodable, ceiling (a cut-out or see-through material that is left to a fallback stays plain; a solid one keeps the average colour of its texture, `coloured`, as before). The coverage record gained `textures` (materials, textured, the three reasons, PNG bytes, GLB bytes), set by the build once the GLB exists; `asRecord` drops a census with a missing, negative or non-numeric number and makes fractions whole, an old record has none and loads. The coverage page has the card "Textures" (totals over the maps with a record, then a table of materials, textured, fallback, PNG and GLB by GLB size, the largest first), `docs/MAP_COVERAGE.md` describes it. `MESH_VERSION` 17 and `MESH_VERSION_GAME` 18.

**Differences from the design.**
*   "Not found" also counts a material with no material file or no `$basetexture` (the design said a missing texture only): from the report's view both are "the texture cannot be found".
*   A material whose texture is missing and which is cut-out or translucent stays plain instead of getting an average colour: a flat colour would fill the shape its alpha cuts out. The old code skipped these too; only the reason is now counted.
*   The area order breaks no ties by name (the order of a tie is the order of the file), so a map with at most 40 materials gives the same texture bytes in the same order as before (a test pins the hash, taken from the code before this change).
*   The request limits to the game's own files were raised (above); the design did not mention them.

**What could not be verified.** The cost on the other 25 maps and the numbers of the card on real data (the card is built and tested on constructed records, and fills when the maps are rebuilt after the deployment); how the larger GLB loads on a phone; the pictures are from a software renderer (swiftshader) and say the textures are on the right faces with the right alpha, not how the page looks on the maintainer's screen. Built; looks right in the pictures; not verified on the maintainer's deployment.
