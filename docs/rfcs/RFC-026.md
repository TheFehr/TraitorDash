# RFC 26: Record Everything That Happens in a Round

> **Status: stage A built (2026-11-06): chat (opt-in), shop purchases and bodies found. Stage B built (2026-11-18): health with each position sample and the damage events, section 4a; heal events, section 4b.** Not built yet: the rest of section 4 (stage C). Decided by the maintainer on 2026-11-06 ("what else to record: everything"), answering the open question of [RFC 12](./RFC-012.md), section 9.
> Bodies picked up and dropped are events too, with the path of the body: [RFC 24](./RFC-024.md).

## 1. Abstract
A replay (RFC 12) knew where players were, when they died, what they were revived by (RFC 14), their role changes and the doors they used (RFC 20). It did not know what they said, bought, found or how hurt they were. This RFC adds those as events in the same track, step by step, until the replay can tell the round's whole story.

## 2. Design
*   **One list of events** (`events` in the track, RFC 14, 4.3): `{ t, p, k, ... }` with `t` in milliseconds since the round began and `p` a player of the round. Each kind has a cap of its own in the bridge (a round that is chatty cannot use up the room of the others); a kind that reaches its cap marks the track truncated. The backend takes at most 6 912 events (the sum of the bridge's caps of every kind, sections 4a and 4b) and checks every field of every kind; an unknown kind refuses the whole track.
*   **Nothing blocks the game.** The hooks return nothing, run under `pcall`, and do one lookup when nothing is being recorded.
*   **Deploy the backend first.** A backend from before this RFC refuses a track with an event kind it does not know, so a new bridge against an old backend loses its tracks (with a log line). The other way round is fine.

## 3. Stage A: chat, purchases, bodies (built)
| Kind | Fields | Hook | Cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| `say` | `text` (at most 200 characters, control characters made spaces, invalid UTF-8 reduced to ASCII), `ch`: `all`, `team` (the team's own chat, the traitors' among them) or `dead`. **Only with `traitordash_track_chat` 1**, read when the round begins | `PlayerSay` | 1 024 |
| `buy` | `item` (the class), `it` true for a passive item | `TTT2OrderedEquipment`, `TTTOrderedEquipment` (the same purchase within a second is one) | 512 |
| `found` | `of`: whose body, when that player was in the round | `TTTBodyFound` | 256 |

Only players of the round are recorded (a spectator who was not in it is not). The replay shows them in a feed ("Chat, shop and bodies", every line a link that seeks to it) and in the selected player's own list. They are not marks on the time bar.

**Tested** with the bridge in the Fengari harness (the hooks as the game calls them) and the backend's checks. **Not yet seen on the real server:** the names and arguments of the three hooks are those of TTT and TTT2 as the maintainer of this code knows them; whether `TTT2OrderedEquipment` and `TTTBodyFound` fire as assumed is for the first real round to show (a round with a purchase and a body found).

## 4. Next stages (not built)
*   **B, health** (built, section 4a): a health value with each position sample (delta coded, mostly zeros) and the damage events (who hurt whom, with what, how much), so the replay can draw a health bar and tell who was hurt by whom before a death; section 4b adds the heal events (who was healed, by what, how much).
*   **C, the rest:** weapon pickups and drops and the weapon in hand; shots fired; credits gained and spent; C4 planted, defused and exploded; DNA scans; traitor-button use; karma changes; who a spectator watches; voice chat (who talks, not what); the round's phase changes. Each needs its hook checked on the real server first; the stage is as long as that list.

## 4a. Stage B as designed
Decided before the code (2026-11-18); the as-built section below says where the build differs.

**Health with each position sample.** Each player's segment gets `hp`, an array parallel to `x`, `y`, `z`, `a`: the player's health at the same tick, a whole number from 0 to 1 000, the first absolute and then differences (almost all zeros while nobody is hurt, so a round costs about 1 to 2 bytes a sample). The bridge reads `ply:Health()` at the sample, with no hook of its own, and `ply:GetMaxHealth()` once for each player of a round; that goes in the player's entry as `mh` (1 to 1 000), the scale of the bar. Health is cut to 0 to 1 000 (TTT2 roles can have more than 100; a negative value is 0). A sample whose health cannot be read (a call that fails, not a number) leaves the whole segment without `hp`: the replay then draws no bar rather than a wrong one. A track without `hp` (every track before this) loads and has no bar.

**Damage: the event `hurt`.** `{ t, p, k: "hurt", a?, w, d, h }`: `p` the victim (a player of the round), `a` the attacker when a player of the round did it (left out for the world, falling, fire, oneself, and someone who was not in the round), `w` the cause, `d` the damage taken (a whole number from 1 to 10 000) and `h` the victim's health after (0 to 1 000).
*   `w` is the class of the weapon the attacker holds when the inflictor is the player (the inflictor of a bullet is the player), else the class of the inflictor (a prop, an explosive, fire), else, when there is neither or it is the world entity (`worldspawn`), the name of the damage type: `fall`, `drown`, `fire`, `explosion`, `crush`, `poison`, `radiation`, `shock`, `acid`, or `world`. Letters, digits, `_`, `.` and `-`, at most 40.
*   **Merge:** all the damage to one victim from the same attacker with the same cause within 50 ms of the last of them is one event with the damage summed and the health of the last (the pellets of a shotgun, the hits of a blast).
*   `d` is what the game says (`GetDamage` after the damage was applied), at least 1 (a hit under half a point is not recorded), and for the blow that kills no more than the health the victim still had (the last health seen, from a sample or an event): the overkill of a shotgun at point blank is not counted.
*   **Cap:** 2 048 `hurt` events a round; a round that reaches it is marked truncated, like the other kinds.
*   **Hook:** `PostEntityTakeDamage(ent, dmginfo, took)`, the hook of the game that fires after the damage was applied, for any entity; the handler leaves at once unless a round is recorded (one lookup), `took` is true and `ent` is a player of the round, and runs under `pcall` and returns nothing. It is the hook `stats.lua` already uses for the damage statistics (RFC 21), with the same arguments, so no argument order is guessed. TTT2 has hooks that carry more (for armour and role damage scaling), but they are TTT2's own and their arguments are not known to this code; `PostEntityTakeDamage` is the one that is the same in every TTT and says what was really taken.

**Backend.** `validateTrack` checks `hp` (as many whole numbers as there are positions, each step within +-1 000, the sums from 0 to 1 000) and `mh`, and every field of `hurt`: `p` and `a` players of the round (`a` not the victim), `w` a word as above, `d` and `h` whole and in range; what else an event carries is dropped, as with the other kinds. The events' total cap is the sum of the bridge's caps of every kind: revive 1 024, role 1 024, say 1 024, buy 512, found, pickup and drop 256 each (4 352, the value since RFC 24) and hurt 2 048, **6 400** (6 912 since section 4b, with the 512 `heal` events). It is a safety limit of the backend, and it has to be at least what a bridge can send, or a track the bridge was right to send is refused. Events cost about 100 bytes each when encoded, so 2 048 `hurt` events are about 200 KB of the 8 MB a track may have; `hp` adds about 2 bytes a sample.
*   **Storage:** a track is stored as the JSON of the checked object; `hp`, `mh` and `hurt` are optional parts of it, so no migration and no version field is needed.
*   **Deploy order: the backend first.** A backend from before stage B refuses a whole track that holds an event kind it does not know (`hurt`), so a new bridge against an old backend loses its tracks (with a log line) as in stage A. The `hp` and `mh` fields alone would be dropped quietly by an old backend. A bridge from before stage B against the new backend sends neither, and works as it did.

**Replay.** A health bar over each player's name in the 3D and the flat drawing: small, a dark outline and a dark ground, the colour by the share of the most health (green from 60 %, amber from 30 %, red below), hidden for a dead player and for one with no health data, and drawn again only when it changes. The health at a time is the value of the last sample at or before it, replaced by the `h` of a `hurt` event between that sample and the time (a sample comes up to a second after the blow). The player list gets the health as a number in a box of its own width, the selected player's panel a line "Health 78 / 100" that keeps its height when empty, so nothing moves while time runs. The feed "Chat, shop, bodies and damage" gets `hurt` lines, each a link that seeks to it: "0:57 TheFehr hurt Tonda: 22 (MAC10), 78 left", "1:12 Tonda took 15 (Falling), 61 left"; weapons go through the display-name helper of the statistics pages (`weaponName.ts`), the class stays in the tooltip. The selected player's own list gets the damage they took and the damage they dealt.

**Questions resolved**
*   *One sample interval or a hook for health?* The sample's, so health costs one call a sample and no hook. The `hurt` event covers what happens between samples.
*   *Weapon of a bullet:* the attacker's active weapon at the time of the hit, as RFC 21 does for the statistics.
*   *Damage to oneself or by a non-player:* no `a`. The cause says what it was.
*   *Why 1 000 as the health cap:* TTT2 roles with more than 100 exist; nothing in the game goes near 1 000. It is a bound that keeps a corrupt value from drawing a wrong bar, not a promise.
*   *Total cap:* 6 400, see above; RFC 26's earlier 3 840 was already stale after RFC 24.

**Not verified on a real server, and what to look for** (the Fengari harness fakes the game): that `PostEntityTakeDamage` fires for every hit on a player with `took` true and the health already down (the first round with a recorded fight should have `h` falling by `d`); that `dmginfo:GetInflictor()` of a bullet is the player (else `w` would be the class `player` or the world's type name); that `dmginfo:GetDamageType()` and the `DMG_*` constants are there (a fall must say `fall`, not `world`); that `Health()` and `GetMaxHealth()` give the numbers a health bar needs (a TTT2 role with other health must show its own `mh`). See the as-built section.

## 4a, as built (2026-11-18)
Built as designed in 4a above, with these differences and measurements.

*   **The 3 840 of the first text was stale.** The backend's cap on events was already 4 352 since RFC 24 (pickup and drop); it is now **6 400**, the sum of the bridge's caps, and a test builds a track with every kind at its cap and checks that the backend takes it.
*   **Where the health sits.** The bar is a sprite over the name (3D) or a rectangle over the name (flat drawing), the player list has the number at the right edge, the selected player's panel a line "Health 52 / 100" **above** the role line (below it, it hung after the 2.7 em the role line keeps free). The feed is called "Chat, shop, bodies and damage". The statistics' display names also got the damage types (`fall` is "Falling", `world` "The world").
*   **The most health (`mh`)** is read at the first sample of each player. A player whose `GetMaxHealth` is missing or below 1 has none, and the bar is measured against 100 (a bar is full when the health is more than that).
*   **A hit before the round began** (the preparation) and one on a player who was recorded in the preparation but not in the round is not recorded.
*   **Colours of the bar:** green from 60 %, amber from 30 %, red below (`#22c55e`, `#f59e0b`, `#ef4444`), on a grey ground (`#4b5563`) in a black outline; the text of the health is the palette's `--dim` on paper and `--soft` on the selected row (both pass 4.5 : 1 in a test).

**Size** (a synthetic 5-minute round: 7 players, a sample every 0.2 s, 1 500 ticks, 400 `hurt` events; in the Bun container with the real `validateTrack`): without `hp` and `hurt` 128 595 bytes; `hp` adds 21 189 bytes (**2.0 bytes a sample**, 16 % of the track with no events); the 400 `hurt` events add 43 047 bytes (107 bytes each, mostly the two 17-digit player ids); 192 831 bytes in all, far below the 8 MB limit. The bridge's size estimate (18 bytes a sample) was measured at 12 bytes a sample without `hp` and 14 with it, so it still errs on the safe side. 2 048 `hurt` events are about 220 KB.

**Pictures** (a synthetic copy of the recorded round of 2026-11-03 with invented health and damage: a fight, a merged shotgun, a fall, fire, an explosion, deaths, a player with 150 health and one with no health data; the page is the real page and script, served by the real backend in a container, in Chromium with software GL; the map is its real 3D model; the player model is the box figure, as the model file was not available):
*   the 3D view at 360 x 580 CSS pixels, device ratio 3: a bar over the name of each player, amber at 52 %, amber at 30 %, a small red part at 17 %, green at full health; legible, but where players stand together the bars pile up as the names already do; looks right in my pictures.
*   the view from above and the overview: the bars keep their screen size and dominate the players in the overview; acceptable, same as the names.
*   the flat drawing (the game's browser, no WebGL) at the same size: a bar over each name, amber and green; looks right.
*   the player list: a right-aligned number for everybody who is alive and has health data, empty for the dead and for the player without data, 150 for the survivalist; the selected row is legible; the feed with the damage lines (wrapping to three lines at 360 px, as the other lines do) and the selected player's list ("took 48 from Scip.MLP (Shotgun), 52 left").
*   **No layout jump:** the top, height and width of the 20 boxes of the page (the cards, the player rows, the lines of the selected player) were measured at ten times of the round (before the fight, in it, at two deaths, while health regenerated) at 360 px (device ratio 3) and at 1100 px: not one changed. The health line and the number in a player's row have a fixed box.
*   Not rendered: the page with a real recorded round from the owner's server (none has health yet), and the player model's figure with a bar over it.

**Tests** (2 611 in all, tsc clean, `bun run build` with the Chrome 86 check): in the Fengari harness the hook records a hit with attacker, weapon, damage and health, merges pellets (50 ms inside, 51 ms outside, another attacker, cause or victim apart), names the cause by weapon, entity or damage type, leaves out the attacker for oneself, the world and someone outside the round, ignores players outside the round, hits that were not taken, under half a point, in the preparation and broken arguments, cuts the killing blow at the health left, caps at 2 048 and marks the track truncated, codes `hp` as differences, clamps and sanitises it, leaves a segment without `hp` when it cannot be read, asks for nothing when recording is off, and returns nothing. The backend: valid and invalid `hp`, `mh` and `hurt`, old tracks, the total cap. The replay: the health between samples and with events, the bar's numbers and colours, the feed lines, the markup and CSS facts. **Mutation checks** (29 changes to the code, each run against the tests): the merge window (`<` for `<=`, 500 ms, no merge), the cap, delta coding, victim and attacker swapped, the cap of the killing blow, hits in the preparation, `took`, clamping, an attacker for oneself, an unreadable `hp` sent, the `hp` length and sums, an attacker not in the round or the victim, an event player not in the round, the total cap, damage 0, health unbounded, the cause not whitelisted, `hp` not rebuilt in the replay, hurt events not used between samples, an event exactly at the time asked, the colour threshold, a bar at 0 health, swapped names in the feed, damage dealt missing in the player's list, the bar drawn again every frame: every one failed at least one test.

**Not verified on a real server** (the Fengari harness fakes the game with the argument orders of `stats.lua`, which RFC 21 has not seen on a real server either), and what to look for in the first real round with a fight:
1.  `PostEntityTakeDamage(ent, dmginfo, took)` fires for a hit on a player and `Health()` is already the health after it. Look for: `hurt` events whose `h` falls by `d` from one to the next for one victim. If `h` stays the old value, the hook fires before the damage is applied.
2.  `dmginfo:GetInflictor()` of a bullet is the attacking player, so `w` is the weapon class. Look for `w` values that are weapon classes; if they are `player`, `worldspawn` or damage-type words for gun fights, the attacker's weapon is not found.
3.  `dmginfo:GetDamageType()` and the `DMG_*` constants: a fall must say `fall`, fire `fire`, a blast `explosion`. If every non-player cause says `world`, `bit.band` or the constants are missing there.
4.  `ply:GetMaxHealth()`: a role with more than 100 health shows its own `mh` and a full bar.
5.  `hp` comes with every sample, the first value is the health at the round's start, and the track stays under about 15 bytes a sample.
6.  TTT2 may scale damage after `PostEntityTakeDamage` (armour, role modifiers): `d` is what `GetDamage()` said then; compare `d` with the fall of `h`, and if they differ often, `h` is the truth and `d` should be taken from it.
7.  The deploy order: a bridge with this stage against a backend without it loses its tracks (the log says "track rejected"), so the backend goes first.

## 4b. Heal events
Decided on 2026-11-18 (the maintainer chose "a real heal event from a game hook" over working heals out from the health series). The hooks were read from the real source before any code was written; this section says what was read, what was built from it and what could not be read.

**The event.** `{ t, p, k: "heal", a?, w, d, h }`: `p` the healed player (a player of the round), `a` the healer when a player of the round gave the health (a medic's knife on another player, or on oneself), `w` the source, `d` the health actually gained (a whole number from 1 to 10 000: what `Health()` says after the call minus what it said before, so a value the game cuts at the most health counts only what was gained) and `h` the health after (1 to 1 000). `w` is the class of the entity or weapon that gave it (`ttt_health_station`, `ttt_surgeryknife`) or a word: `regen` (the file or class has "regen" in its name), `revive` (accepted by the backend, never sent by the bridge: see below) or `other` (the bridge could not tell). Letters, digits, `_`, `.` and `-`, at most 40, as for `hurt`.

### Source evidence (read on 2026-11-18)
*   **The TTT2 health station**: repository `TTT-2/TTT2`, branch `master`, file `gamemodes/terrortown/entities/entities/ttt_health_station.lua` (raw: `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/TTT-2/TTT2/master/gamemodes/terrortown/entities/entities/ttt_health_station.lua`). `ENT.HealRate = 1`, `ENT.HealFreq = 0.2`; `ENT:Use(ply)` (line 166) calls `self:GiveHealth(ply, self.HealRate)` (line 176) and waits `self.HealFreq` before the next: one hit point every 0.2 s while the key is held. `ENT:GiveHealth(ply, healthMax)` (line 108): `local healed = self:TakeFromStorage(math.min(healthMax, dmg))`, `local new = math.min(ply:GetMaxHealth(), ply:Health() + healed)`, then `if hook.Run("TTTPlayerUsedHealthStation", ply, self, healed) == false then return false end` (line 120) and only after that `ply:SetHealth(new)` (line 124). So in TTT2 the hook `TTTPlayerUsedHealthStation(ply, ent, healed)` runs **before** the health is set and **can cancel** the heal (`gamemodes/terrortown/gamemode/client/cl_changes.lua` line 1260, the changelog: "return `false` to cancel health regeneration tick").
*   **The TTT base station**: repository `Facepunch/garrysmod`, file `garrysmod/gamemodes/terrortown/entities/entities/ttt_health_station.lua`: the same `ENT:GiveHealth(ply, max_heal)` (line 98), but `ply:SetHealth(new)` (line 107) comes first and `hook.Run("TTTPlayerUsedHealthStation", ply, self, healed)` (line 108) after it. The hook has the same name and arguments in both and runs at a different moment, and in TTT2 it can cancel. A recorder that took its source from this hook would either record a cancelled heal or give the next, unrelated `SetHealth` the station as its source. **The hook is therefore not used.**
*   **Everything else in TTT2**: all 207 Lua files of `gamemodes/terrortown/gamemode` and `gamemodes/terrortown/entities` of TTT2 `master` were downloaded and searched for `SetHealth(`, `Heal`, `regen` and `hook.Run(`. `ply:SetHealth` appears in `gamemode/server/sv_player.lua` line 162 (karma health scaling inside `GM:PlayerSpawn(ply)`, line 108: `ply:SetMaxHealth(health)`, `ply:SetHealth(health)`), in `gamemode/server/sv_player_ext.lua` line 636 (`plymeta:SpawnForRound(deadOnly)`: for a player who is alive, `self:SetHealth(self:GetMaxHealth())`) and line 898 (`plymeta:Revive(...)`: in its timer `self:SetMaxHealth(100)` and `self:SetHealth(100)` while the player is still dead, then `self:SpawnForRound(true)`), and in the station's line 124. **TTT2 has no health regeneration for players, no medkit weapon and no hook about health being gained besides `TTTPlayerUsedHealthStation`.**
*   **Addons that heal**: repository `Skatcat/TTT2Medic`, file `role-medic/gamemodes/terrortown/entities/weapons/ttt_surgeryknife/shared.lua`: `SWEP:PrimaryAttack()` (line 55) heals the player under the crosshair with `ent:SetHealth( math.min( ent:GetMaxHealth(), ent:Health() + 25 ) )` (line 73), `SWEP:SecondaryAttack()` heals the owner (`local ent = self.Owner`, the same call, line 100). **No hook at all**: only `SetHealth` on the healed player, called from a method whose `self` is the weapon. Repository `menof36go/ttt2-role-defibrillator`, file `lua/weapons/weapon_ttt_defib_traitor.lua`: `SWEP:HandleRespawn()` runs `ply:SpawnForRound(true)` and then `ply:SetHealth(GetConVar("ttt_rdef_healthOnRespawn"):GetInt())` (line 343): **a revival that sets the health after the player is alive again**, which is why a rise just after a spawn is not recorded as a heal. The maintainer's checkout of the necromancer role (`gamemodes/terrortown/entities/weapons/weapon_ttth_necrodefi.lua`) derives from `weapon_ttt_defibrillator` (`SWEP.Base`; the cvars `ttt_necro_defibrillator_revival_health` and `..._revival_max_health`, both 75) and calls no `SetHealth` itself; its zombie role has `v:SetMaxHealth(100)` at `TTTEndRound` only. **Not read: the base `weapon_ttt_defibrillator`** (it is in none of the checkouts and not in the TTT2 repository), so how it sets the health of the revived is not known; the grace period below is the answer to that.
*   **GMod** (`https://wiki.facepunch.com/gmod/`): `Entity:SetHealth( number newHealth )` is a method of Entity, client and server; `Global.FindMetaTable( string metaName )` returns the metatable of a class; `GM:PlayerSpawn( Player player, boolean transition )`, server. There is **no engine hook for a rise of health**.

### Design
**A detour of `SetHealth` of the Player metatable**, the one place every heal passes through (the station, the knife, a role's regeneration, an addon's own timer). `FindMetaTable("Player").SetHealth` is replaced by a wrapper when a round starts being recorded (`Track.Begin`; not in the preparation) and put back when the recording ends for any reason (the end of the round, `traitordash_track` 0, a new preparation, a cap that stops the sampling: all of them already call the function that removes the timer, which now also restores the method).
*   **It never changes what the game does.** The wrapper calls the original with every argument and returns everything the original returns (`return finish(..., orig(self, ...))`: the recording runs after the original, under `pcall`, and then hands on the original's values). The gate before the call (`Track.HealBefore`) is under `pcall` too; if it fails or says no, the original is called as it is. With recording off the wrapper is not there at all; if somebody else wrapped after ours, ours is not removed (that would remove theirs) but switched off, and passes everything on.
*   **Idempotent.** The state (the wrapper, the original, whether the table had a `SetHealth` of its own, whether it is on) lives in `TraitorDash.TrackSetHealth`, not in the file, so a reload of the file finds the wrapper of the old load and takes it off at the next round's start instead of leaving a second one recording; a new install of the wrapper that is already on top reuses it. When the Player table only inherited `SetHealth` (from Entity), restoring sets the field back to nil and the inheritance is back.
*   **What is a heal.** In `Track.HealBefore(ply, new)` (cheap, on every `SetHealth` while recording): recording is on and not in the preparation, `new` is a number, `ply` is a player of the round and alive (`IsTerror`), and `Health()` before is at least 1 and below `new`. A decrease, no change, a dead player or one at 0 health (TTT2's `Revive` sets 100 on a dead player, line 898) are out. After the original ran (`Track.Healed`): the health gained is `Health()` after minus before, at least 1. Then two more rules: no heal within 1 000 ms of the player's last spawn (`PlayerSpawn`, which `Revive` and `SpawnForRound` reach through `Spawn`) or of a revival the recorder saw (the defibrillator addon above sets the health after the spawn), and none in the first 1 000 ms of the round (roles and loadout set health then; nobody can use a station in the first second anyway).
*   **The source and the healer** (`HealSource`), in this order: (1) the object the calling method belongs to: `debug.getinfo(2, "S")` and `debug.getlocal(2, 1)` are read in the wrapper itself (the levels count from there); when the first local of the caller is called `self` and is a valid entity that is not a player, its class is `w` (the station's `GiveHealth`: `ttt_health_station`; the knife's `PrimaryAttack`: `ttt_surgeryknife`), and when it is a weapon whose owner is a player of the round, that owner is `a` (the owner may be the healed player: healing oneself); (2) else the calling file's `short_src` as a word (`SourceOfPath`: the folder or file of a weapon or entity, `role_<folder>` for a role, the addon's folder, else the file's name; `[C]` and `[string ...]` give nothing); (3) else `other`. A name with "regen" in it is `regen`. It is cheap (it runs only for a rise of a living round player's health, a few times a minute, and only reads) and judged safe: whitelisted characters, at most 40, `getinfo` and `getlocal` with a level that exists cannot raise an error, and without `debug` the source is `other`.
*   **Left out, because it could not be read from the source:** wrapping `ENT:GiveHealth` through `scripted_ents.GetStored` (whether an entity created later copies the stored table's functions is not something the code read says) and any hook of a heal addon that was not found. The hook `TTTPlayerUsedHealthStation` is left out for the reason above.
*   **Merge:** all the health one player gained from the same source and the same healer within 1 000 ms of the last of it is one event: `d` summed, `h` of the last, the time of the first (a station is 1 hit point every 0.2 s: a use of 8 seconds is one event of 40). **Cap:** 512 `heal` events a round; a round that reaches it is marked truncated, as with the other kinds.
*   **Total cap:** the sum of the bridge's caps, 4 352 + `hurt` 2 048 + `heal` 512 = **6 912** (was 6 400).
*   **Backend** (`validateTrack`): `p` a player of the round, `a` (when there) a player of the round; `a` equal to `p` is allowed when `w` is a class (a weapon or entity: healing oneself with a knife), and when `w` is `regen`, `revive` or `other` the `a` is dropped and the heal kept (nobody heals themselves by regeneration); `w` a word as for `hurt`, `d` 1 to 10 000, `h` 1 to 1 000; unknown fields are dropped. **Deploy order: the backend first**, as in stages A and B: an old backend refuses a whole track holding an event kind it does not know (`heal`), so a new bridge against an old backend loses its tracks (the log says "track rejected"). The other way round is fine; a track with no heal is as before.
*   **Replay:** the feed ("Chat, shop, bodies, damage and healing") has `heal` lines, each a link that seeks to it: "1:40 Tonda healed +25 by Health station, 77 left"; with a healer "Tonda healed +25 by Scip.MLP (Surgeryknife), 77 left"; oneself "Tonda healed themselves +25 (Surgeryknife), 77 left"; the source is left out when it is `other`. ## 4b, as built (2026-11-18)
Built as designed in 4b above, with these differences and measurements.

*   **No hook of TTT2 is used at all.** The source was read first (see "Source evidence"): the only health hook, `TTTPlayerUsedHealthStation`, runs at a different moment in TTT2 and in the TTT base, so the station is found from the caller of `SetHealth` (the `self` of `GiveHealth`), the same way a knife is.
*   **The "healer context" is the caller, not wrappers.** Instead of wrapping `ENT:GiveHealth` and known weapon functions (not verifiable from the source), the wrapper reads who called `SetHealth` (`debug.getinfo(2, "S")`, `debug.getlocal(2, 1)`), which needs no list of known paths and also finds addons nobody listed.
*   **The detour is installed from `Track.Begin`, not from the preparation**, so nothing is wrapped while the preparation is recorded (it would be ignored there anyway).
*   **A grace of 1 000 ms** after a spawn, a sampled revival and the start of the round was added after reading the defibrillator addon (it sets the health after `SpawnForRound`).
*   **The replay's totals line** ("Took 48, dealt 22, healed 40") is new: the selected player's panel had no sums before; it has a line of its own with the height of the health line, so it does not move.
*   **Names:** `regen`, `revive` and `other` have display names ("Regeneration", "Revival", "Other"); a heal from `other` has no "by" in the feed.
*   **The health between samples does not use heals** (see 4b, Replay).

**Not verified on a real server** (the Fengari harness fakes the game from the source read above), and what to look for in the first real round with heals (a round where somebody uses a health station, and ideally a medic and a revival):
1.  `FindMetaTable("Player").SetHealth` reads as a function (it may only be inherited from Entity) and the wrapper put on the Player table is the one `ply:SetHealth(...)` calls. Look for: any `heal` event at all after a station use. If there is none and the log says nothing, the detour was not installed or not called (GMod may bind the method differently); the line "recording round" is in the log either way.
2.  A station heal is `w = "ttt_health_station"` with no `a`, `d` about 1 for each 0.2 s of use merged into one event (a use of 5 seconds should be one event of about 25, not 25 events). If it says `other`, `debug.getlocal(2, 1)` did not give `self` (a tail call, or a different local order in LuaJIT); if the sources are file names, the entity path of the file was matched instead.
3.  `debug.getinfo(2, "S").short_src` has the form the pattern expects (`addons/<name>/lua/...`, `gamemodes/terrortown/entities/...`); if every `w` is `other` or a strange file name, the paths look different on the server.
4.  A revival (defibrillator, necromancer, TTT2 `Revive`) records **no** heal. Look at the `heal` events within a second after a `revive` event: there should be none.
5.  No heal in the first second of the round and none in the preparation (a role with more health than 100 should not make a heal at the start).
6.  A medic's knife heal has `a` the medic and `w = "ttt_surgeryknife"`, if the maintainer's server has that role; healing oneself has `a` equal to `p`.
7.  The cost: a SetHealth call costs a pcall and a few lookups while recording; nothing to measure unless a server calls it every tick for every player (a regeneration addon), in which case the heal events merge into one per second and the call's cost shows in the tick cost line of the log.
8.  The deploy order: a bridge with this stage against a backend without it loses its tracks ("track rejected"), so the backend goes first.

**Could not be verified at all:** the base `weapon_ttt_defibrillator` (not in any checkout or in the TTT2 repository), whether a read of a Player metatable field that is inherited from Entity gives the Entity function in GMod, and `debug.getlocal` behaviour on LuaJIT's frames in GMod.

## 5. Privacy
Chat is the first thing in a track that is what a person *wrote*, not what the game did, so it is **off by default** (decided 2026-11-06): the ConVar `traitordash_track_chat` (a bool, also in the dashboard's settings of the bridge, RFC 23, group "Position tracking", applied from the next round) turns it on. With it on, a replay is for signed-in users only (RFC 13), but every signed-in user sees all of it, **including a team's private chat and the dead's chat**, after the round. That is the point of a replay and is also a spoiler and an exposure; the operator who turns it on should tell the players (the server's message of the day, for example).
