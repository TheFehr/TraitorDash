# RFC 39: First-Person Follow, the POV Mode (the Replay Through the Player's Eyes), with Weapon Models and Looking Up and Down

> **Status: stages A, B and C built (2026-11-21), none of it run on the real server or looked at in the replay on a real map or a real figure (originally proposed 2026-11-20). A: the recording, the backend check, the replay's decoding and the pitch curve. B: the POV camera, its controls and overlay, the hit marker and the damage vignette. Open questions answered by the maintainer the same day: the button is called POV; at death the camera goes to the killer's POV; 90 degrees to start; the centre dot and the weapon name both in the overlay; hit markers and the damage vignette are in (section 4). Decided later the same day: the weapon's world model (section 3.4, 4) and the head and follow-camera tilt with the pitch (section 4a) are **part of this RFC**; sound, per-shot flashes and head bob stay out.** Asked for by the maintainer ("instead of RFC 38 I'd like to write RFC 39: first-person follow"). Includes the saved idea of 2026-11-05 to record the player's view pitch, which the first-person camera cannot do without. Builds on RFC 12 (the track and the follow camera), RFC 22 (the player model and its crouch), RFC 34 (the hole of the third-person follow camera) and RFC 26 (the events that fill the overlay).

## 1. Abstract
The replay follows a player from behind (RFC 12, 12 to 14; RFC 34). This RFC adds a second way to follow: **first person**, the camera at the player's **eyes**, looking where they looked, so that a round can be watched as it was played: what a traitor had in front of them, what a detective could see when they walked into a room. It needs two things the track does not have today: the **view pitch** (the track stores only the yaw `a`, so the head and the follow camera never look up or down) and the **eye height** (the track stores the position of the feet). Both are small additions to the bridge and the track; the camera itself is a new mode of the replay page.

## 2. Rules
1.  **A choice of the viewer, not a replacement:** the third-person follow camera stays as it is; first person is a mode next to it (section 5).
2.  **What the camera shows is what the track knows:** the position, the view direction (yaw and pitch), the eye height (standing and crouching), the map, the other players, things, parts and events as the replay already draws them. It shows the weapon held as its **world model** (section 3.4), but no hands or viewmodel animations, no game HUD and no sound: those are not recorded (section 6). It **does** draw two cues that the recorded damage events (RFC 26 B) make possible: a **hit marker** when the followed player deals damage and a **damage vignette** (a short red edge flash) when they take damage.
3.  **The followed player's own body is not drawn** in first person (it would be inside the camera). No hole, no ghost and no mask (RFC 34) are used: the camera is not behind anything of its own.
4.  **Old tracks keep working:** a track without pitch shows first person with the pitch fixed at level and says so (a note on the page); a track without eye heights uses the defaults (section 3.3).
5.  **Nothing new is exposed:** the replay is for signed-in users (RFC 13) after the round, and shows the player's position and view yaw already; pitch adds how high or low they looked. No other data is recorded for this RFC.

## 3. Design A: what the bridge records
### 3.1 View pitch
*   Each position sample (RFC 12, 3: `x`, `y`, `z`, `a` and from RFC 26 `hp`) gains `ap`, the **view pitch in quarter degrees** (an integer from -360 to 360, positive looking down as in the game's `Angle.p`), **delta coded like the other arrays** (mostly small steps), read as `ply:EyeAngles().p` at the sample (the same call that gives the yaw; this is a number read, no trace and no hook). It is clamped to -360..360 and omitted when the angle cannot be read; the backend accepts a track without it. The cost is one more array: about 8 % more track data (measured on the synthetic rounds of RFC 26 at about 2 bytes a sample).
*   The replay's smoothing of the yaw (a Hermite curve through the samples, RFC 12, 227) is used for the pitch too, so that a look up or down is a curve, not a step every 180 ms.

### 3.2 Eye height
*   The eye is not at the feet: in the game it is `GetViewOffset().z` above the origin when standing (64 units by default) and `GetViewOffsetDucked().z` when crouching (28 by default), and TTT2 roles or addons can change both. The bridge records them **once per player per round** as two small fields of the player's segment, `eh` (standing) and `ed` (crouching), integers 1 to 128, read when the round begins (like the maximum health `mh` of RFC 26). The crouch itself is already in every sample (the state bits `s` of RFC 12 and 22: bit 1 is crouching).
*   Defaults when a field is missing: 64 and 28.

### 3.3 Backend
*   `validateTrack` (`tracks.ts`) checks `ap` like `hp`: the same length as the position arrays, integer steps, a running sum inside -360..360; `eh` and `ed` are integers from 1 to 128; unknown fields are dropped as for the other fields.
*   **Deploy order:** an old backend drops `ap`, `eh` and `ed` silently (no track is lost), so the order only costs the pitch until the backend is new; an old bridge and old tracks are unchanged (rule 4).

### 3.4 Weapon models
*   The `hold` events (RFC 26 C2) say which weapon class a player held and when; they do not say what the weapon **looks like**. The bridge records, **once per round per weapon class that appears in a `hold` event**, the weapon's **world model path** (`weapon.WorldModel`, a string such as `models/weapons/w_pist_usp.mdl`) in a small top-level map of the track, `wm: { "<class>": "<path>" }`, at most 64 classes, the path matched against `^models/[a-z0-9_./-]{1,120}\.mdl$` (lower-cased) and dropped otherwise. Reading a string field of the weapon table (or `weapons.GetStored(class).WorldModel`) is no hook and no cost. The class is a safe word as in the other events.
*   **Backend:** `validateTrack` checks the map (count, class words, the path rule); unknown fields are dropped; an old backend drops `wm` silently and an old track simply has none (the replay then shows no weapon model, only the name).
*   **Where the model comes from:** the replay's model machinery (RFC 18, 4.3 to 4.5: the `.mdl`, `.vvd` and `.vtx` reader, the game's own content for stock models, the textures and materials of RFC 18 5 and RFC 36) loads it by path. Stock TTT weapons use the stock Counter-Strike and Half-Life models, which the server's game content option already fetches; **weapons from workshop addons use models inside their addon**: those come from **RFC 40** (the server's Workshop collection as a content source: the collection is indexed, and a weapon's model is built from the addon that holds it, in the background, when a track's `wm` names it). Until a model is built, or when its addon cannot be fetched, the replay draws a **placeholder** (a small neutral box of the weapon's rough size, or nothing) and picks the real model up on a later load. RFC 39's stage C therefore depends on RFC 40 for addon weapons; stock weapons work without it.

## 4. Design B: the camera
*   **Position and direction:** the eye is the sampled position (smoothed along the same curve as the third-person follow camera) plus the eye height: `eh` standing, `ed` crouching, **blended over about 150 ms** when the crouch bit changes (the game's own duck takes a moment; a jump of 36 units between two samples would be seen). The direction is the smoothed yaw and the smoothed pitch; roll is zero. A player in the air or in water uses the same eye.
*   **Lens:** the page's perspective camera for this mode has its own near plane (about 2 units, so that the face of a wall pressed against it is not cut away too early and the view does not clip through a thin wall) and a **field of view that the viewer chooses**, default 90 degrees horizontal on a wide canvas. On a tall (phone) canvas the horizontal field would make the vertical one absurd, so the vertical field is capped (at 100 degrees) and the horizontal one follows from it; the viewer's choice is kept on the device like other view settings. A thin slider or two steps (Normal 90, Wide 105) in the controls row, not a settings page.
*   **What is drawn:** the map (floors, walls, props, water, decoration, parts), the other players, things and the labels as in the third-person view; the followed player's body, ring and label are **hidden**; the level cut (RFC 12, 14) is off (the camera is inside); **no hole, no mask, no ghost** (RFC 34 is for the camera behind a player). The mesh is a shell with faces of mixed winding; the materials the replay uses already draw both sides (the follow camera is often inside buildings), and this is **checked** in the verification (section 8) because first person is the first mode that spends all its time inside rooms.
*   **Overlay** (small, quiet, at the edge, fixed size so nothing jumps): the **weapon in hand** by name (from the `hold` events, RFC 26 C2), the **health** (the `hp` series, RFC 26 B) and a short note when the pitch is missing, the **centre dot** (a small crosshair-like dot, on by default, a toggle in the same row) and the two cues of rule 2: the **hit marker** (a short cross at the centre, when `hurt` events name the followed player as the attacker; stronger for a kill) and the **damage vignette** (a red edge flash when they are the victim; its strength follows the damage `d`). The cues are drawn from the events at their recorded times (shots are merged per second and are **not** used for flashes: no muzzle flash). The other event feeds stay in the page's panels as now.
*   **When the player dies: go to the killer's POV** (decided). At the moment of death the camera **cuts to the killer's POV** (the `attacker` of the death record, RFC 12 and 14), which is what the victim was looking at, and **follows the killer from then on**: the followed player changes to the killer, so the player list, the panel and the overlay change with it. If the killer dies later, the same rule applies once more (the viewer follows the chain of killers); a time-ordered chain cannot loop. The overlay says whose view it is and why ("Watching Scip.MLP, who killed Tonda"), and the viewer can pick any player or switch to Follow (third person) at any time. **Fallbacks:** when the death has no killer that is a player of the round with positions (the world, falling, fire, a trap with no attacker, suicide), or the killer has no position at that time, the camera stays at the victim's last eye position, turns slowly to the body over about a second and stays there. The dead player's own spectator view (`spec` events) is not shown.
*   **Switching** between the modes keeps the followed player and the time; the camera position is not blended between modes (a cut).
*   **Performance:** one camera, no extra pass (no mask, unlike RFC 34), and the same scene: cheaper than the third-person follow camera with its hole. The in-game browser (Chrome 86) and phones are the targets.

## 4a. Looking up and down in every camera (the idea of 2026-11-05)
Today the third-person view never looks up or down: only the yaw is recorded, so the character always looks level and the follow camera always sits level behind. With the pitch recorded (3.1) both can follow it.
*   **The head:** the character's head (and a share of the spine) tilts by the smoothed pitch in every camera, with the stock model's rig (RFC 22; the head bone is turned about its side axis by the pitch, limited to the model's natural range, about -60 to 60 degrees of head tilt, the rest of the pitch carried by the spine bones). A track without pitch keeps the head level as today.
*   **The weapon in the hand:** the world model (3.4) is attached to the right hand of the character in the third-person views and for the **other** players seen in POV; it follows the hand, so it points where the player aimed.
*   **The third-person follow camera:** its height angle follows the pitch by a **factor** (start 0.4, smoothed with the same damping as the follow position; tuned on real rounds) so that looking up or down is felt without the picture swinging with every glance; a toggle in the controls row ("Look with them", on by default) turns it off. RFC 34's hole and mask are unchanged: they depend on the camera and the player, not on the pitch.
*   **In POV** the weapon is drawn **in the view**: its world model in a fixed **viewmodel-like pose** at the lower right of the screen (a small offset, slightly forward and tilted), without hands, without sway and without animation: it names the weapon in the picture and shows its shape. It is hidden when the player holds nothing or the model is a placeholder.

## 5. The page
*   A **POV** button in the camera row (3D, From above, Follow, **POV**, Theater), enabled only when a player is selected and the track has positions for them; the shortcut and the toggle in the player panel ("Watch from their POV") do the same. The view-field control and the optional dot appear only in this mode.
*   Styles: the case-file look, RFC 30 spacing variables, fixed sizes, no layout jump between modes, contrast tests, 390 px (the controls row must not grow a line).

## 6. What this does not do
*   No hands, no viewmodel animations (fire, reload, draw, inspect), no recoil, no muzzle flashes (the shot events are merged per second, RFC 26 C3), no sound, no head bob (derivable from speed, left out on purpose: it only makes a replay camera nauseating), no game HUD. **Weapon world models are in** (3.4; addon weapons through RFC 40).
*   No first person for a player who was dead before the round began or has no positions; no first person for spectators' views.
*   The third-person follow camera changes only as section 4a says (the pitch factor, switchable); the third-person view is otherwise as it is.
*   No recording of the field of view the player used (the game's `fov_desired` differs per player): the viewer's own choice is used.

## 7. Tests (what must fail on a broken build)
*   **Bridge** (Fengari harness, `track.lua`'s tests): `ap` is written with every sample, delta coded, clamped, omitted when unreadable; `eh` and `ed` are written once per player and sanitised (non-numbers, out of range); no cost when recording is off.
*   **Backend** (`tracks.ts`): valid and invalid `ap` (length, steps, running sum), `eh` and `ed` bounds, unknown fields dropped, an old track and an old bridge work, size on the synthetic round.
*   **Replay logic** (`replaylogic.ts`): the decoded pitch series, the smoothed pitch between samples (no jumps at the sample times, wrap-free), the eye height with the crouch bit and the 150 ms blend, the defaults when the fields are missing, a track without pitch gives level and the note; the camera pose function for a given time (position, yaw, pitch, height) in plain code with tests; the vertical-field cap on a tall canvas; the death behaviour (stay at the last eye, turn to the body).
*   **Page** (page-code tests): the button exists and is disabled without a selected player; the mode hides the followed player's body, ring and label, switches the hole, mask and level cut off, and switches them back when leaving; the overlay shows the held weapon and health from the events and series, fixed size; the field-of-view control is remembered on the device; the mode keeps player and time.
*   **Weapon models:** `wm` is written once per class that appears in a `hold` event, sanitised (the path rule, 64 classes, no non-strings), absent when unreadable; the backend checks it; the replay finds the model by class and path, places the world model on the hand and in POV at the viewmodel pose, shows the placeholder for an unavailable model, and nothing when the track has no `wm`.
*   **Tilt:** the head angle follows the smoothed pitch within the limits, the spine takes the rest, a track without pitch is level as today; the follow camera's height angle follows by the factor and not at all with the toggle off; RFC 34's hole and mask still work with a tilted camera.
*   **The death rule:** at a death with a killer who is a round player with positions the followed player becomes the killer at the death time and the camera is the killer's eye; chains (the killer killed later); a world or self death and a killer without positions use the fallback (last eye, turn to the body); the overlay text; the viewer's own choice overrides.
*   **The cues:** a hit marker at the recorded time of a `hurt` event with the followed player as attacker (stronger for a kill), a vignette at a `hurt` event with them as victim (strength by damage), none without events, none from shots; fixed-size overlay, no layout jump, contrast.
*   **Mutation checks** on the key rules (the pitch sign, the head limits, the follow factor and its toggle, the weapon path rule and the placeholder, the eye height and the crouch blend, the smoothing, the body hidden, the hole off in the mode, the vertical cap, the death rule and its fallbacks, the hit and damage cues).

## 8. Verification (required before it is called done)
*   **On a synthetic round** (the recorded rounds in `track-samples` have no pitch: make a copy with invented `ap`, `eh`, `ed`): render first person in real Chromium at phone and desktop size, from rooms, stairs, outdoors, a crouch and a death, and **look**: the walls and floors seen from inside are drawn (no missing faces from the mixed winding), the near plane does not show the shell through thin walls, the overlay is legible, no layout jump when switching modes. The numbers go into this RFC.
*   **Weapon models and the tilt:** render the stock weapons of a synthetic round (invented `wm` paths of stock models) in the hand of a character in third person, in POV and for another player seen from POV: the model sits in the hand and points where the player looks; a character looking up and down (head and spine), the follow camera with the factor at 0, 0.4 and 1; list for the maintainer's weapons how many are stock models and how many would get the placeholder (from a real recorded round's `wm`).
*   **On the real server:** record a round with the new bridge and watch it in first person; judge the direction and the height (look at a door, a window and a ledge the player stood at) and the smoothness at 5 samples a second. Unverified until then: that `EyeAngles().p` is the pitch the player's view had (it should be), the eye heights of roles, and how choppy a fast turn looks with samples 180 ms apart (the smoothing is a guess that the real round judges).

## 9. Stages
*   **A:** the recording: pitch, eye heights and weapon model paths (3), the backend, the replay's decoding and smoothing. Deployed first, so that rounds recorded from then on have them.
*   **B:** the POV camera, the page, the overlay and the cues (4, 5).
*   **C:** the weapon world models (3.4, 4a) and the tilt of the head and the follow camera (4a). Each can be built and judged on its own; all are useful only for rounds recorded after A.

## 10. Open
Answered by the maintainer on 2026-11-20: the button is **POV**; at death the camera goes to the **killer's POV**; the field of view starts at **90** degrees horizontal ("a first try", adjustable by the viewer); the overlay has **both** the centre dot and the weapon name; **weapon world models** and the **tilt of the head and the follow camera** are in this RFC; sound, per-shot flashes and head bob stay out. Still open:
*   **The follow camera's pitch factor** (0.4 to start): tuned on real rounds; and whether "Look with them" should be on by default (proposed: on).
*   **Weapons from workshop addons** come from RFC 40 (decided 2026-11-20: "not good enough" as a placeholder); this RFC keeps the placeholder only for models not yet built or addons that cannot be fetched.
*   **The right-hand bone and the hold pose of the stock model** (RFC 22's rig): to be read from the model when it is built; a pistol and a rifle are held differently, and the first version attaches every weapon the same way.
*   **Whether the killer chain should be limited** (for example to three hops) so that an unattended replay does not wander through the whole round: proposed unlimited, because the viewer can leave at any time.

## 11. As built, stage A (2026-11-21)
*   **Bridge** (`addon/lua/traitordash/track.lua`): every sample reads the view pitch from `EyeAngles().p` (`ReadPitch`: quarter degrees, rounded, clamped to -360..360, positive looking down, delta coded in `ap` like `hp`; one sample that cannot be read leaves the whole segment without `ap`); each player's `eh` and `ed` are read once from `GetViewOffset().z` and `GetViewOffsetDucked().z` (1 to 128, each missing when unreadable); the world model of each weapon class is read when its first `hold` event is written (`NoteModel`: `WorldModel`, else `GetModel()`, lower-cased, backslashes to slashes, `^models/[a-z0-9_./-]+\.mdl$`, at most 120 characters, no `..`, at most 64 classes) into `wm` of the track. Everything is under `pcall`; a game that cannot answer gives a track as before.
*   **Backend** (`services/tracks.ts`): `ap` is checked like `hp` (whole numbers, one for each position, the running sum inside -360..360), `eh` and `ed` are whole numbers from 1 to 128, `wm` is an object of at most 64 entries with class words and the strict path shape; anything else refuses the track. Old tracks and old bridges are unchanged.
*   **Replay logic** (`replaylogic.ts`): `decodeTrack` gives `ap` in degrees for each segment, `eyes` (per player, the default 64 and 28 for what is missing, `EYE_STAND`, `EYE_DUCK`, `eyeHeights`) and `weaponModels`; `pitchAt` is the pitch on the same kind of curve as the position (Hermite through the samples, clamped to -90..90, null where the track has none). `poseAt` is untouched.
*   **Tests:** 9 in `track_bridge.test.ts` (the Lua bridge against the fake game: delta coding, rounding, clamping, unreadable values, once-only eye heights, the path rule, the 64-class cap, the backend accepts the result) and 13 in `rfc39a_record.test.ts` (the backend check, the decoding, the curve, no jumps, `poseAt` unchanged). **Mutation checks:** the sign of the pitch, the `..` check of the model path and the no-pitch guard / clamp of `pitchAt` were each broken, each failed a test, and each was restored.
*   **Not verified:** that `EyeAngles().p`, `GetViewOffset` and `WorldModel` give what the code assumes on the real server (the fake game says what the code asks for); how big a real track gets (the RFC estimates 8 %: measure on the first real round). Stages B and C (the camera, the cues, weapon models in the hand, the tilt) are not built.

## 12. As built, stage B (2026-11-21)
*   **Logic** (`replaylogic.ts`): `povVerticalFov` (the vertical field from the horizontal one and the canvas, capped at 100), `eyeHeightAt` (standing or crouching by the crouch bit, blended over `DUCK_BLEND_MS` = 150 ms with a smoothstep), `povEye` (smooth position plus eye height, heading, pitch; level without pitch), `povDirection` (pitch clamped to +-89 degrees so the camera always has a side), `turnToBody`, `povSubject` (the killer chain: the followed player; once dead their killer, a player of the round with a position then; and their killer if dead too; a loop or an outsider ends it), `hurtsDealt` and `cueLevels` (hit marker 250 ms, stronger for a kill; vignette 450 ms, by damage; shots are not used), and `shownLevels` takes a `'pov'` camera that cuts no level.
*   **Replay** (`replay.ts`): `POV` is a camera mode next to `Follow`: near plane 2 and the field of view of the viewer's choice (`lens`: Normal 90 or Wide 105 horizontal, capped at 100 vertical on a tall canvas; every other mode keeps 1 and 40), the eye of `povSubject` for position and direction, that subject's figure, ring, name, pin and health bar hidden, no hole and no mask and no ghost, no level cut. The overlay is filled in each frame: whose view ("Watching X, who killed Y", or "X is dead" with no killer), the weapon in hand, health, the centre dot, the hit marker (a cross; red and larger for a kill) and the vignette. The choices (field of view, dot) are kept in `localStorage` under try/catch. Choosing POV with no player selected says so and changes nothing.
*   **Page:** the `POV` button, a controls row (View: Normal / Wide, Centre dot, and a note when the track has no pitch) that keeps its place when hidden, and the overlay (`data-pov-*`), fixed in size and out of the layout. The flat no-WebGL view hides the POV button.
*   **Deviation from the proposal:** after a death the camera and the overlay show the killer's view, but the **selected player does not change** (the player list and the panel stay on the player who was selected): changing the selection by itself would have to be undone when the viewer scrubs back before the death.
*   **Not built in this stage:** weapon models in the hand and in the view, and the tilt of the head and the third-person camera (stage C).
*   **Tests:** 21 in `rfc39b_pov.test.ts` (the lens, the eye and its blend, the direction, the death and the killer chain, the cues, the levels, and the facts of the page and the code). **Mutation checks:** the killer chain's loop guard, the POV level rule and the hit marker's fade were each broken, each failed a test, and each was restored. The spacing test caught negative margins in the first overlay stylesheet; they became transforms.
*   **Not verified:** how POV looks on a real map (the mesh is a shell with faces of mixed winding; the materials draw both sides, but inside rooms is where it counts; the near plane against thin walls; the overlay on a phone at 390 px; how a fast turn looks with samples 180 ms apart). Open it on a real round (an old track works: the view stays level) and look, before this is called done.

## 13. As built, stage C, first part: the tilt (2026-11-21)
*   **Figures** (`figureTilt.ts`, used by `replay.ts`): after the animation has set a figure's pose, its head and spine bones (`Bip01_Head1` and `Bip01_Spine`, `Spine1`, `Spine2`, ... with or without the `ValveBiped.` prefix) are turned about the figure's side axis by the sampled pitch. The head takes half (limited to 60 degrees: with a half share and a pitch of at most 89 the limit cannot bite; it stays as a guard if the share is changed), the spine bones the rest between them (`tiltShares`). The turn is worked out in the figure's space and converted into each bone's own frame, so it does not depend on how the model's bones are oriented; a test turns bones with arbitrary orientations under a rotated figure and checks the head's direction. A track without pitch gives 0 and no turn. The figure is posed 12 to 33 ms apart, so the tilt moves at that rate.
*   **Third-person follow camera** (`followTilt`, `followHeights`): its height follows the pitch by the factor 0.4 (within 35 degrees, settled with the same 200 ms damping as the heading); looking down the camera goes up and what it looks at goes down, looking up the other way (`170 + 200 sin(t)` and `50 - 90 tan(t)`; the first version used 300 and put the camera below the floor at the limit, which a test found). A switch **Look with them** (on; the choice is kept on the device) turns it off. The hole and the mask are unchanged.
*   **Tests:** `rfc39c_tilt.test.ts` (11). **Mutation checks:** the conversion into the bone's frame, the head limit's formula and the sign of the camera's target were broken; the conversion and the sign failed tests (the limit is the unreachable guard above).
*   **Not verified:** how the tilt looks on the real figure and on the real follow camera (the sign and the spine share are right by the maths of the test, not seen on the stock model), and the factor 0.4, which the real rounds decide. **Not built:** the weapon models (the other half of stage C).

## 14. As built, stage C, second part: stock weapon models and the route (2026-11-21)
*   **Stock weapons** (`workshopContent.ts`, `workshopRuntime.ts`, `index.tsx`): a model that no addon of RFC 40's index holds is asked of the game's own content (`ContentDeps.stockModels`, the pipeline's stock source); if the game has it, it is built the same way (its materials from the addons or the game) and recorded as built from `game`. A model that neither has stays `unknown` and is looked at again at the next run; one that an addon holds is built from the addon and the game is not asked.
*   **The route** `GET /servers/:serverId/rounds/:roundId/weapon-model.glb?class=<weapon class>` (signed-in users, like the track): only a class that the round's own checked track recorded in `wm` can be asked for (so it is no way to fetch a model by path; inherited keys such as `constructor` are refused), the answer is the built GLB (`private, max-age=3600`), or 404 with `no-store` while it is not built, and the request queues the build. 404 as well when `WORKSHOP_COLLECTION` is not set, since the building is RFC 40's: **weapon models need that setting.**
*   **Tests:** 2 in `rfc40_content.test.ts` (the game's model, the order of the sources) and 4 in `replay_routes.test.ts` (access, the recorded class only, queued while not built, off when the source is off). **Mutation check:** the own-property guard of the class lookup was removed and a test failed; restored.
*   **Not built yet:** the replay loading and attaching the model (the hand in third person and for others seen in POV, the viewmodel pose in POV, the placeholder), part 3 and 4 of stage C.

## 15. As built, stage C, third part: the weapon in the hand and in the view (2026-11-21)
*   **Loading** (`replay.ts`): for each weapon class a player holds (the `hold` events), the replay asks the route of section 14 (`weapon-model.glb?class=`) once, only for classes that the track recorded in `wm`; the GLB's `z-up` node is cleared (its vertices are in Source's axes, as the world group wants them). A 404 (not built yet, or the source off) is asked again every 20 seconds, at most 5 times; until the model is there the weapon is a small plain grey box, and the real model replaces it when it arrives.
*   **In the hand** (`holdWeapon`): the weapon is a child of the figure's group, at the right hand bone's place (`Bip01_R_Hand`, found by `findHand`), turned only by the player's pitch about the figure's side axis, so it points where the player aimed; **the hand's own turn is not followed** (the model's attachment pose is not in the exported rig, and an arbitrary turn would show a weapon pointing anywhere): a rifle is held the same way as a pistol, and does not swing with the arm. Done after the pose, 12 to 33 ms apart. A dead player holds nothing (the `hold` list says so), and the followed player's own figure is hidden in POV, so no weapon shows there either.
