# RFC 29: The Voice Gate and Automatic Sensitivity

> **Status: stage 1 built (2026-11-12), not yet tried on the real server; stage 2 not built.** What stage 1 is, and what could not be checked, is in section 8. Asked for by the maintainer ("I am not sure the current Level and sensitivity solution is very good"), after two researchers read how the major platforms and open-source projects do it. It replaces the way the voice page of [RFC 27](./RFC-027.md) decides when the microphone is open. The research is in section 3; where a fact could not be verified it says so.

## 1. Abstract
The voice page opens the microphone when the sound is loud enough. Today that is one threshold on a linear meter, the decision is made by a timer that looks every 50 ms, and the microphone is opened and closed through LiveKit's mute switch. It works, but it clips the beginning of words, chatters at the edge of the threshold, crowds quiet speech into the first few steps of the slider, and gives the player no help in setting it. This RFC replaces it with a proper noise gate that runs in the audio thread, with two thresholds, a decibel scale, a short look-ahead, and a button that measures the room. A second stage uses the speech probability that RNNoise already computes.

## 2. What is there now, and what is wrong
| What | Today | Problem |
|---|---|---|
| Level | RMS of a buffer, x400, clipped to 0..100, read every 50 ms from an `AnalyserNode` | Linear: quiet speech sits in the first few percent. A 50 ms look over a long buffer is jittery. |
| Decision | one threshold (slider 1..60), 400 ms hold | Noise at the edge makes the microphone open and close (chatter). No hysteresis. |
| Opening | `LocalAudioTrack.mute()` / `unmute()` | The first syllable can be lost: the gate cannot open before the sound it reacts to. |
| Setting it | a slider and a red line on the bar | The player has to guess; no help from the room's noise level. |
| Noise | browser suppression, then RNNoise | RNNoise knows how likely it is that it hears speech, and the gate ignores that. |

## 3. What the research found
**The platforms** (the Discord points are secondhand: its help pages refused the researcher; several apps have no official documentation, and what is said here comes from third-party guides where it is marked so):
*   **Mumble** has the closest design. Its voice activity setting has two values, "Silence Below" and "Speech Above": below the first it is silent, above the second it is voice, and in between it counts as voice only if the player is already talking. That is hysteresis. Defaults in its source (`Settings.h`): voice activity is the default transmit mode, `fVADmin` 0.80, `fVADmax` 0.98, a voice hold of 20 frames. Its level is either the Speex speech probability or `1 + peak dB / 96` (dB mapped to 0..1), see `AudioInput.cpp`. Its audio wizard has the player drag the slider until speech sits in the yellow or green zone of a coloured bar. It has no ramp and no look-ahead.
*   **OBS's noise gate** has an open threshold, a close threshold, attack, hold and release, all in dB. Defaults: open -26 dB, close -32 dB, attack 25 ms, hold 200 ms, release 150 ms. Its advice: "Select a close threshold above the noise volume and an open threshold slightly below your voice input." (the open/close pair is why it does not chatter; the official text does not say so in as many words).
*   **TeamSpeak 3.5** measures in dB (the SDK documents values from -50 to 50) and has the modes Automatic, Volume Gate and Hybrid.
*   **Discord** has an automatic mode and a manual slider; users complain that the automatic mode "cuts my voice off a lot" and go back to manual (secondhand). Aggressive noise suppression is reported to push quiet speech under a manual threshold.
*   **Teams, Zoom, Meet** expose no threshold: they send audio all the time and adjust the volume. **Steam voice** has an input volume with a test and a gain switch, no threshold (no official source found).
*   **Noise gates in general:** a second, lower close threshold (a few dB below the open one) and a hold are the standard cures for chatter; a gain that jumps makes clicks, so it is ramped.

**The technique:**
*   **RNNoise's speech probability.** The library's `rnnoise_process_frame()` returns it (0..1, one value per 10 ms frame of 480 samples at 48 kHz). The package the page uses (`@sapphi-red/web-noise-suppressor` 0.4.1) calls it and **throws the value away**: its worklet never posts anything to the page. Exposing it is a small patch (about a hundred lines of the worklet are involved). Jitsi uses the same score for its "you are talking while muted" notice (`VADTalkMutedDetection.ts`: a score of 0.9 or more starts a 700 ms window, and the notice fires if the window's average is above 0.6; its noise detector uses 0.2 for the average score and a 1500 ms window). The score is much more robust than loudness against keys, fans and breath, but it is jumpy per frame, exactly 0 for very quiet frames, and may be low for whispering. The researcher found no published accuracy figures.
*   **A gate on a gain, not on mute.** Muting a track in LiveKit switches `MediaStreamTrack.enabled`; a gate that decides on what it hears cannot open before the sound arrives. A gain stage with a short delay line in front of it (look-ahead) decides on the undelayed sound and lets the delayed sound through: the first syllable is not lost. The gain is ramped (about 2 to 5 ms to open, 50 to 100 ms to close). The chain already delays the sound by about 13 ms in RNNoise.
*   **Measure in the audio thread.** RMS over 10 to 20 ms windows with a fast-attack, slow-release envelope; reading a 2048-sample buffer every 50 ms from the main thread is both coarse and jittery. The library's own `NoiseGateWorkletNode` (same package) has the open/close/hold states in dB but no ramp, no smoothing and no look-ahead.
*   **Calibration.** Measure the room for 2 to 3 seconds of silence; the floor is the 90th to 95th percentile of the short-term levels; open about 10 to 12 dB above it and close about 6 to 8 dB above it (the usual starting values; no authoritative source for the exact numbers was found). Do it after RNNoise, so it measures what the gate will hear.
*   **Not recommended now:** a separate speech-detection model (Silero through `@ricky0123/vad-web`): it adds several MB and a second WebAssembly runtime, works in 32 ms frames, and its pre-roll is made for recording utterances.

## 4. Design
### 4.1 Stage 1: the gate
*   **A pure core** (`src/gate.ts`, no browser objects): the state machine and the numbers below, fed with levels and times, answering open or closed and the gain. The worklet and the tests use the same code, so the logic is tested without a browser.
*   **An audio worklet** (`/public/gate.js`, built from that file) after RNNoise and before the output. It measures the level in dBFS over 10 ms windows with an envelope (fast up, slow down); decides with the state machine; delays the sound by the look-ahead; applies the gain with ramps; and posts the level and the state to the page about 20 times a second, for the meter and the "talking" mark.
*   **The numbers (starting values, to tune on the real server):** open threshold -50 dBFS and close threshold 6 dB lower before the room is measured; hold 250 ms (the present 400 ms is more than OBS's 200 plus 150 release); look-ahead 25 ms; open ramp 3 ms; close ramp 80 ms. The slider covers -70 to -20 dBFS. **The hold counts from the window's own level, not from the envelope** (fixed 2026-11-12): the envelope falls slowly (it is for the meter), and a hold that began only once it had fallen below the close threshold was 350 to 600 ms long instead of 250 (measured by the reviewer on the engine); the tests now close the gate at 250 ms plus one window for tones of -13 to -43 dBFS.
*   **The track stays on.** The published track is never muted by the page for the gate: the gain does it. What the rest of the page treats as "microphone open" (the "talking" mark) is the gate's state, not LiveKit's mute. **To check before building:** whether the LiveKit publish options have silence suppression (DTX) on by default; if not, they are set, so silence costs the server nearly nothing. Ten players would not notice either way.
*   **The page:** a meter in dBFS with coloured zones (quiet, where speech should sit, too loud), two markers (open solid, close dashed), one slider that sets the open threshold with the close one following 6 dB below (a second slider under "Advanced" for those who want both), and the "Measure my room" button of 4.2. The old saved setting (a linear 1 to 60) is not carried over; a player without a new one is offered the measurement.
*   **Push-to-talk** (RFC 27, stage 2) does not use the gate.

### 4.2 Measure my room
A button: "Stay quiet for 3 seconds", then the page reads the post-RNNoise levels, takes the 90th percentile as the floor and sets open = floor + 10 dB and close = floor + 6 dB, kept inside the slider's range, and says what it set. If the levels vary a lot during the measurement (speech or a bang) it says so and asks to try again instead of setting something bad. The player can move the slider afterwards.

### 4.3 Automatic mode (optional, off by default)
A switch "Adjust by itself": the page follows the room's floor while the gate is **closed** (the running minimum of the smoothed level over about 5 seconds, rising slowly) and keeps the open threshold 10 dB above it. It never adapts while the gate is open. **What it adjusts is never saved** (the player's own setting, from the sliders and the measurement, stays what is saved), and turning it off puts that setting back and gives the sliders back (changed 2026-11-12: it used to keep its last value, which overwrote the saved setting for good). It is off by default because Discord's automatic mode is the thing its users complain about most (it cuts their voice off); the button of 4.2 does the same job once and leaves the result in the player's hands.

### 4.4 Stage 2: RNNoise's speech probability
**Investigated 2026-11-12 (a spike, not built into the page).** What it showed, in a real Chromium with the real RNNoise (`@sapphi-red/web-noise-suppressor` 0.4.1):
*   **It can be done, and reproducibly.** The shipped worklet (`dist/rnnoise/workletProcessor.js`) already calls `_rnnoise_process_frame` and `processFrame` returns the probability; its caller, `n=e=>{u(e),t.processFrame(e),d(e)}`, throws it away. An edit of three exact spots (keep the largest probability of the channels in a global of the worklet's scope; after each block post `{vad}` to the page every fourth block, about every 10.7 ms; reset it) makes the worklet send it: **about 94 messages a second, one every 10 ms, no errors.** `bun patch` also worked (a clean `bun install --frozen-lockfile` and a `--production` install, as the egg does, both applied it; the patch file is 127 KB because the worklet is one minified line), **but it is not needed**: the page's own code (`RnnoiseWorkletNode`) is not changed, only the worklet file, and that file is already copied by the build (`copy:rnnoise`). So **the way to build it is to do the same edit in the copy step** (a small script with the three exact-match replacements, failing the build if one does not match exactly once, and a test that fails when an upgraded package no longer matches), not to patch `node_modules`.
*   **What the probability does on test sounds** (3 seconds each, 48 kHz, through the real worklet; the probability is one value per 10 ms frame; digital silence sends nothing, because the library leaves it at 0, so a missing message means 0):

| Sound | messages/s | mean | highest | share of frames at 0.5 or more | at 0.9 or more |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| silence | 0 (nothing sent) | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| white noise, -30 dB | 94 | 0.28 | 0.64 | 13 % | 0 |
| hum, 100 Hz, -30 dB | 94 | 0.60 | 0.92 | 88 % | 3 % |
| keyboard clicks, 8 a second | 92 | 0.04 | 0.43 | 0 | 0 |
| a synthetic vowel (120 Hz pulses through three resonances, 4 syllables a second, -12 dB peak) | 94 | 0.54 | 1.0 | 57 % | 50 % (the half of the time the syllable is on) |

*   **Reading it.** Clicks and noise stay under 0.9 (and clicks under 0.5): the probability does what loudness cannot. A **tonal hum looks half like speech (mean 0.6, a peak of 0.92)**: so the probability alone is not enough, and the rule keeps the loudness in: open when the probability is at least 0.9 **for two frames in a row** (a single frame of the hum's peak does not open it) or the level is clearly above the floor; close when the probability has been below 0.8 and the level below the close threshold for the hold time. These are test sounds, **not recordings of speech**; whether the numbers hold for real voices, a whisper and a real room's hum is for the owner's microphone to say (a recording of a few seconds each, through the same harness, would answer it: the harness is a 60-line page and a Playwright script).
*   **Cost and path.** The edit costs nothing in the audio thread (it keeps a number). The probability goes worklet to page to the gate's worklet (`port.postMessage`), a hop that adds a few milliseconds and can wait for the page's main thread; the gate's 25 ms look-ahead covers it. A second output channel of the RNNoise worklet carrying the probability to the gate inside the audio graph would avoid the hop but means changing `RnnoiseWorkletNode`'s construction too; not worth it unless the hop proves late.
*   **Recommendation:** worth building as the player's choice ("By loudness" / "By speech"), loudness staying the default until it has been tried on real voices; about a day of work with tests (the transform and its test, the message to the gate, the state machine with probability and the two-frame rule, the page switch).

### 4.5 What is not planned
Silero or another separate model; per-player settings on the server; anything that sends the level to the server. The gate decides in the browser and the server never sees a level.

## 5. Tests
*   The state machine (`gate.ts`) with synthetic levels: opens above the open threshold and not between the two; closes only after the hold and below the close threshold; no chatter at the edge; the look-ahead means the first block of speech is passed; the ramps never step.
*   The calibration arithmetic: the percentile, the offsets, the clamping, refusing a noisy measurement.
*   The floor tracking: follows the room down quickly and up slowly, does nothing while open.
*   The page's wiring with fakes, as the page's other tests do: the worklet is loaded, the track published is the gated output and is never muted by the page, the meter and the marker follow the posted levels, the saved settings are read and written.
*   What fakes cannot show is listed for the real server: clipped first syllables, clicks, the feel of the numbers.

## 6. Rollout
Stage 1 replaces the present gate in one change (the old threshold and the 50 ms timer go). Stage 2 is separate and optional. Nothing changes on the server or the bridge.

## 7. Open
*   **DTX: answered.** In `livekit-client` 2.23 the publish defaults are `dtx: true` and `red: true` (`publishDefaults` in the bundle; `options.d.ts`: "dtx ... enabled by default for mono tracks"). Only a stereo track would have it switched off, and only when the option is left unset. The page publishes a mono microphone through a `MediaStreamAudioDestinationNode`, whose track can report two channels, so it no longer relies on the default: `publishTrack(..., { dtx: true })` is set explicitly. What the media server sees with an always-on track is therefore a track that sends almost nothing while the gate is closed. Not measured on the real server.
*   (Decided in stage 1) The meter is drawn by the page from the levels the worklet posts, about 20 a second.
*   The right default numbers; they are starting values from other programs, not measurements of this chain.
*   Whether a player sometimes wants "always open" (a test mode, or a quiet room); it is one more mode of the same state machine and costs nothing, and is not decided here.

## 8. Stage 1: what was built, and what was not checked
**Built** (sections 4.1, 4.2, 4.3 and 5; the old threshold, its 50 ms timer and the page's use of `mute()`/`unmute()` for the opening are gone):
*   `src/gate.ts`, the pure core, no browser objects: `LevelMeter` (mean power in 10 ms windows, dBFS, rises at once, falls 120 dB a second), `GateState` (open at or above the open threshold; closes only after the level has stayed at or below the close threshold for the 250 ms hold; between the two nothing changes), `GainRamp` (3 ms up, 80 ms down, a fixed step per sample, never a jump), `GateEngine` (meter, state machine, a 25 ms delay line per channel, the ramped gain; `force` for push to talk), `calibrate()` (90th percentile of the levels as the floor, open = floor + 10 dB, close = floor + 6 dB, both kept inside -70 to -20 dBFS; refuses fewer than 20 readings, and refuses when the 10th-to-90th percentile spread is above 12 dB unless the room is near digital silence) and `FloorTracker` (running minimum over 5 s in 0.5 s buckets; falls at up to 20 dB a second, rises at 2 dB a second; does not move while the gate is open).
*   `src/gate-worklet.ts`, the audio worklet (`td-gate`), built by `build:js` as one file with no imports to `/public/gate.js` (`--outfile`, so the name is the one of 4.1; ignored by git). It sits after RNNoise and before the output node, so the monitor ("Hear myself") hears what is sent. It posts `{ t, level, open }` about 20 times a second and at once when the state changes, and takes `set` (thresholds) and `force` messages.
*   `src/voice.ts` and `VoicePage.tsx`: the published track stays enabled and is never muted for the gate; the "talking" mark and the meter follow what the worklet posts. A meter in dBFS (-80 to 0) with three zones (quiet, where speech should sit, too loud), the open marker solid and the close marker dashed, one sensitivity slider (-70 to -20) whose close threshold follows at the same distance, "Advanced" with a second slider for the close threshold, "Measure my room" (3 s of the posted levels, then the thresholds are set and the page says what it set, or asks to try again) and "Adjust by itself" (off by default). Settings are kept under the new keys `td_voice_gate_open`, `td_voice_gate_close` and `td_voice_gate_auto`; the old `td_voice_threshold` is never read; a player without a new value gets -50 / -56 and a note offering the measurement. The room measurement needs the player to be joined (it listens to the microphone). Push to talk, which is still disabled, would hold the gate open through `force`.
*   **The fallback:** each of the noise filter and the gate is optional. If the filter cannot start, the gate still runs on the unfiltered microphone. If the gate cannot start (its file, or no audio worklets), the page says so and does the opening itself as before: a 50 ms look at the microphone, the same state machine and thresholds, LiveKit's `mute()`/`unmute()` on a copy of the sound (so the first part of a word may be cut). If the whole chain cannot be built the raw microphone is published, with the same fallback.
*   Tests: `test/gate.test.ts` (the state machine, hysteresis, hold, no chatter, the ramps, the engine on synthetic sound with the first syllable passed whole at several offsets to the 10 ms windows and no step in the output, calibration, clamping, refusing a noisy measurement, the floor tracker, and the real worklet file bundled and its `process()` run on blocks) and the page tests in `test/voice.test.ts` (the worklet and the chain, the track published on with `dtx` and never muted, the meter and markers following posted levels, the sliders, the saved settings, the measurement, automatic mode, and the three fallbacks).

**Deviations from the text above:** the close marker's distance is kept when the open slider moves, so moving the advanced slider changes it for good (4.1 says only that the close one follows 6 dB lower); the worklet decides on 10 ms windows, so a start of speech is seen up to 10 ms late, which the 25 ms look-ahead and the 3 ms ramp absorb in the tests; the floor in automatic mode is computed in the page from the posted levels, not in the worklet.

**Stage 2 (4.4) is not built.** The RNNoise package throws the probability away inside its worklet and its WebAssembly glue, so exposing it needs either a patch of the dependency that survives `bun install --frozen-lockfile` or an own worklet built from the MIT source, and neither could be made reproducible and tested here without a browser. It stays an open proposal.

**Not verified** (no browser, microphone or media server was available; everything above was checked with fakes and with synthetic sound through the real processor code):
*   That the first syllable is not clipped with a real voice and a real microphone; the tests show it for synthetic tones starting at a peak, which is a hard case for a gain but not a speech case.
*   How it sounds: clicks at the edges, the 80 ms close, the 25 ms delay (and what it adds to the latency of the RNNoise 13 ms), whether 250 ms of hold feels right.
*   That the starting numbers (-50 / -56, the zones of the meter, +10 / +6 dB from the floor, the 12 dB spread limit) fit real rooms and RNNoise's output; they are from other programs.
*   That `AudioWorkletNode` and `addModule` work with the page's content security policy and the build in a real browser (the policy already allows scripts from the dashboard itself, which is what a worklet module is), that the worklet's output channel count follows the input as intended, and that `MediaStreamAudioDestinationNode` is not stereo for the sender in a way that matters.
*   DTX's effect on the media server, and the cost of an always-on track.
*   Automatic mode over a long session (whether the floor drifts upward in a room with constant quiet speech or music).
