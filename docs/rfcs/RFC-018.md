# RFC 18: Fuller 3D Maps (water, props, brush models, calmer colours)

> **Status: all stages built (2026-10-23).** Findings come from running the extractor of RFC 11 on all 27 maps of one real server and reading their files. Open questions are in section 8.

## 1. Abstract
RFC 11 turns a map's world geometry into a mesh and RFC 12 uses it as the replay's background. Looking at the 27 maps of a real server, the mesh is right for the maps that are built from brushes (walls and floors) and wrong or empty for the maps that are not. Four things are wrong, each with a cause that can be named:

1.  **Water and lava are drawn as solid floor** (`ttt_minecraft_haven`: a 9216-unit square slab over the whole sea).
2.  **Models are not drawn at all** (`ttt_lego` has 72 static props and they are all its houses: the mesh is a bare floor and a fence).
3.  **Most brush entities are left out** (`ttt_mc_skyislands` loses 7 009 faces, `ttt_rooftops_2016_v1` 5 555).
4.  **The level colours assume stacked flat floors**, which terrain, islands and water maps are not, and they are loud.

This RFC fixes the first three in the extractor, adds a calmer way to colour the view, and gives the stages in which to do it. It also keeps in view what is *not* a fault: `ttt_lttp_kakariko_a5_fixed` really is two villages in one file.

Where a prop's model lives (counting distinct models per map):

*   **In the map's own pack**, with the mesh data (`.mdl`, `.vvd`, `.vtx`) and the collision shape (`.phy`), **stored uncompressed**: all of `lego` (25 of 25), `theship` (376 of 376), `cape_town` (41 of 41), `skyislands` (25 of 25), `waterworld` (223 of 225), `kakariko` (6 of 6).
*   **In the game or another game** (stock models such as `models/props_c17/...`): not in the map and not in the addon, but available from the free dedicated-server apps (4.5). `alps` uses 153 stock models and 12 packed; `whitehouse` 99 stock and none packed; `aircraft` all 28 stock.
*   Not one of the 27 maps has its models in the addon archive instead of the map.

Two more faults turned up when the maps were looked at one by one (2026-10-23):

*   **Fake sky made of brushes** (`ttt_cquestt`): ten faces of a custom material, `andrewpfjr/skyboxcustom/sunsky`, form walls 1 600 units wide and 2 400 high around the map. They carry no sky flag (only `NOLIGHT`), so our flag test keeps them, and in the 3D view a single wall hides the castle behind it.
*   **Skybox pieces the skybox rule keeps** (`ttt_airbus_b3`): a slab 384 units under the `sky_camera` and a second slab at a height of -6 144 stay in the mesh, 70 triangles each and with no spawn. The rule of RFC 11 joins geometry in 1 024-unit cells, and the slab sits in a cell next to the aircraft's, so it counts as joined to the map and "is never guessed" applies. With cells of 512 it goes; with joining by shared vertices it is a separate component of its own.

*   **Leaf cards drawn as solid sheets** (found in the first prototype, `ttt_alps`): trees are often a few big flat cards that get their leaf shape from a texture with a cut-out (`$alphatest 1` in the material). Without the texture each card is an opaque grey sheet, and `tree_spruce_card` is one of 230 triangles and 2 400 units wide: a forest of grey shards over the map. The model files are read correctly (every mesh fits inside its own bounding box); it is the missing material that makes them wrong. Leaving the `_card` models out removes the shards.

So a good share of the visible content (the houses of `lego`, the ship, the islands) can be drawn from the file we already have, and the stock models need another source, which exists (4.5).

## 3. Decisions
*   **Water is its own thing, never floor.** It is drawn (translucent) and can be switched off; it takes no part in the level model.
*   **What a player can stand behind is drawn**: static props, and brush entities that do not move. Doors, lifts, trains and the like are drawn too, but as parts that can move, marked in their own colour (see "Parts that can move" under stage 3); buttons and triggers stay out.
*   **The mesh stays one standard file (GLB),** now with named groups (`world`, `water`, `fixtures`, `props`), so the viewer can switch groups and a reader of the file can tell them apart.
*   **Colour is a view setting, not a property of the mesh.** The levels are still computed and still drive the cutaway ("Up to level"); how they are painted is chosen in the viewer (section 5).
*   **Nothing is guessed.** What cannot be drawn (a stock model) is counted and said, not replaced by a made-up box.
*   **The file is untrusted** (Workshop content). Every new reader checks every offset and count, and the size of the result is bounded (section 7).

## 4. Extractor changes

### 4.1 Water and lava (stage 1)
A face whose texinfo has `SURF_WARP` (0x8) is a liquid surface. It goes to its own list, `water`, not `floors` or `walls`. In the GLB it is a primitive `water` in a translucent material (a second, orange one for lava and slime, by the texture name where the map's string table is read; otherwise one blue material). The level model is computed without it. On `ttt_minecraft_haven` this removes the slab and the false second storey; on `waterworld_tropical` and `minecraft_b7_summer` it should give back real levels.

### 4.1a Sky and helper materials (stage 1)
The texinfo flags are not enough: a mapper can paste a sky picture on ordinary brushes. The material names are in the map (the texture data lumps, three small ones), so a face is also left out when its material name has `skybox` in it, or ends in `/…sky` (`sunsky`), or is under `tools/` (`toolsblack`, `locked`: helper materials, never a surface a player stands on). The names are **data, not code**, and the rule is narrow on purpose: a plain "contains `sky`" would drop `skyscraper_wallpaper`, `skybridge` and `skynetmountain`, which are real buildings of real maps (found by listing every material of the 27 maps that has `sky` in its name). Tried on `cquestt`: the wall is gone and the castle shows; on `rooftops` and `cape_town` it also drops the pieces that belong to their 3D skybox.

### 4.2 Brush entities (stage 1)
Today only the world model (model 0) is read. Other models belong to brush entities; each entity names its model as `*N`. The entity lump already holds the classes. Read the faces of model N for entities of class `func_brush`, `func_wall`, `func_breakable`, `func_lod`, `func_wall_toggle` and the like, as walls and floors, in the same lists. Leave out `func_door*`, `func_movelinear`, `func_tracktrain`, `func_rotating`, `func_button`, `func_illusionary` (no collision, often decoration the player walks through), `func_areaportal*`, `trigger_*`, `func_precipitation`, `func_occluder`. A model that an entity with an `origin` or parent moves is read at its rest position and flagged `fixtures`; the group is switchable. The class list is data, not code, so it can be corrected from what real maps show.

### 4.3 Props (stage 2)
*   **Placements.** The static prop game lump `sprp` (versions 4 to 11 differ in record size; read by version, unknown versions are skipped and counted) gives each prop's model index, position, angles, skin, solid mode and (v11) scale. Entity props (`prop_dynamic`, `prop_physics*`, `prop_dynamic_override`) are added from the entity lump the same way.
*   **Where the model is.** The map's pack: the ZIP's central directory (at the end of the pakfile lump) is read, and only the needed members are read from it (`.mdl`, `.vvd`, `.vtx` with the `dx90` suffix). Stored members are read as they are; deflated ones are inflated with a size limit. A model that is not in the pack is `unavailable`: counted per map and shown on the map page ("12 of 165 models are from the game and are not drawn"), not drawn.
*   **The mesh of one model.** The first body part's first model at the first level of detail: `.vvd` gives the vertices (with the fix-up table for level 0), `.vtx` the triangle lists, `.mdl` the structure around them (bodyparts, models, meshes, skin-independent). Texture and material are ignored; a prop is one neutral material. A model above a triangle limit is skipped and counted.
*   **In the file.** Each distinct model is written once as a glTF mesh; every placement is a node that points to it with its own transform (angles, scale, position). That keeps `theship` (1 980 placements, 376 models) small. `.phy` is not used: it is the shape physics sees, not what a player sees.
*   **Foliage** (shown to the person as **Glass and leaves**: on `lego` it is the windows, on `alps` the tree crowns). The material files in the pack (`.vmt`, short text) are read for the models that have them. A mesh whose material has `$alphatest` or `$translucent` (leaves, grass, fences, chain-link, glass) is foliage: it goes to its own `foliage` group, switchable. A material that is not in the pack (a stock one) counts as solid. In `ttt_alps`, 5 of the 213 materials in the pack are of this kind.
*   **Cards.** A foliage mesh whose triangles are on average bigger than `CARD_TRIANGLE_AREA` (50 000 units squared, about 220 by 220) is a **card**: a few huge flat sheets that get their shape from a texture that is not read. It is **left out** and counted (a model that is nothing but cards is not drawn). Drawn at all, a card is only a sheet: the first version drew foliage translucent, and the cards of `alps` (230 triangles over 2 400 units) came out as columns of fog; drawn solid, the same crowns look like trees. The threshold comes from the 228 foliage meshes of the 27 maps: the cards and backdrops (`alps` `tree_*_card` 64 650, the cloud models of `waterworld` and `rooftops`, a mountain backdrop) are over 50 000, and the largest real crown (`alps` `broadleaf1`) is 19 659. Foliage that stays is drawn solid, a little darker than the rest.
*   **Not walkable.** Props are not part of the level model and do not become floors; the cutaway cuts them like walls.

### 4.5 Stock models: the game's own content (stage 2b)
The 819 distinct models that the 27 maps use and do not carry (`props_c17`, `props_wasteland`, `props_junk`, `models/props/cs_*` and the like) are Half-Life 2 and Counter-Strike: Source content. **Tried on 2026-10-23 with the downloader the backend already uses, with no Steam account:**

*   The game's own depots (app 4000: 4001, 4002) are **not** available anonymously ("not available from this account"); they need an account that owns the game, which this RFC does not ask anyone to hand over.
*   The **dedicated server apps are free and anonymous**, which is how operators install them: Garry's Mod Dedicated Server (app 4020: `sourceengine/hl2_misc`, `garrysmod/garrysmod`) and Counter-Strike: Source Dedicated Server (app 232330: `cstrike/cstrike_pak`, `hl2/hl2_misc`). Their `*_dir.vpk` index files are 0.2 to 0.7 MB each.
*   Reading the indexes against the 27 maps: **817 of the 819 models are there** (417 in `hl2_misc`, 391 in `cstrike_pak`, 9 in `garrysmod`). The two that are not: a Team Fortress 2 model (`props_2fort`) and one name with a stray `./` in front (normalised). The three files each model needs (`.mdl`, `.vvd`, `.dx90.vtx`) come to about 85 MB for all 27 maps.
*   **The cost is the archives, not the models.** The downloader fetches whole files, and the models sit inside archives of about 110 MB (`hl2_misc`: 4, 0.45 GB; `cstrike_pak`: 13, 1.4 GB; `garrysmod`: 3, 0.8 GB, needed only for 9 models). So the first stock model of a map costs up to about 1.9 GB of transfer.

**Built (2026-10-23, stage 2b).** Design: an **optional** setting (`GAME_CONTENT=1`, off by default, like `MAP_GEOMETRY`). The backend keeps the small index files, reads which archives hold the models that the maps in its queue are missing, downloads **those archives once**, copies the wanted `.mdl`, `.vvd` and `.vtx` files into a model cache (a few tens of MB; kept in the database or the volume), and **deletes the archives**. A model is cached by name, so the next map pays nothing for models already seen. If the free disk is too small for the largest archive, the job fails with that reason and the map keeps its mesh without stock props. The mesh of a model comes from the same reader as 4.3.

**How it works, as built.** `maps/vpk.ts` reads a package index (`*_dir.vpk`, versions 1 and 2; bounded: tree size, entry count; data in an archive, in the index itself, or preloaded). `services/gameContent.ts` holds the three sources (app 4020 `sourceengine/hl2_misc`, app 232330 `cstrike/cstrike_pak`, app 4020 `garrysmod/garrysmod`), fetches the index files once with the same helper (`-filelist`, exact paths), and for the models a map lacks looks up `.mdl`, `.vvd`, `.dx90.vtx`, downloads each archive that holds one of them (refusing an archive over 600 MB or when the free disk is under 1.5 times its size plus 64 MB), copies the three files of each model into the cache (`GAME_CONTENT_DIR`, default `.game-content`; one file per model and extension, named by a hash of the model path, so a name from a map never becomes a path), and deletes the archive. Model names from a map are normalised and anything that is not `models/….mdl` is never looked up. `buildPropSet` asks for the missing names in one batch; the answer is `{ models, error? }`, so a failed download costs the map only the models it could not get: the mesh is built, the page says "The game's own models could not all be fetched: …" (kept with the mesh as its note) and the map is not retried at every report. Switching `GAME_CONTENT` on raises the wanted mesh generation to 4, so stored meshes are rebuilt once with the game's models; switching it off again leaves them alone. Materials of the game's own models are not read (their `.vmt` files live in other packages), so they all count as solid. The map page says how many of the drawn models are the game's own.

**Measured with the real helper and real maps:** the three index files are 14 MB in all; `ttt_alps` fetched its 228 models (its other 5 were in the pack) in 55 s, drew **772 places of 236 models** (it was 56 of 8) in a 3.21 MB file, 28 places dropped by the triangle limit; `ttt_airbus_b3` added 135 models (911 places, 2.0 MB) in 73 s. The cache after Alps is 28 MB (233 models); a second build of Alps from the cache takes 0.5 s and no download.

**Decided (2026-10-23): the "Download GLB" link goes** (stage 1). Nobody had a use for it, and it was the only place where the dashboard offered the mesh as a file, which is where Valve's content would have been handed out. The viewer still fetches the mesh from the same address, signed in, to draw it, as any viewer fetches what it shows; the dashboard no longer offers the file. Stock props are drawn in their own group.

### 4.4 Spawns and parts (stage 3, built)
*   **Spawns** (already read for the skybox, RFC 11) go into the GLB's extras and are drawn as markers, by kind (`info_player_terrorist`, `info_player_deathmatch`, ...).
*   **Parts.** The triangles are grouped into connected parts by **shared vertices** (corners within 8 units), not by grid cells; this is also what the skybox rule of RFC 11 should use (see section 2: `airbus`). A part is a **main part** when it holds a spawn or at least 5% of the triangles; the other parts are *detached* (the two slabs of `airbus`, the stray floor piece beside `lego`). The default view shows the main parts only, and a **Parts** switch (Main / All) shows the rest. `kakariko`, two villages 8 000 units apart with a spawn room between them, keeps both villages as main parts (each holds over 40% of the triangles), and a "Part" filter can still pick one. The parts are listed in the extras with their boxes and counts. Nothing is deleted: a detached part may be a secret room or a teleport target, so it is hidden, not removed.
*   **Storeys or terrain.** The level model has a rule of its own for when there are no clear storeys: if no set of floor heights, within 96 units of each other, holds at least 60% of the floor area, the map has **no storeys**. The page then offers no "Up to level" buttons (nothing is hidden by a level that is not one) and colours by height (section 5). The mesh still lists its levels, flagged `storeys: false`.

## 5. The viewer
A **Colour** control next to the level buttons, three choices, kept in the browser (`localStorage`, never needed):

*   **Plain** (proposed default): one warm grey for floors and a lighter one for walls, props a third; shading from the light only. Form reads, nothing is announced.
*   **Height**: the same greys with a gentle tint by height, low to high, over the whole map (not per level), so a hill reads as a hill. Used automatically when a map has no storeys.
*   **Levels**: today's look, one hue per level, for people who want to count floors.

A **Show** group of switches: Walls (today), Water, Props, Glass and leaves (the foliage group), Spawns. Defaults: all on except Spawns. The levels' cutaway applies to world, fixtures and props alike (a prop is cut with the storey it stands on).

## 6. What we do not do
*   **Textures, for now.** Nothing is read from `.vtf`; the view is a layout, not a screenshot. That is a choice, not a limit: it is doable (the project above does it, and the game's own textures come from the same free dedicated-server apps as the models, 4.5), but it costs a texture decoder, material rules, and a mesh many times the size (tens of MB per map, not 2 to 15), and a replay's paths are easier to read over grey than over a picture. It would be an optional **Textured** view in a later stage, after stage 3; nothing in stages 1 to 3 stands in its way. (The short `.vmt` files are read for the foliage rule in 4.3.)
*   **Moving parts** (doors, lifts, trains). Not drawn at rest or in motion.
*   **Lighting, glass and fog volumes.**

## 7. Limits, safety and cost
*   Every offset, count and index in the pack, the `.mdl`, `.vvd`, `.vtx` and `sprp` data is checked against the size of what it points into; a file that fails a check loses that model (counted), not the whole map.
*   Limits, in the open so tests can pin them: at most 50 000 triangles per model, 600 000 triangles and 32 MB of GLB per map, 4 000 placements, 2 000 distinct models, 64 MB of pack members read per map, an inflate limit per member. Over a limit, the largest models go first, and the page says so.
*   The pack is read by range, never whole; only the directory and the chosen members. On `theship` (156 MB) that is a few MB.
*   **Rebuild.** A mesh gets a `version` (new column on `map_geometry`, schema v24). A row below the current version is rebuilt in the background by the same queue, one map at a time, **keeping the old mesh until the new one is ready**; a map that fails to rebuild keeps its old mesh. An admin can also ask for a rebuild of one map. Rebuilding means fetching the map again from the Workshop (about 3 GB for the 27 maps of this server, sequentially).
*   The replay draws the same file, so it gets the props too; tracks and the replay's controls do not change.
*   **Tests** use synthetic files (the builders in `test/helpers`, extended with a pack, a model and a prop lump), the Khronos validator on every output, and the limits above. The real maps are not kept in the repository; the survey script that produced section 2 is kept in `tools/` so the numbers can be repeated.

## 8. Decided and open questions
**Decided (2026-10-23):** the default colour is **Plain**; **water is on by default** (it is the truthful picture of a sea map, and it hides the sea bed in the cutaway views unless it is switched off).

**Open:**
1.  **How much to draw of a model**: the first level of detail only (proposed), or a simplified one on very large maps?
2.  **Archive cost** (4.5), decided: it is the operator's setting (`GAME_CONTENT=1`, off by default); the archives are fetched as the maps need them (an archive is about 110 MB), not all at once, and each is deleted after use.
3.  **One GLB or two** (world, then props)? One is simpler; two lets the 3D view show the layout at once and load the props after. Proposed: one file, groups inside; revisit if a map's GLB passes 15 MB.
4.  **Split the map code out as a package** (8b): the folder now and the package later (proposed), the package and a workspace now, or leave it in the backend?

## 8a. Prior art (looked at 2026-10-23)
A short search, not a survey; it does not prove that nothing else exists.

*   **noclip.website** (TypeScript, active: last push 2026-10-20). The biggest. Its source tree has a Source engine part (`BSPFile.ts`, `Studio.ts` for models, `VPK.ts`, `GMA.ts`, `VMT.ts`, `VTF.ts`, static props and scenes for Half-Life 2, Counter-Strike: Source and others). Licence: MIT, with a note that some parts were reverse-engineered and not written clean-room. It is a viewer application with materials, lighting and a WebGL renderer, not a library and not a batch converter, and its goal is the opposite of ours (look as the game looks, from files the visitor supplies).
*   **vbsp** (Rust, MIT, archived May 2025 and moved to Codeberg), with **vmdl** (the models) and **vbsp-to-gltf** (a command line from map to GLB, with a web page for it). Aimed at Team Fortress 2: textures and props are taken from a TF2 install. It does make **coloured, textured** output: its source reads the `.vmt` and `.vtf` files, writes the textures into the GLB as PNG with their coordinates and transforms, handles water materials and prop skins, and takes entity props as well as static ones. The last two show no licence on GitHub, and `vbsp-to-gltf` has none on Codeberg either (no `LICENSE` file; its last commit is from June 2025). A Rust program next to a Bun backend is another thing to build and ship, and a tool without a licence cannot be taken.
*   **Smaller viewers** (`Iskuri/BSP-Viewer` on three.js, `WebGL-BSP`, `bsp_viewer` on OpenGL, `BSPInspector`): faces and displacements, no props or models found.
*   **Nobody found does what this needs**: a server-side, bounded and checked reader of **untrusted** maps from the **Workshop** (addon archives, old LZMA items, ranges instead of whole files), that makes a **simplified GLB** with levels, water, foliage and parts, from which the replay and a 3D view are drawn with no textures and no WebGL-less fallback assumed.
*   **What to take from it**: noclip is the best check on the formats. Before the stage 2 readers are written, read its model and static-prop code against the spike: the places the spike is most likely to be wrong in an edge case are the `.vvd` fix-ups, body groups, the static-prop record versions and the `.vtx` strip types. Nothing is copied; if code were ever taken from it, the MIT licence and its note come along.

## 8b. Splitting the map code out
The code that reads maps and makes meshes (`bsp.ts`, `gma.ts`, `meshGlb.ts`, the level model of `mapplan.ts`, and in stage 2 the pack, model and VPK readers, and the Workshop fetcher) could be a package of its own, with a command line (`map-to-glb`), and the repository a workspace with `backend/`, `addon/`, `docs/` and `maps/`. For: a clean boundary, tests and licence of its own, other people can use it, and the survey script becomes one of its commands. Against, now: a workspace changes how the backend installs and is built, and whatever builds and ships the app today assumes `backend/` stands on its own; the API is still moving (three stages are planned).

**Done (2026-10-23, stage 1):** the cheap half. (The proposal was:) The map code lives in one folder inside the backend (`backend/src/maps/`), imports nothing from the rest of the backend (a test checks it), and has its own `README`. Extraction to a package is then a move, not a rewrite, and can be done when stage 2b is built and the interface has settled, or sooner if a second user appears. The folder is `backend/src/maps/` with a `README` and `test/maps_boundary.test.ts`; the package is decided in section 8, question 4.

## 9. Stages
**Stage 1 is built** (all in the 2026-10-23 commits, with 1 449 tests passing). What it does, and what was measured with the production code on real maps (not the prototype): `ttt_minecraft_haven` builds in 142 ms to a 0.88 MB file with 1 926 triangles of water and two levels (the slab is gone); `ttt_cquestt` loses the fake sky wall; `ttt_alps` keeps 271 triangles of water apart from its floors. The map code is in `backend/src/maps/`; liquid surfaces go to a `water` group outside the levels; material names are read (`isNotSurfaceMaterial`); brush entities that stand still are read (`FIXTURE_CLASSES`); `MESH_VERSION` is 2 and schema v24 adds `map_geometry.version`, so every stored mesh is rebuilt in the background, keeping the old one until the new one is in (a failed rebuild keeps it and says so on the map page); the map page has the Colour choice (Plain, Height, Levels; Plain by default; remembered in the browser) and a Water switch for maps with water; the Download GLB link is gone. The replay uses the same colouring (the choice made on the map page, Plain without one) and the same water, but has no colour control of its own yet. Not in stage 1: the skybox rule by shared vertices and the Parts switch (stage 3, 4.4).

1.  **Water, brush entities, the Colour control, sky and helper materials, and removing the Download GLB link** (4.1, 4.1a, 4.2, 5). No new file format is read; the biggest visible change for `haven`, `minecraft_b7_summer`, `island_2024`, `waterworld`, `skyislands`, `rooftops`. Includes the rebuild mechanism (7), because every stage changes the mesh.
2.  **Props from the map's pack** (4.3), **built**: the pack's ZIP directory, `sprp`, `.mdl`/`.vvd`/`.vtx`, and the foliage rule. The change for `lego`, `theship`, `cape_town` and the other packed-model maps.
**Stage 2 is built.** The pack (`pak.ts`), the static props and the entity props, the model reader (`model.ts`), the limits and the foliage rule (`propset.ts`, `PROP_LIMITS`: 4 000 places, 2 000 models, 50 000 triangles a model, 600 000 in all, props within 2 048 units of the map), and a `props` node in the mesh (a transform per place with a solid and a foliage child; each model's meshes are written once). `MESH_VERSION` is 3, so every stored mesh is rebuilt once more. The page has **Props** and **Glass and leaves** switches (the second only when a drawn model has such a material) and says how many models belong to the game and are not drawn. Measured with the production code on real maps: `ttt_lego` 79 places of 28 models (all in the map file), 60 362 triangles of props, built in 119 ms to a 0.58 MB file; `ttt_alps` 85 places of 10 models drawn and 233 models of the game not drawn, 150 ms; `ttt_cquestt` 97 places, 123 ms. The reader found the same models and triangle counts as the prototype. The first version of the foliage switch looked as if it did nothing on `lego` (it hides only 478 pixels of windows; the label was changed from "Foliage" to "Glass and leaves"), and on `alps` it drew the leaf cards as translucent fog, which looked terrible; foliage is now drawn solid and the cards are left out (`ttt_alps` after: 56 places of 8 models drawn, 2 models with cards left out). Known limits: only the first body part and the first level of detail are read, triangle lists only (no strips), the scale of a static prop (version 11 records) and of entity props is not read.
2b. **The game's own models** (4.5), behind its own setting, **built**: `maps/vpk.ts`, `services/gameContent.ts`, `GAME_CONTENT` and `GAME_CONTENT_DIR` (also an egg variable). The change for `alps`, `whitehouse`, `aircraft` and every map that uses `props_c17`-style content. Measured on `alps` and `airbus` in section 4.5.
3.  **Spawns, parts and the storeys rule** (4.4), **built**.

**Stage 3 is built.** What it does and what was measured on the 27 real maps with the production code (`maps/parts.ts`, `maps/levels.ts`, `maps/meshGlb.ts`; `MESH_VERSION` was 4, and 5 with the game's models; see the sliding brushes below for 6 and 7):

*   **A bug found by the survey, fixed first.** Brush entities (`func_brush`, `func_breakable`, …) are stored around their own origin, and the entity's `origin` and `angles` say where they stand. Stages 1 and 2 drew them at the world origin: windows, breakables and small fixtures were heaped near (0, 0, 0) (`cquestt` 55 of 55 fixtures, `skyislands` 255 of 329, `waterworld` 160 of 197 have an origin). `Fixture` now carries the origin and angles and the faces are turned and moved (`angleMatrix`, the same order of turns as the props).
*   **Parts by shared corners** (`findParts`): corners within about 4 units are one corner; triangles that share one are one part (union-find over a grid of 8 units; 40 to 150 ms a map). A part is **main** when a spawn point stands on it (found by the floor triangle under the spawn, so a spawn on one huge triangle works: all spawns of all 27 maps were placed) or it holds 5% of the triangles or more, or when it is near a main part (a corner within 256 units of a main corner, or resting within 48 units on a main floor, which is what a crate built of brushes on a big floor is); the rest are **detached**. A map always has a main part (the largest, if need be).
*   **The skybox by shared corners.** The old rule grouped cells of 1 024 units and left `lego`, `yucatan`, `desperados`, `forest` and `airbus` alone because the sky was joined to the map by the cells. The sky is now the parts that **hold the camera in their box** (padding 256) and have no spawn, plus every part with no spawn inside the box around those; never more than half of the triangles. First tried with "a corner within 1 536 units of the camera", which took the first village of `kakariko` (15 138 of 54 542 triangles) because the camera is 1 200 units from it; "in the box" does not. Measured: `kakariko` 3 200 triangles of sky (as before), `lego` 2 048 (one slab at z -1 024, which the cell rule had kept), `yucatan` 8 475 (the ground and pieces 3 000 units below the map), `desperados` 10 080, `forest` 512, `airbus` none left (the sky pieces were already left out as sky materials in stage 1).
*   **Detached parts** are kept, not deleted: they go into a `detached` group of the mesh, outside the levels (so a stray slab no longer makes a storey, as the one at z -6 144 in `airbus` did) and hidden by default; the page has a **Parts apart** switch when there are any. Measured: no detached triangles in 14 of the 27 maps; `airbus` 20 (the slab), `alps` 12, `minecraft_b7_summer` 1 140 (three pieces floating at z 2 300 to 2 500), `terrortrain` 335, `rooftops` 3 972 (skybox buildings the camera is not inside), `67thway` 1 010, `yucatan` 104, `bowling_v5a` and `pool` 104 each. The camera does not frame them.
*   **Storeys or terrain.** The share of floor within 48 units of the height of a storey (`storeyCoverage`) must be 60% or more. Measured: `alps` 0.42, `desperados` 0.55, `waterworld` 0.57 are terrain; `sandcrawler` is at the line (0.60); the others 0.62 (`airbus`) to 0.99. A map without storeys has no level buttons on its page (`data-storeys="0"`), says why, and opens in Height colour unless the person chose one; the levels are still in the file and the replay. The threshold is a rule of thumb from these 27 maps.
*   **Spawns** are in the file's extras (class and position, at most 512) and drawn as small cones, red for the terrorists', blue for the counter-terrorists', green for the rest, behind a **Spawns** switch (off by default).
*   **Parts that can move (found on `ttt_rooftops_2016_v1`, where the building a traitor can bring down was missing).** Doors, lifts and the like are part of how a map is laid out, so they are drawn, but marked: `func_door`, `func_door_rotating`, `func_movelinear`, `func_rotating`, `func_tracktrain`, `func_physbox` and `func_physbox_multiplayer` go into a `movable` group of the mesh, in orange whatever the colouring, outside the levels (so a lift's floor makes no storey and takes no part in the parts logic), cut by the level buttons, with a "Movable parts" switch that is on. They are drawn where the map file stores them, which is where the mapper built them: a door shut, a lift at its start, the collapsing building standing. Where the entity starts open or part way along its track, the engine moves it at spawn and this is not applied (not done). Buttons, triggers and anything with a parent stay out. Measured on the 27 maps: movable parts are 0 to 13% of the triangles (Airbus 5 970 of 45 458 with 143 models, Whitehouse 2 696, Rooftops 2 507 with 31 models of which the collapsing building is 615 triangles in a box of 1 344 by 1 024 by 2 008 units, Aircraft 1 890). An earlier version drew only sliders that travel at least 1 024 units, to keep doors out; that was dropped, because doors are layout. `MESH_VERSION` is 6, and 7 with the game's models, so stored meshes are rebuilt once more.
*   **Not done:** a "Part" filter to pick one part of a map, and listing every part (only the detached ones are in the file, with their boxes, up to 64).

Each stage is a commit series on its own, with this RFC updated to say what was built and what was measured.

Fixed, with a test on the vertex colours. The ramp is now deep blue (low), teal and yellow (high), mostly the ramp (85%) and a little of the surface's own colour, so walls stay a shade darker than floors. It is no longer blue to orange, because orange is the colour of the parts that move. The range is the middle 96% of the heights, so one spire or pit does not squeeze the rest of the map into one colour. The replay uses the same code.

*   **Leaves have their textures (2026-11-05, 4.6).** Foliage was drawn opaque, as a texture was needed to cut it out and none was read; a camera in a jungle saw only grey sheets. 4.6 reads the textures. The replay has a "Leaves" switch, on by default; see RFC 12.

### 4.6 The leaves of plants, with their textures (2026-11-05)
**Why.** 4.3 drew a foliage mesh (a material with `$alphatest` or `$translucent`) as opaque grey sheets because the texture that gives a leaf its shape was not read, and noted that this was a choice and not a limit. On Yucatan, a jungle map, 356 of 504 props are plants and a player standing among them saw only a pile of grey shards (the maintainer's replay of 2026-11-05). Hiding the leaves was tried and rejected as a way of not showing the problem. The textures are in the map's own pack, so they can be read.

**What is read.**
*   `maps/vtf.ts` reads a Source texture (`.vtf`): the header (7.0 to 7.6, the resource list of 7.3 and later), the formats the game's textures are in (DXT1 with and without one bit of alpha, DXT3, DXT5, and the plain 8-bit ones), one mip level (the largest of at most 256 on its longer side), the first frame. A cube map, a volume, a float or bump format, a cut-off file, a wrong version and sizes that are not credible give `null`, and the file is untrusted: 300 random corruptions of a good file never throw. `maps/png.ts` writes the pixels as a PNG.
*   `maps/model.ts` reads the texture coordinates of the model (they are in the `.vvd`, 40 bytes into each vertex) and keeps, for the foliage, a pair for each corner and runs of triangles with the paths their material could be at.
*   `maps/propset.ts` reads the `.vmt` of each foliage material in the pack (`parseVmt`: `$basetexture`, `$alphatest` or `$translucent`, `$alpha`), then the `.vtf` it names from the pack, once for the whole map. Limits (`LEAF_LIMITS`): 24 textures, 256 pixels on the longer side, 4 MB of PNG, one member at most 24 MB; over a limit, or with no texture in the pack, a material has none.

**What is written.** The foliage mesh of a model has a primitive for each material, with `TEXCOORD_0`, a material named `foliage` that is cut out (`MASK`, cutoff 0.5) for `$alphatest` and blended (`BLEND`, with `$alpha`) for `$translucent`, double sided, with its PNG as the base colour texture; materials and textures are shared by every model and every place. **Leaves with no texture are not written** (they were opaque grey sheets: not leaves); the report counts their triangles (`leavesWithoutTexture`). The mesh generation is raised (8, and 9 with the game's own models) so that stored maps are rebuilt with textures.

**What the viewers do.** The loader of the map (`mapLoader`) reads the images inside the file as images: the pages' policy has `connect-src 'self'`, which stops the loader's default way (a `fetch` of a `blob:` address) and left every leaf white; `img-src` already has `blob:`. The replay's "Leaves" switch is on by default.

**Measured** on the real files (Yucatan, 67th Way, Community Bowling): every foliage material is textured (8, 9 and 5 textures, 0.5 to 0.6 MB of PNG for a map, the mesh of Yucatan 1.22 to 1.82 MB), reading a texture takes 3 to 15 ms. Rooftops was not, before 4.7: 24 of its 26 leaf runs pointed at the game's own textures (the Counter-Strike bushes and metal grates), which are not in its pack (7,328 triangles).

**Tested.** `vtf.test.ts` (every format's pixels worked out by hand, the mip, the headers, the corrupt files), `png.test.ts`, the coordinates and runs in `model.test.ts`, and in `propset.test.ts` the textures read once for a map, the modes, the missing and unreadable and over-limit textures, shared textures, `parseVmt`, and the file written (primitives, coordinates, the PNG in it, a cut-out material, the validator, no mesh for bare leaves). In a browser: the same replay moment before (a pile of grey shards) and after (trees, bushes and grass).

### 4.7 The game's own textures for the leaves (2026-11-05)
**Why.** A foliage material of a map often points at a texture of the game itself: Rooftops' bushes are the Counter-Strike bushes of de_inferno, its ivy and grates are stock. Those textures are not in the map's pack, so 4.6 left such leaves out (7,328 triangles on Rooftops).

**How.** The helper of 4.5 already has the index of the game's packages and fetches the archive that holds a wanted file. It now fetches textures the same way: `gameContent().textures(paths)` takes `materials/<name>.vtf` paths (checked like the model names: lower case, no `..`, no odd characters), answers by the path that was asked, keeps each texture by name for good (`textures/` of the cache), deletes the archives, and shares the one queue with the models so an archive is not fetched twice. The Half-Life 2 textures (`hl2_textures_dir.vpk` of the Garry's Mod server files) are a source now, next to `hl2_misc`, the Counter-Strike pack and the Garry's Mod pack. `buildPropSet` collects the textures that leaves need and the pack does not have, asks for them in one request for the map (at most 64), and then uses them as the pack's own; the report says how many of the textures came from the game (`leafTexturesFromGame`). A failed or empty answer leaves the leaves bare, as in 4.6, and the report carries why.

**Measured** on the real download (2026-11-05, anonymous, no account): the glass texture of Half-Life 2 and a Counter-Strike bush came in 23 s with the three index files; Rooftops now has 16 textures, 14 of them the game's (the bushes, the ivy, the shield glass), and its mesh 4.5 MB (3.3 before): the inferno bushes, the ivy and the textures of the CD sleeves that the old test had taken for plants (translucent props) are drawn as the game draws them.

**Costs.** The first build of a map that needs textures fetches archives that were not needed for its models (a package is about 110 MB; cached textures are kept, the archives are not), once. The mesh generation (8, 9) already makes every stored map rebuild.

**Tested.** `gamecontent.test.ts`: names, the archives fetched and deleted, the answer by the path asked, the cache across a restart, a failed download, the archive limit, the shared queue; `propset.test.ts`: one request per map, the pack first, nothing, a failure and a file that is not a texture each leave the leaves bare with the reason.

### 4.8 How far the build is (2026-11-05)
A stored mesh of an older generation is rebuilt in the background and stays in place until the new one is ready, so after an update (4.6 and 4.7 raise the generation) nothing on any page said that anything was happening, or how much was left (the maintainer asked). `Store.getGeometryProgress()` counts, over the maps that were asked for (each map file once), how many wait for a build or a rebuild (no mesh yet, or one of an older generation), how many failed, and the total; `geometryBuilding()` names the map the queue is working on. The Maps page shows an admin one line while anything waits: "Building the 3D maps: 12 of 27 done, now Yucatan (1 failed). A map keeps its earlier 3D view until its new one is ready."; for a visitor it shows nothing, and once nothing waits the line goes. The count is for the instance (a map file is shared by the servers that have it). Tests in `map_geometry.test.ts`.

### 4.9 The game's own materials, light shafts and glass (2026-11-05)
**What was seen.** On the Yucatan replay a pale grey column 620 units tall with thin vertical lines stood in the plaza (the maintainer: "what is wrong with this texture?"). It was `models/effects/vol_light.mdl`, a volumetric light shaft: in the game an additive glow over a fountain, here a solid prop, because 4.3 asks "is this material see-through" only of material files in the map's pack, and the shaft's material (`materials/models/effects/vol_light001.vmt`, with `$additive 1` and no `$translucent`) is the game's own. A model of the game with a material of the game was always solid.

**What is read now.** After the files of every model are in (the pack's, then the game's own), the paths their materials could be at that the pack does not have are asked of the game's own files in one request (`gameContent().textures` takes `.vmt` as well as `.vtf` paths, kept apart in the cache; at most 600 paths, most of which the index says do not exist and cost nothing) and read like the pack's. Then the models are read, so a material of the game that is see-through makes its mesh foliage-like with its texture, as in 4.6. Checked on Yucatan: the shaft is now a glow and the fountain under it shows; 3 more models are drawn with textures (21, was 18) and 3 textures came from the game.

**Additive materials.** `$additive` (a light shaft, a glow, a sprite) adds its colour to what is behind it, which a glTF material cannot say. Its texture's brightness is made its alpha (0.7 of the brightest channel), so dark is nothing and bright a soft glow, blended.

**Glass.** A `$translucent` material whose texture has no alpha channel and that names no `$alpha` is drawn at `0.4` instead of opaque (the see-through of a window that comes from the lightmap in the game, which is baked into the map and not in any file read here). One with an `$alpha` or with alpha in its texture keeps its own.

**Tested.** `parseVmt` (additive, explicit alpha), the game's materials asked once and read before the models, an additive shaft not solid and its texture, the default for glass, and the fetching of `.vmt` files in `gamecontent.test.ts`.

### 4.10 What it would cost to texture the whole map (2026-11-05)
Asked after 4.6 to 4.9: the leaves have textures and nothing else does ("a partially textured map"). Measured on six maps with their files (the materials of the faces that are drawn; where the texture lives, in the pack or in the game's own files):

| Map | Materials of faces | In the pack | The game's own (archives) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Yucatan | 164 | 84 | 30 (10) |
| Rooftops | 723 | 532 | 179 (23) |
| 67th Way | 220 | 165 | 51 (9) |
| Airbus | 604 | 542 | 60 (13) |
| Cape Town | 30 | 28 | 2 (1) |
| Bowling | 40 | 34 | 5 (5) |

**What it takes.** (1) Texture coordinates for the faces of the map: from the `texinfo` vectors and the size of the texture (read from its header), carried through every step that now works on positions alone (the cut into storeys, the crop, the water, the movable parts): each must interpolate them. (2) A primitive for each material of each storey: at 30 to 720 materials a map, 6 storeys, thousands of draw calls, which a phone cannot do, so only the largest materials by area can have their texture, the others a flat colour. (3) The textures themselves: at 128 to 256 pixels a PNG is 40 to 90 KB, so 100 textures are 4 to 9 MB a map (the largest maps would be tens), against a mesh of 1 to 4 MB now; the game's own textures need up to 23 archives of 110 to 600 MB fetched once. (4) What a map does not carry: lightmaps (the shadows and the colour of the light are baked), the blend of displacement terrain, environment maps; textured and unlit it looks flat and too bright. (5) A decision about the picture: the views colour the storeys on purpose (plain, by height, by level) so a replay can be read; a textured world competes with the players and the paths, so it would be a mode of its own.

**Two sizes of the job.** A *colour for each material* (the average of its texture, per face, no coordinates, no draw calls, no textures in the file) gives bricks brown and grass green and costs the fetching and decoding of every texture once and 3 bytes a vertex; about the size of 4.6. *Real textures* with coordinates, a budget of materials by area and the rest flat is several times that: the coordinates through the pipeline, the grouping, the budget and the viewer's mode, and the maps would be 5 to 15 MB each. Neither is built.


## 5. Textured floors and walls (2026-11-05)

Decided after 4.10: texture the whole map ("2. but all colours turned off (leaves too) for the colour-by-storey view").

**Faces.** The map reader keeps, for each triangle, the face it came from, and for each face how it is textured (the `texinfo` vectors, the size of the texture, the material name; brush entities with their placement undone). A corner's texture coordinate is `u = (x*s0 + y*s1 + z*s2 + s3) / width`, likewise `v`; a whole number of tiles is taken off each triangle so the numbers stay small. Displacements carry the mapping of their face.

**Materials.** The materials are ranked by the ground they cover; the 160 largest are looked at. A material file comes from the map's pack or from the game's own files; a patch material (what a map makes for a cubemap) is laid over the material it includes. The 24 largest get their texture at up to 128 px (all PNG held to 3 MB); the next get one plain colour, the average of the texture taken to 8 shades per channel, so near colours share a material; the rest are the plain floor or wall. Materials that name one texture the same way are one material. Anything that cannot be read is left out, never an error.

**Cost, measured on Yucatan:** 150 materials looked at, 150 resolved, 134 textured, 15 coloured; 23 textures from the game's own files; the mesh 3.6 MB with 35 textures (1.2 MB of PNG); 175 primitives, down from 585 before materials were shared.

**Views.** Textured is the first and default colouring and replaces Plain (a saved Plain choice is Textured). Height and Levels are the colour-by-storey views and have none of the texture colour: surfaces are the plain grey tinted by height or storey, and leaves keep only their cut-out shape in a flat colour. A mesh made before this has no textures and shows the plain greys under Textured. **Removed 2026-11-06:** the Height and Levels views and the switch (on the map page, in the replay and in the 2D fallback) are gone; the map is always drawn textured, and a surface with no texture is the plain floor or wall grey.

**Not yet:** solid props are still one flat colour; lightmaps (the baked shadows) are not carried: they need the map's lightmap lump packed into an atlas with a second set of coordinates, which is its own piece of work.

**Solid props (2026-11-05).** A solid model's meshes carry their texture coordinates and runs of triangles by material, as the plants' do. Each material of a run is the first of its possible paths that exists (the map's pack, then the game's own); its texture is read at up to 64 px into the one book the leaves use (48 textures, 5 MB of PNG for the whole prop set), and one that does not fit becomes the average colour of its texture; a run with neither is the plain prop colour. The viewer shows them in Textured and as the plain prop colour in Height and Levels, with the leaves as their cut-out shape in a flat colour. Yucatan: 25 textures in the props (16 from the game's own files), the mesh 3.8 MB.

**Movable parts (2026-11-05).** The parts that can move (doors, a collapsing building) were the one orange thing left. Each carries the face of every triangle, with the texture's own mapping (their triangles are local to the part, so nothing is undone), their ground counts when materials are ranked, and they are written like the levels: a primitive for each material. A part whose material did not make the budget (the doors of Rooftops) is the plain prop colour, not orange; in Height and Levels all movable parts are that plain colour. Measured: Rooftops 695 materials, 160 looked at, 65 textured, 93 plain colours, the mesh 4.4 MB; 67th Way 4.8 MB.

**Which map is done (2026-11-05, to 4.8).** The progress line says how many, not which. An admin now sees it on each map's card on the Maps page, in the one pill that links to the 3D view: `3D · v10` when the mesh is of the generation the backend wants (green), `3D · v9 · waiting` while an earlier one is shown and the new one is not ready, `3D · v9 · building…` for the map being made now (both amber), and a pill of its own, `3D queued` / `3D failed`, for a map with no mesh to link to. Nobody else sees them.

**A generation for each change to what a mesh holds (2026-11-05).** The solid props and the movable parts were added to the meshes after the generation of the textured floors had been deployed, without a new generation: the maps rebuilt for the first one kept their orange movable parts and untextured props, and showed `3D v10` all the same. Any change to what the extractor writes now raises the generation (10 and 11 with the game's own models).

**Two textures on one floor (2026-11-05).** "The floor is grass, not grey dirt": the ground of 67th Way is a `WorldVertexTransition` material, dirt (`$basetexture`) with grass (`$basetexture2`) laid over it where the alpha of the displacement's corners says so, and only the first was drawn. A displacement triangle now has a record of its own with the mean alpha of its corners in five steps (0 the first texture, 4 the second), and such a material is five materials, from the first texture to the second, each a small PNG mixed at build time (the second stretched to the size of the first). It is blocky where the map's blend is smooth (a step for each triangle, not each pixel), and costs five textures for each blended material; a shader that mixes two textures by vertex colour would be exact and is the next step if this is not good enough. The map page's own text no longer says that parts that move are orange or that there is a Plain colouring.

**Serving the mesh (2026-11-06).** `mesh.glb` was sent with `no-store`, uncompressed, so every view downloaded the whole file again (first look at a real map: about 0.5 s of download, after about 0.5 s queued in the browser; size not yet noted). It now carries an `ETag` and `Cache-Control: private, no-cache`: the browser keeps the file and asks again each time, a few bytes answer `304`, and the sign-in check and the view log still run on every request. It is sent gzipped where the browser accepts it (compressed once per version and kept for the last eight). **Measured on all 27 stored meshes (2026-11-06; a first note, taken from one map only, is replaced by this).** Raw, a mesh is 1.6 to 5.5 MB; gzipped (what is sent now) 0.4 to 3.4 MB, median 1.9 MB, which is about 40% less than raw. Textures (never more than 88 images per map) do not compress: 0.03 to 1.9 MB, median 0.83 MB, and **6% to 73% of the gzipped download, median 42%; for 9 of the 27 maps more than half.** The geometry compresses to about half, the glTF JSON well. The largest textures-first case is `ttt_community_bowling_re_v1b` (1.83 of 2.50 MB); the largest file is `ttt_alps` (3.4 MB gzipped, 1.6 of it textures). A separate texture file would therefore save the first paint 0.8 MB on a typical map and up to 1.8 MB, which is 0.1 to 0.2 s at the 9 MB/s seen on a laptop and 0.4 to 0.9 s at 2 MB/s. Whether that is worth a second file, a second request and a mesh-version change is open; the first look at one map also showed 0.5 s queued in the browser before the request started, which a smaller file does not change.

**What came before the mesh (2026-11-06).** In a browser's network panel the request for `mesh.glb` started 517 ms after the navigation ("Queued" there is a point in time, not a wait). The page's script is `defer` and asks for the mesh only when it runs, and `mapview.js` (three.js and the viewer) was served as 662 KB, uncompressed and with no cache header (`replay.js` 719 KB), although a comment said it was kept for a week. Scripts are now sent with `Cache-Control: public, max-age=604800, immutable` when their address carries the version (`?v=`, which it always does), `no-cache` otherwise, and gzipped where accepted (about a quarter of the size). Not done: starting the mesh download from the page head, in parallel with the script (`<link rel="preload">`), which would hide most of what is left on a first visit.
