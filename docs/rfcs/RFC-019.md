# RFC 19: The Replay and the Map in the Game's Window, Without WebGL

> **Status: stage 1 built (2026-11-04): the replay is drawn from above on a 2D canvas when WebGL is missing. Stage 2 (the 3D map on the canvas) is not started. Tried in Chromium with WebGL switched off, not yet in the game's own window.** Follows RFC 17 (3b) and RFC 18.

## 1. Abstract
The game's built-in browser is Chrome 86 with **WebGL switched off** (seen on a real server: "WebGL is not available"). Chrome 86 itself supports WebGL; the game turns it off, and neither the dashboard nor the addon can turn it back on for a player's computer. So today the replay and the 3D map cannot be drawn in the game's window: the page hides their controls, keeps the lists, and offers **Open in your browser** (RFC 17, 3b). This RFC proposes drawing them there anyway, with the 2D canvas, which every browser has.

## 2. What is there
*   The map page and the replay draw with three.js (the WebGL renderer, the glTF loader and the orbit controls). With no WebGL, `makeRenderer` throws and `explainNoWebGL` takes over.
*   three.js ships `SVGRenderer` (a renderer that needs no WebGL) in its examples. It makes one page element per triangle, so it is for a few thousand triangles, not for a map of 40 000 to 100 000.
*   Measured on 2026-10-23 with a plain 2D canvas and the painter's algorithm (sort the triangles by depth, fill them back to front), in a browser with WebGL disabled, on the real meshes of `ttt_minecraft_haven` (53 085 triangles) and `ttt_cquestt` (43 020): **70 to 85 ms a frame**, about 13 frames a second, flat colours with no lighting, and legible: the castle, its water and its levels can be read. Not yet tried in the game's own browser.

## 3. Proposal

### 3.1 The replay, top-down (the part worth doing first)
The replay is mostly a top-down picture. From above nothing needs sorting by depth: each level's floors are filled shapes, drawn lowest level first; walls are outlines; then the players, the tracks and the markers. It is the work of a few hundred triangles' worth of drawing per frame at most (floors only), so it is fast even in Chrome 86 with no GPU.

*   The same payload as today (`background.kind = 'mesh'`), the same level bar and controls, the same colours (RFC 18, 5: Plain by default). The page picks WebGL when it can and the canvas when it cannot; one `Renderer` interface in `sceneKit.ts`, two implementations.
*   Floors are taken from the mesh's level nodes, projected on the ground plane. The cutaway ("up to level") hides the levels above, as now.
*   The picture is a still of the map with the players moving over it; the map layer is drawn to an off-screen canvas **once per view change** (zoom, pan, level), and each frame only copies it and draws the players. That keeps the frame cost independent of the number of triangles.
*   What is lost from the WebGL replay: the 3D perspective view of the replay (the top-down view stays), lighting, and props if RFC 18 stage 2 makes them heavy. What stays: every control and list, the positions, the levels, the colours.

### 3.2 The 3D map view, roughly (optional, after 3.1)
The same painter's algorithm as measured above, for the map page's "3D" view: rotate by dragging, flat shading from the face normal and a fixed light (cheap and it makes the form readable, which the measured version lacked), water drawn last and translucent. About 13 frames a second for a 50 000-triangle map is enough to turn it with a mouse; while dragging it can draw a thinned set (every third triangle) and draw everything when the mouse stops.

*   Needs a **triangle budget**: the world, the water and the fixtures only. Props (RFC 18, 4.3) can reach 450 000 triangles on a map, far too many for this; this view leaves them out, or draws only the largest models.
*   Depth-sorting by the centre of the triangle gives errors where triangles are very different in size (a floor of two huge triangles under a building); the mesh already cuts walls at storey heights (RFC 11), which helps, and a floor can be drawn before everything else in a level without being sorted at all. Where this is still wrong, the view is a rough one, and says it is ("A simple view: open the page in your browser for the full one").

## 4. What we do not do
*   Turn WebGL on in the game: not ours to do; it is a setting of the game's browser on each player's computer.
*   Use `SVGRenderer` (section 2).
*   Render pictures on the backend (a drawing library on the server, an image per map, view and level): possible, and images cache well, but it adds a dependency and a job to the backend for something the browser can do.
*   A WebGL fallback to software (SwiftShader) inside the game: not under our control either.

## 5. Open questions
1.  **How rough is acceptable for 3.2?** Shading by the normal and a fixed light, or flat colours as measured?
2.  **Is a replay that is top-down only acceptable in the game's window?** The 3D perspective view stays in the browser.
3.  **Test.** The game's window cannot be driven from outside; the check is a Chromium started with WebGL disabled (as for the measurement), plus the Chrome 86 container of RFC 17 (3a) to prove the page loads and draws there, and one try in the game by a person.

## 5b. Stage 1 as built
*   `replayflat.ts` draws; `replay.ts` is unchanged for browsers with WebGL. Where `makeRenderer` throws, the replay starts `startFlat` instead of showing the "no WebGL" message. The playback, speed, level bar, Follow-the-level box, Walls box and Theater controls were moved in front of the WebGL set-up and are shared by both drawings (a small `hooks` object says what a change does: paint, level, walls, resize).
*   The map: every `level-N` node of the map's mesh becomes triangles (floors) and lines (walls, the longest edge of each wall triangle), in Source's x and y, **grouped by colour**, so a level is a handful of fills. The colouring is the one chosen on the map page (Plain by default), read from the same `applyColouring`. Water is drawn last and translucent. A map with only a floor plan draws the plan's cells; a map with neither draws a grid.
*   The picture of the map is kept in an off-screen canvas twice the size of the window and copied each frame; it is made again when the level cutaway or the Walls box changes, when the view has moved out of the kept picture or zoomed by a quarter, and 180 ms after a zoom stops. The players, trails, kill lines, revival marks and death crosses are drawn over it every frame at one size on the screen.
*   Controls: **From above** (drag to move, wheel to zoom at the pointer, double-click to fit) and **Follow** (the selected player stays in the middle, wheel zooms; dragging leaves the player and keeps the zoom). The **3D** button is hidden. Where the game gives the page `traitordash.open`, an **Open in your browser for the 3D view** button is added.
*   Measured on 2026-11-04, `ttt_airbus_b3` (46 000 triangles, four levels), Chromium with WebGL off, at twice the pixel ratio and the processor slowed four times: From above at 8x speed, 60 frames a second (worst frame 33 ms); Follow, 24 ms on average, with a worst frame of 0.5 s when the kept picture is made again (about 0.13 s on an ordinary processor).
*   Open question 2 is answered: the replay in the game's window is from above only; the 3D view stays in the browser.

## 6. Stages
1.  **The canvas renderer behind one interface, and the replay top-down in it** (3.1). Built; the "one interface" turned out to be the shared controls plus a separate drawing file (5b).
2.  **The 3D map view on the canvas** (3.2), if 3.1 is good enough in the game that the rest is worth having.
