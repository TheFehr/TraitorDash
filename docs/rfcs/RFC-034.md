# RFC 34: A Clean Hole for the Follow Camera (a Circle on the Screen, and a Ghost of the Player)

> **Status: built (2026-11-15), see section 9 (as built); a defect found after deployment is fixed in 9.4 (2026-11-16).** Asked for by the maintainer after a round replay on `ttt_minecraft_b7_summer` ("our peek-through on follow still has issues"): the hole the follow camera makes in the walls is a fan of noisy grain and large triangular wedges. Replaces (supersedes) the cone fade of RFC 12, sections 12 and 14 (the line-of-sight fade in `replay.ts`: `CUT_GLSL`, `seeThrough`, `cutAt`, the per-object `aObject`). Chosen from the research of 2026-11-15 and the prototype of section 3 (a three.js scene, headless Chromium; the pictures are in the maintainer's conversation, the code is section 4).

## 1. Abstract
The follow camera orbits behind a player. What lies between the camera and the player is cut away so the player is seen: every fragment in a world-space cone around the line from the camera to the head is discarded with a screen-door pattern (interleaved gradient noise), walls and floors per pixel and props per object (RFC 12, 12 and 14). It is noisy (a wide stipple band), and it makes **straight-edged wedges** through walls and floors.

This RFC replaces the cone with **one rule for walls, floors and props: a circle on the screen around the player's head, applied only to what is nearer than the player**, with a hard clean edge, and adds **a ghost of the player** drawn over whatever still covers them. No noise, no per-object values, no world-space cone.

## 2. Why it looks bad now (found 2026-11-15, reproduced in the prototype from a copy of the real shader)
*   **The wedges are not a property of large triangles.** The world position used by the cut is interpolated exactly per pixel. They come from the **hard `return 0.0` branches of `cutAt`**: a step from fully faded to fully solid on a world-space plane (the last 8 units before the head; a floor height of "feet minus 24" or "ground plus 16"; the overhead rule's `p.y < head + 16` and `along < 0`). A step on a plane cuts a straight line through a wall or a floor, and a wall that passes near the player is solid on one side of that line and open on the other (in the prototype, a solid triangle across the player's leg).
*   **Props and walls follow two rules.** A prop is faded as one block by a per-vertex value (no hole shape), and a prop above `BIG_PROP` (60 units) switches to the per-pixel rule, so the same kind of wall looks different from one object to the next.
*   **The grain.** The fade band is up to 45 world units wide near the camera, which on screen is a wide stipple of a pattern that is made to be accumulated over frames (it is not, here). The renderer caps the pixel ratio at 1.5 on a touch screen (`sceneKit.ts`) while a phone's pixel ratio is 3, so every pattern pixel is drawn as a 2 x 2 block.

## 3. What was compared (prototype, 2026-11-15)
Same scene and camera (huge floor triangles, long wall quads, an upper slab, merged props, a capsule player; the camera 300 units back and 170 up, field of view 40, as in `replay.ts`), three.js 0.186, Chromium with a software renderer (4x MSAA available), at pixel ratios 1, 1.5 and 3. Treatments: A the current cone; B a circle with the same noise; C a circle with a 4x4 Bayer pattern (cell = the pixel ratio) and a narrow band; **D a circle with a hard edge and no noise, plus the ghost**; E a circle with alpha-to-coverage. Result: A makes the wedges; B is round but grainy; C is tidier but still a stipple; **D is the cleanest: a crisp circular window, no grain, no wedges**, with a small straight remnant of a wall beside the player where the wall really is; E gives four concentric rings (4x MSAA has five coverage levels) and a pale glow whose cause was not found. D is the choice. Not proven: the look on a real phone GPU (all pictures are from a software renderer).

Other findings that shape the choice (read in full, 2026-11-15): Valve's CS2 demo viewer does not cut walls at all, it draws player outlines through them (`spec_show_xray`); the Bombernauts "crop circle" article uses a screen circle and a per-face test (below); a depth-fail second draw of the character is the standard silhouette technique and the three.js forum has the recipe. No primary source describes the method of a shipped commercial game; that is not claimed.

## 4. Design
### 4.1 The rule (one function for walls, floors and props)
In the fragment stage, from the view-space position `P = -vViewPosition` and uniforms only (no per-vertex value, no `aObject`, no world-space cone):

```glsl
uniform vec3 uCtr;      // the player's centre (the chest), view space: ctr.applyMatrix4(camera.matrixWorldInverse)
uniform vec4 uGround;   // the plane they stand on, view space: (n, -n.dot(feetPoint))
uniform float uRad;     // the hole's radius in world units at the player's distance
float holeAt(vec3 P) {
  float dz = (-uCtr.z) - (-P.z);              // > 0: nearer to the camera than the player
  float near = smoothstep(-56.0, -16.0, dz);  // the body's depth is cut; the ramp lies BEHIND the body, so no straight edge
  if (near <= 0.0) return 0.0;
  float h = dot(uGround.xyz, P) + uGround.w;  // height above the floor they stand on
  vec3 nf = normalize(cross(dFdx(P), dFdy(P)));
  if (abs(dot(nf, uGround.xyz)) > 0.6 && h < 14.0) return 0.0; // the floor under their feet stays
  vec2 d = P.xy / -P.z - uCtr.xy / -uCtr.z;   // a true circle on the screen
  float R = uRad / -uCtr.z;                   // the radius shrinks with distance
  return near * (1.0 - smoothstep(R * (1.0 - uBand), R, length(d)));
}
```
After `#include <alphatest_fragment>`: `if (holeAt(-vViewPosition) > 0.5) discard;`. The edge is **hard** (`uBand` small, no noise): the clean circle of treatment D. (A soft band with a Bayer pattern, treatment C, stays available as a setting of the constant `uBand` and a pattern line, if the hard edge looks harsh on a real phone; it is not the default.) The `dFdx/dFdy` face normal needs WebGL2 (three.js 0.186 is WebGL2 only).

### 4.2 The ghost
The followed player is drawn a second time: the same geometry, a flat colour (the player's team colour or white) at about 50% opacity, `depthFunc = GreaterDepth`, `depthWrite = false`, `renderOrder` after the opaque scene, so it shows **only where something still covers the player** (a small remnant of the cut, an object the circle does not reach). It guarantees the player is never lost. One extra draw of one model.

### 4.3 Walls beside the player (a second step, built after the first is checked)
Only a wall that really separates the camera from the player should be cut. The Bombernauts test: a face is cut only when the camera and the player lie on opposite sides of its plane (`dot(n, cam - point)` and `dot(n, ctr - point)` of opposite signs; the face normal from the derivatives as above). A wall beside the player and not between is then kept whole. Added to `holeAt` as a multiplier, behind a constant, and judged on the same recorded moments.

### 4.4 What is removed
`CUT_GLSL`/`cutAt`, the `seeThrough` kinds `prop`/`wall`/`floor`, the per-vertex `aObject` attribute on merged props (if nothing else uses it; check `mergeDrawn` and the moved-props logic), `BIG_PROP`, the `lineOfSight` uniforms and their per-frame update, and the overhead fade rules of `replaylogic.ts` (`overheadFade`, `FADE`) whose job the circle takes over. What stays: the follow camera itself (position, damping, the level logic of RFC 12, 14), the level bar, Walls, and the camera cut for the orbit and top cameras.

### 4.5 Per frame
The player's chest position (the head minus a fixed drop) and feet point go to view space; `uGround` is built from the world up axis (Source z, three.js y) and the feet point; `uRad` follows the player's size (a constant in world units, about 36 at first; tuned on the recorded moments). Nothing else changes per frame.

## 5. Not changed, and not planned
*   The camera position and damping, the level logic, and the other cameras.
*   Alpha-to-coverage (E): rings and an unexplained glow; reconsidered only if a real phone shows a reason.
*   Camera pull-in on collision: the research and RFC 12 both show it jitters and zooms into the player in multi-storey maps.
*   Hiding a floor or a room by data (storey ids, BSP visibility): a possible later help for ceilings, not needed for the hole.

## 6. Tests (what must fail on a broken build)
*   The shader source: one rule for all three material kinds (`seeThrough` is gone; a single function patched into the standard materials of walls, floors and merged props); no `aObject`, no `lineOfSight`, no `cutAt` left; the uniforms the rule needs exist and are updated each frame from the player's pose (a test on the page code with a stand-in scene, as the replay's other tests do).
*   The uniform math in plain code (extracted into `replaylogic.ts` or a small module so it is testable): the player's centre and the ground plane in view space for a given camera and player, the radius shrinking with distance, and a unit test of the same `holeAt` written in JavaScript (the shader is a transcription of it): a point nearer than the player and inside the circle is cut, a point outside the circle, behind the player, or on the floor under the feet is not; **no discontinuity**: the value changes by at most a small step between points one pixel apart (the cause of the wedges).
*   The ghost: the player's mesh is drawn twice, the second with `GreaterDepth` and no depth write, and it follows the followed player only.
*   The replay's other behaviour is unchanged (level logic, cameras, the lying-place list); the existing replay tests pass (those that tested the cone's constants are replaced by the above).
*   Layout, contrast: nothing in the markup changes.

## 7. Check on real moments (required before it is called done)
Render the replay's own scene code with the stored mesh of `ttt_67thway_rework_b1` and the recorded round of 2026-11-06 (`track-samples`), and with `ttt_minecraft_b7_summer`, in Chromium at a phone size (the same camera as the follow camera would have), before and after, at: the moments RFC 12 names for 67th Way (0:57, 0:58: the sunken passage, the crates, the second player) and three or four others where the old cut was wrong (a wall close to the player, a ceiling above, a ledge at the feet); judge: the player is seen, no wedges, no grain, the hole reads as a clean window, the floor under the feet stays. The pictures and the judgement go into this RFC's as-built section.

## 8. What cannot be verified here
*   The look on a real phone GPU and the choice of the hard edge over the soft one: the prototype ran on a software renderer. The constants (radius, depth ramp, floor height) are tuned on recorded moments, not on a device.
*   Moments of the owner's `b7_summer` round (the screenshot): only the 67th Way round is in the samples; the replay of `b7_summer` is checked with a synthetic follow path through its map.

## 9. As built (2026-11-15)
Code: `backend/src/followHole.ts` (the rule in the shader, `holeAt` in plain code, `updateHole`, the ghost), `sceneKit.ts` (`mergeDrawn` moved here, without `aObject`; the renderer has a stencil buffer), `replay.ts`. Removed: `CUT_GLSL`/`cutAt`, `seeThrough`, `aObject`, `BIG_PROP`, `lineOfSight`, `screenDoor`, and `FADE`/`overheadFade` in `replaylogic.ts` with their tests. Tests: `backend/test/followhole.test.ts` (27), and the whole suite is 2,402 tests, `tsc` and `bun run build` clean. RFC 12, sections 12 and 14 are marked superseded by a pointer in RFC-012.md.

### 9.1 What was built, and where it differs from the design
*   **One rule, fragment stage only** (4.1): walls, floors and every material of the props (also the ones that are not merged) get `holeAt(-vViewPosition) > 0.5` after the alpha test. Water, decoration and the movable parts are not patched (as before: the old fade did not touch them either); the ghost covers a player behind one of them.
*   **No branch makes a step** (the design's lesson). The design's floor exception `abs(n.up) > 0.6 && h < 14` was itself a hard branch, a step on a plane for a slope that crosses that height; it is now a smooth ramp (`upFrom/upTo`, `floorFrom/floorTo`). The face normal from `dFdx/dFdy` is taken before any early return (derivatives in non-uniform control flow are undefined). The edge is `band` = 4% of the radius (a hard edge, no noise); the soft Bayer variant of the RFC was not built (it stays a note: widen `band` and add a pattern line).
*   **Numbers tuned on the recorded moments** (all in `HOLE`): radius 80 (the RFC said "about 36", the prototype 70; at 56 the window is a small porthole on a phone, at 110 it is as wide as the screen), centre 40 above the feet, the ramp behind the chest -30 to -14 (the design's -56 to -16 cut a wall standing 40 units behind the player and showed the black void behind it, found at 0:57 on 67th Way).
*   **The ghost** (4.2) is the player's meshes drawn again in the team colour at 0.5, `GreaterDepth`, no depth write, `renderOrder` 5 (the pins are 10); skinned meshes share their skeleton. **Added to the design:** a stencil. Without it a limb behind the chest is "behind" it too and the ghost showed as pale patches on the player's own body (seen in the first picture); the body now marks the stencil and the ghost is drawn only where it is not marked. Needs `stencil: true` in `makeRenderer`. Shown only for the followed player in the follow camera while that player is drawn (`ghostShown`); not for the body that lies.
*   **Per frame** (4.5): `updateHole` runs after the follow camera is placed, from the player's drawn pose (not the settled camera's), works the camera matrices out itself, and any other camera sets the radius to 0 (no hole).
*   **The side test (4.3) is built and OFF** (`HOLE.sideTest`). On 67th Way it was neutral or slightly better (the pool edge at 0:44 stays whole where the plain rule leaves a notch and a fan of light blue). On `b7_summer` it kept wall pieces in front of the player's upper body in the tower (0:22, 0:55), so the player was carried by the ghost at half strength: the rule "when in doubt the hole wins over the wall" decides against it. It stays a constant for a later look.

### 9.2 Checked on real moments (2026-11-15)
The replay's own scene code in a harness (not committed): `mapLoader`, `prepareProps`, `mergeDrawn`, `makeRenderer`, `patchHole`, `updateHole` and the ghost from the sources, the old cone copied verbatim from main for the BEFORE pictures; real Chromium (software GL), 360 x 580 CSS pixels, device pixel ratio 3 with the renderer's cap of 1.5, the camera as `replay.ts` places it, the recorded players' poses from `poseAt`. Data: `ttt_67thway_rework_b1` and the round of 2026-11-06 (0:05, 0:12, 0:28, 0:36, 0:44, 0:57, 0:58, 1:00, 1:08), and the owner's `ttt_minecraft_b7_summer` round (following Scip.MLP: 0:00, 0:03, 0:06, 0:09, 0:15, 0:22, 0:29, 0:40, 0:55, 1:10, 1:30, 1:50, 2:10 to 3:50).
*   **Player seen:** yes at every moment looked at (about 40 pairs). **Wedges gone:** at `b7_summer` 0:29 the old cut put a red triangle of wall across the player's chest and legs, a dithered ring at the feet and grain over the whole tower; the new picture is a clean window with the shelves and the floor, and the figure whole. **Grain gone:** no dither anywhere; the edge is a clean circle. **Floor under the feet stays:** yes, also on the stairs of 0:57 (the risers of the stairs are wall faces and are notched at the edge of the circle, small).
*   **The ghost** (a test with the radius cut to 22 at `b7_summer` 0:22, 0:29, 0:55): where a wall covers the body the ghost shows its silhouette in the team colour; the visible part of the body has no patches (stencil).
*   **Frame to frame** (11 frames 50 ms apart, the mean absolute difference per channel over the picture, as RFC 12 measured; the camera is placed straight on each pose, no settling, so the player's own motion dominates): 67th Way 0:57 largest step 23.6 (old 23.2, nothing cut 20.2), 67th Way 0:20 33.5 (old 41.4, nothing cut 13.6), `b7_summer` 0:22 17.3 (old 23.1, nothing cut 14.8), `b7_summer` 0:29 0 (the player stands still: the circle does not flicker by itself). So the new hole adds less to the motion than the old cone did, but at 0:20 it is still 20 above the motion alone: a window onto a different surface each frame is a change of the picture, not a flicker of one pixel. Not a regression, not fixed to zero.
*   **What is worse than the old cut (honest):** the window is a porthole. Under a big roof or ceiling that covers the screen (67th Way 0:05, 0:12, 0:28, 1:08) the old cone showed the surroundings through a wide, noisy band and the new shows only the circle, the rest is the roof. The radius 80 is a compromise on a 360 px wide screen (it reaches the edges). Where the map has no faces behind a cut wall the window shows the black background (67th Way 0:36, `b7_summer` at the edge of the world), as before. A wall that stands just in front of the player and is nearly edge-on leaves a thin straight remnant in the circle (`b7_summer` 1:50).
*   Pictures are kept outside the repository (`.../tmp/rfc34/out`), the harness is not committed.

### 9.4 Defect found after deployment (2026-11-16): the ground in the hole was see-through
*   **Symptom.** The owner, replaying `ttt_minecraft_b7_summer` at 1:19 following SiJo on a phone: in front of and below the player the grass terrain was cut open in a curved edge and showed grey stone and light blue water under it, like a hole in the ground ("the see-through ground artifact is ugly"). The hole is the circle of 4.1 and the terrain is a hollow shell (faces only), so cutting it shows the underside of what lies below.
*   **Cause (confirmed by a debug picture, not assumed).** The circle cut every face nearer than the player, whatever it was: the part of the circle that lies BELOW their feet on the screen hits terrain that is lower than them, and a pixel ray that passes under the soles cannot hide the player, so cutting there only opens the shell. The debug colours (cut fragments by kind: floor-like, wall-like; above or below the camera-to-chest line) showed it was not the floors: at that moment the cut fragments were the **side faces of the grass blocks, wall-like and below the sight line** (yellow in `before_b7_sijo119_dbg.png`). The first candidate, "floor-like faces are cut only above the line of sight", was tried too (`pA.png`): it leaves this picture as it was, because the faces are not floor-like. The ground exception of 4.1 (a floor within 14 units above their feet) did not help either, these faces are not floors and are lower than that. The old cone of RFC 12 cut less there because it was a cone around the line of sight, not a disc.
*   **Fix, one rule for every kind of surface (`followHole.ts`: `lowFrom`, `lowTo`, `gFrom`, `gTo`).** The test is geometric: a surface hides the player only if its pixel ray reaches their body. At the player's depth the pixel ray `Q = P * (ctr.z / P.z)` passes `dot(up, Q - ctr)` above the chest (negative: below). Below `lowFrom` (-52, the feet are at -40) the surface is kept, above `lowTo` (-30) it is cut, with a smoothstep between (no step). The rule depends on the pixel only, so it is smooth on the screen and cannot make a wedge. The upper half of the circle is as before (a roof, a ceiling or a wall above the line is cut). **Ground ring:** a ceiling or roof that covers the floor around their feet (and the selection ring) is a *flat* face whose pixel ray lands on the ground plane within `gFrom` (40) to `gTo` (60) of the feet; for flat faces only, this stays cut (so under a roof the window shows the floor around the feet and the ring), with the same smooth ramps; block sides are not flat and are not exempt. Without this exception the window under a roof lost the ring and the floor in front of the feet (found on 67th Way 0:05, 0:28).
*   **Checked on real moments** (the harness of 9.2; before = the same build with the new rule's ramp switched off): `b7_summer` at 1:19 (SiJo, the screenshot), 0:00, 0:22, 0:29, 0:50 (SiJo), 0:55, 1:50, 2:30 (SiJo), 3:20 (SiJo, in the water), and 67th Way at 0:05, 0:12, 0:28, 0:36, 0:44, 0:57, 0:58, 1:08. At 1:19 the cut ground is gone: the grass, the dirt sides and the steps are whole, a few pixels of light blue remain beside the shins (a face right at the soles, inside the ramp). The roof and ceiling cases are still opened (0:05, 0:12, 0:28, 0:57, 0:58, 1:08 on 67th Way, 0:00, 0:22, 0:29, 0:55 on `b7_summer`), the player is seen in every picture, no wedge and no grain appeared. 0:44 on 67th Way (the pool edge) is better, the notch of light blue is gone. **Worse than before:** the window is no longer a full circle but a circle with its lower part flattened (a chord below the feet) where a wall or a roof that is wall-like (a slanted face) covers the ground in front of them; at 0:36 on 67th Way and 0:29 on `b7_summer` the selection ring is partly hidden behind the wall in front of their feet, and some less of the floor in front of them is shown under a roof (0:58, 1:08). The pictures are in `.../tmp/rfc34fix/out` (before and after pairs, sheets, the debug pictures), outside the repository.
*   **Tests:** `followhole.test.ts` has 34 tests (7 new, one of them the regression test of this bug), the whole suite 2,409, `tsc` and `bun run build` clean. Mutations of the new rule (flip above and below, remove the ramp, remove the below-the-feet branch, remove the ground ring, ring for every kind, ring as a hard step) each fail at least one test.
*   **Not verified:** the same moments on a real phone GPU; the ramps (-52 to -30, 40 to 60) were tuned on these moments only; a small wall-like sliver right at the soles may still show the shell behind it.
*   **Lesson.** The first checks of RFC 34 used moments where the ground in front of the player was flat or the window mostly showed walls. Test a hole rule on moments from the real maps where the ground in front of the player is **lower** and **higher** than their feet (steps, ledges, pits, pools), not only on flat floors, and look at which surface KINDS the rule cuts before judging the picture; the unit tests now carry a step lower than the feet (`the bug: terrain in front of and below the player ...`).
