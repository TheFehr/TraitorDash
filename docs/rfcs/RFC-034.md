# RFC 34: A Clean Hole for the Follow Camera (a Circle on the Screen, and a Ghost of the Player)

> **Status: proposed (2026-11-15), not built.** Asked for by the maintainer after a round replay on `ttt_minecraft_b7_summer` ("our peek-through on follow still has issues"): the hole the follow camera makes in the walls is a fan of noisy grain and large triangular wedges. Replaces the cone fade of RFC 12, sections 12 and 14 (the line-of-sight fade in `replay.ts`: `CUT_GLSL`, `seeThrough`, `cutAt`, the per-object `aObject`). Chosen from the research of 2026-11-15 and the prototype of section 3 (a three.js scene, headless Chromium; the pictures are in the maintainer's conversation, the code is section 4).

## 1. Abstract
The follow camera orbits behind a player. What lies between the camera and the player is cut away so the player is seen: every fragment in a world-space cone around the line from the camera to the head is discarded with a screen-door pattern (interleaved gradient noise), walls and floors per pixel and props per object (RFC 12, 12 and 14). It is noisy (a wide stipple band), and it makes **straight-edged wedges** through walls and floors.

This RFC replaces the cone with **one rule for walls, floors and props: a circle on the screen around the player's head, applied only to what is nearer than the player**, with a hard clean edge, and adds **a ghost of the player** drawn over whatever still covers them. No noise, no per-object values, no world-space cone.

## 2. Why it looks bad now (found 2026-11-15, reproduced in the prototype from a copy of the real shader)
*   **The wedges are not a property of large triangles.** The world position used by the cut is interpolated exactly per pixel. They come from the **hard `return 0.0` branches of `cutAt`**: a step from fully faded to fully solid on a world-space plane (the last 8 units before the head; a floor height of "feet minus 24" or "ground plus 16"; the overhead rule's `p.y < head + 16` and `along < 0`). A step on a plane cuts a straight line through a wall or a floor, and a wall that passes near the player is solid on one side of that line and open on the other (in the prototype, a solid triangle across the player's leg).
*   **Props and walls follow two rules.** A prop is faded as one block by a per-vertex value (no hole shape), and a prop above `BIG_PROP` (60 units) switches to the per-pixel rule, so the same kind of wall looks different from one object to the next.
*   **The grain.** The fade band is up to 45 world units wide near the camera, which on screen is a wide stipple of a pattern that is made to be accumulated over frames (it is not, here). The renderer caps the pixel ratio at 1.5 on a touch screen (`sceneKit.ts`) while a phone's pixel ratio is 3, so every pattern pixel is drawn as a 2 x 2 block.

## 3. What was compared (prototype, 2026-11-15)
Same scene and camera (huge floor triangles, long wall quads, an upper slab, merged props, a capsule player; the camera 300 units back and 170 up, field of view 40, as in `replay.ts`), three.js 0.186, Chromium with a software renderer (4x MSAA available), at pixel ratios 1, 1.5 and 3. Treatments: A the current cone; B a circle with the same noise; C a circle with a 4x4 Bayer pattern (cell = the pixel ratio) and a narrow band; **D a circle with a hard edge and no noise, plus the ghost**; E a circle with alpha-to-coverage. Result: A makes the wedges; B is round but grainy; C is tidier but still a stipple; **D is the cleanest: a crisp circular window, no grain, no wedges**, with a small straight remnant of a wall beside the player where the wall really is; E gives four concentric rings (4x MSAA has five coverage levels) and a pale glow whose cause was not found. D is the choice. Not proven: the look on a real phone GPU (all pictures are from a software renderer).

Other findings that shape the choice (read in full, 2026-11-15): Valve's CS2 demo viewer does not cut walls at all, it draws player outlines through them (`spec_show_xray`); the Bombernauts "crop circle" article uses a screen circle and a per-face test (below); a depth-fail second draw of the character is the standard silhouette technique and the three.js forum has the recipe. No primary source describes the method of a shipped commercial game; that is not claimed.

## 4. Design
### 4.1 The rule (one function for walls, floors and props)
In the fragment stage, from the view-space position `P = -vViewPosition` and uniforms only (no per-vertex value, no `aObject`, no world-space cone):

```glsl
uniform vec3 uCtr;      // the player's centre (the chest), view space: ctr.applyMatrix4(camera.matrixWorldInverse)
uniform vec4 uGround;   // the plane they stand on, view space: (n, -n.dot(feetPoint))
uniform float uRad;     // the hole's radius in world units at the player's distance
float holeAt(vec3 P) {
  float dz = (-uCtr.z) - (-P.z);              // > 0: nearer to the camera than the player
  float near = smoothstep(-56.0, -16.0, dz);  // the body's depth is cut; the ramp lies BEHIND the body, so no straight edge
  if (near <= 0.0) return 0.0;
  float h = dot(uGround.xyz, P) + uGround.w;  // height above the floor they stand on
  vec3 nf = normalize(cross(dFdx(P), dFdy(P)));
  if (abs(dot(nf, uGround.xyz)) > 0.6 && h < 14.0) return 0.0; // the floor under their feet stays
  vec2 d = P.xy / -P.z - uCtr.xy / -uCtr.z;   // a true circle on the screen
  float R = uRad / -uCtr.z;                   // the radius shrinks with distance
  return near * (1.0 - smoothstep(R * (1.0 - uBand), R, length(d)));
}
```
After `#include <alphatest_fragment>`: `if (holeAt(-vViewPosition) > 0.5) discard;`. The edge is **hard** (`uBand` small, no noise): the clean circle of treatment D. (A soft band with a Bayer pattern, treatment C, stays available as a setting of the constant `uBand` and a pattern line, if the hard edge looks harsh on a real phone; it is not the default.) The `dFdx/dFdy` face normal needs WebGL2 (three.js 0.186 is WebGL2 only).

### 4.2 The ghost
The followed player is drawn a second time: the same geometry, a flat colour (the player's team colour or white) at about 50% opacity, `depthFunc = GreaterDepth`, `depthWrite = false`, `renderOrder` after the opaque scene, so it shows **only where something still covers the player** (a small remnant of the cut, an object the circle does not reach). It guarantees the player is never lost. One extra draw of one model.

### 4.3 Walls beside the player (a second step, built after the first is checked)
Only a wall that really separates the camera from the player should be cut. The Bombernauts test: a face is cut only when the camera and the player lie on opposite sides of its plane (`dot(n, cam - point)` and `dot(n, ctr - point)` of opposite signs; the face normal from the derivatives as above). A wall beside the player and not between is then kept whole. Added to `holeAt` as a multiplier, behind a constant, and judged on the same recorded moments.

### 4.4 What is removed
`CUT_GLSL`/`cutAt`, the `seeThrough` kinds `prop`/`wall`/`floor`, the per-vertex `aObject` attribute on merged props (if nothing else uses it; check `mergeDrawn` and the moved-props logic), `BIG_PROP`, the `lineOfSight` uniforms and their per-frame update, and the overhead fade rules of `replaylogic.ts` (`overheadFade`, `FADE`) whose job the circle takes over. What stays: the follow camera itself (position, damping, the level logic of RFC 12, 14), the level bar, Walls, and the camera cut for the orbit and top cameras.

### 4.5 Per frame
The player's chest position (the head minus a fixed drop) and feet point go to view space; `uGround` is built from the world up axis (Source z, three.js y) and the feet point; `uRad` follows the player's size (a constant in world units, about 36 at first; tuned on the recorded moments). Nothing else changes per frame.

## 5. Not changed, and not planned
*   The camera position and damping, the level logic, and the other cameras.
*   Alpha-to-coverage (E): rings and an unexplained glow; reconsidered only if a real phone shows a reason.
*   Camera pull-in on collision: the research and RFC 12 both show it jitters and zooms into the player in multi-storey maps.
*   Hiding a floor or a room by data (storey ids, BSP visibility): a possible later help for ceilings, not needed for the hole.

## 6. Tests (what must fail on a broken build)
*   The shader source: one rule for all three material kinds (`seeThrough` is gone; a single function patched into the standard materials of walls, floors and merged props); no `aObject`, no `lineOfSight`, no `cutAt` left; the uniforms the rule needs exist and are updated each frame from the player's pose (a test on the page code with a stand-in scene, as the replay's other tests do).
*   The uniform math in plain code (extracted into `replaylogic.ts` or a small module so it is testable): the player's centre and the ground plane in view space for a given camera and player, the radius shrinking with distance, and a unit test of the same `holeAt` written in JavaScript (the shader is a transcription of it): a point nearer than the player and inside the circle is cut, a point outside the circle, behind the player, or on the floor under the feet is not; **no discontinuity**: the value changes by at most a small step between points one pixel apart (the cause of the wedges).
*   The ghost: the player's mesh is drawn twice, the second with `GreaterDepth` and no depth write, and it follows the followed player only.
*   The replay's other behaviour is unchanged (level logic, cameras, the lying-place list); the existing replay tests pass (those that tested the cone's constants are replaced by the above).
*   Layout, contrast: nothing in the markup changes.

## 7. Check on real moments (required before it is called done)
Render the replay's own scene code with the stored mesh of `ttt_67thway_rework_b1` and the recorded round of 2026-11-06 (`track-samples`), and with `ttt_minecraft_b7_summer`, in Chromium at a phone size (the same camera as the follow camera would have), before and after, at: the moments RFC 12 names for 67th Way (0:57, 0:58: the sunken passage, the crates, the second player) and three or four others where the old cut was wrong (a wall close to the player, a ceiling above, a ledge at the feet); judge: the player is seen, no wedges, no grain, the hole reads as a clean window, the floor under the feet stays. The pictures and the judgement go into this RFC's as-built section.

## 8. What cannot be verified here
*   The look on a real phone GPU and the choice of the hard edge over the soft one: the prototype ran on a software renderer. The constants (radius, depth ramp, floor height) are tuned on recorded moments, not on a device.
*   Moments of the owner's `b7_summer` round (the screenshot): only the 67th Way round is in the samples; the replay of `b7_summer` is checked with a synthetic follow path through its map.
