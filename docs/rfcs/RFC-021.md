# RFC 21: Weapon Statistics and Killzones

> **Status: built on the backend and in the bridge (2026-11-04); not yet run on a real server.** Follows RFC 4 (the round report), RFC 14 (what the report holds), RFC 13 (the pages) and RFC 10 (the floor plan). Adds data to the round report, two views of the statistics page and one picture on a map's page, all drawn on the server so they work in the in-game browser.

## 1. Abstract
The statistics say who won, who killed whom and when. They do not say with what, how much damage a weapon did, or where on a map people die. This RFC adds: **damage and kills by weapon** (for the server, and for each player), and **killzones**: where on each map the kills happen, as a picture. Everything is drawn into the page as HTML tables and inline SVG by the backend, with no script, because the in-game browser runs old, limited web code (RFC 16).

## 2. Motivation
*   "Which weapon does the most damage on this server?" and "what does this player kill with?" are the first things a group argues about.
*   A map's hot spots (the corridor everyone dies in, the spawn that is always camped) show a map's balance. A round's kills are already in the replay one at a time; nothing adds them up.
*   The data is not there to be summed: a death in the round report has a victim, a killer and a second, nothing more. A track has the weapon and the position of each death, but only for the rounds whose track is still kept (older ones are removed to make room), and it is one JSON document for each round, not something to query. Damage is in neither.

## 3. What is there
*   `deaths` (migration v7): victim, attacker, `at_seconds`. The report is `POST /api/v1/round`; `deaths` is validated against the round's players. The bridge's `Death` hook (`stats.lua`) writes them.
*   `track.lua` already finds the weapon of a death (`Weapon(inflictor, attacker)`) and the positions; they stay in the track.
*   The statistics page has the tabs Overview, Leaderboard and Rounds; a player has a page; a map has a public rounds page and an admin page with the floor plan (RFC 10, admins only, drawn by `renderPlanSvg`).

## 4. Design

### 4.1 What the bridge sends (all optional; an old bridge sends none)
*   **Each death** gets `weapon` (the class name of the weapon or other thing that did it: `weapon_ttt_m16`, `prop_physics`, `worldspawn`; letters, digits, `_ . -`, at most 64) and `x`, `y`, `z`: where the victim died, in whole world units.
*   **`damage`**: one entry for each attacker, weapon and kind (`friendly` or not), summed over the round: `{ attacker, weapon, damage, hits, friendly }`. Only damage that a player did to another player counts; the world, props and self-damage are left out. `damage` is the points of damage the game reported, each hit capped at the victim's maximum health (a shotgun at point-blank range does not count 8 pellets of overkill), as a whole number. `hits` is how many hits (a pellet is a hit). `friendly` is true when victim and attacker were on one team and are not the same player. At most 1,024 entries; a round has a few dozen.
*   The bridge hooks `PostEntityTakeDamage`, which runs after a hit has been applied (and says whether it was). The handler is wrapped like the others, adds to a table keyed by the three values and returns nothing. It does no work for a hit on anything but a player in the round. The cost is a table lookup and two additions for each hit; it is measured by the parts probe's method (section 7).
*   The bridge never sends the positions of the **attacker**; killzones are where people die.

### 4.2 Storage (migration v25)
*   `deaths` gets nullable `weapon TEXT`, `x INTEGER`, `y INTEGER`, `z INTEGER`. A death with no position is not drawn; a death with no weapon is "unknown weapon" in the weapon tables. Old rounds keep `NULL`, and the pages say how many rounds the numbers rest on.
*   New table `round_damage (server_id, round_id, steam_id, weapon, friendly, damage, hits)`, primary key `(server_id, round_id, steam_id, weapon, friendly)`, removed with its round and server like `deaths` and `round_revives`.
*   **The tracks that exist are used.** A track already holds the weapon and the place of each death (RFC 12; places in steps of 8 units). Migration v26 gives them to the death rows of every round that still has a track, matching a track's death to the death row of the same victim nearest in time (within 3 seconds, one row for each). A track that does not read is skipped. So the weapon kills and the killzones have history from the first day, for the rounds that kept a track; the damage columns start when the bridge is updated. Rounds without a track, and rounds whose track was removed, stay unknown.
*   Validation as for deaths: an attacker who is not a player of the round, and a weapon or a number that does not fit, are dropped or stored as unknown, not refused; more than 1,024 entries, or a body that is not an array, is a `400` that stores nothing. A server's 50,000-round cap holds the table in check.

### 4.3 The weapon tables
*   **The statistics page gets a tab "Weapons"** (`view=weapons`): one row for each weapon: damage (not friendly), share of all damage, hits, average damage for a hit, kills, how many people died to it, how many players used it and in how many rounds. Sorted by damage by default; `sort=kills|hits|damage` is a link in the heading, so it works with no script. Friendly damage is in its own column and never counts toward the ranking. Weapon names are made readable (`weapon_ttt_m16` is "M16", `weapon_zm_shotgun` is "Shotgun"); the class name stays as a tooltip.
*   **A player's page gets "Weapons"**: the same columns for that player (damage done, hits, kills with it, and how often they died to it).
*   Counting rules: a **kill** is a death with a killer who is not the victim; a death by the world is "deaths by" only. Bots follow the same switch as everywhere else (RFC 14). Rounds before this RFC are not in the damage columns; the tab says "from N rounds" next to the total of rounds.

### 4.4 Killzones
*   **What it is.** All deaths of one map with a position, summed into squares of 128 units (the position divided by 128, rounded down), shown as a picture from above: the more deaths in a square, the brighter and warmer it is. North is up, like the floor plan. The picture is an SVG made by the backend and placed in the page (not an image to load and not drawn by script), a rectangle for each square with a count in its tooltip, so the in-game browser shows it (an SVG with circles, rectangles and gradients).
*   **Where it is.** The map's public page (`/servers/<id>/maps/<map>/rounds`, which the in-game window also shows) gets a "Killzones" section: the picture, the number of deaths and rounds it rests on, the five squares with the most deaths as a list (with their coordinates, for the server's console and for the map's author), and links to filter by weapon. Filters are links with a query string (`?weapon=weapon_ttt_m16`), not controls that need script.
*   **The map underneath.** The picture is drawn on the map itself, flattened. The stored mesh (RFC 11, 18) has a node for each storey with its floors and walls; the backend reads it once and turns each storey into a grid of cells (16 units or more, at most about 360 across), each a floor, a wall or nothing, and keeps the result for the few maps in use. The grid is drawn as runs of rectangles: floors dark, walls lighter, so it is an outline of the map as the 3D view shows it, with no height. Who sees it follows RFC 13 and RFC 16: **a signed-in visitor sees it, and so does a player in the game, whom the game server vouches for with a user session (never an admin)**; opening it is logged like a view of the 3D map (once an hour for each person and map). An **anonymous** visitor on the web sees the deaths on a blank background. An admin whose map has no mesh but has a floor plan (RFC 10, admin-only) sees the plan's floor instead, in the same way. The choice of storey (a link to `?level=n`, the mesh's own storeys, a death belongs to the highest storey whose floors start at or below it) is there when a mesh or a plan is drawn. With the 3D views switched off (`MAP_GEOMETRY`) no mesh is drawn for anybody.
*   **The deaths.** Each square of 128 units is a soft blob (a radial gradient from yellow to red, larger where more died), the busiest on top, so a corridor reads as a stripe and a spawn as a spot; its tooltip gives the count. Five colour steps, one gradient for each.
*   **Limits.** At most 10,000 squares drawn; a map with more is drawn with the 10,000 busiest. Only deaths since this RFC are in it, and the section says so.
*   **Privacy.** Positions are where a player died, with no name and no time. The picture and its list name nobody.

### 4.5 What it does not do
*   No accuracy (a shot that missed is not a hit the game tells us about), no damage taken, no per-round weapon timeline. Damage taken, kill lines (killer to victim) and a heat map of where killers stood are later options (section 8).
*   No change to the replay, the track or any existing statistic.

## 5. Security considerations
*   The new body fields are numbers and names checked as the existing ones are; nothing is stored that is not a whole number or a class-name string. The SVG is built from numbers; the only text in it is a count and a map name, both escaped. Filter values (`weapon`, `level`) are matched against the stored values and a pattern before they are used in a query or in the page.
*   The queries are limited (50 weapons, 10,000 squares) and use the indexes on the round and the player; the pages are answered as the other statistics pages are, for a read-only visitor, like them.

## 6. Compatibility and rollout
*   Old bridges send no `weapon`, positions or `damage` and are accepted as before; the new tab and section say there is nothing yet. A new bridge on an old backend sends fields the backend ignores (as with every earlier addition).
*   Order: (1) migration and validation, with tests; (2) the queries and the tab; (3) the player's weapons; (4) the killzone picture and the section; (5) the bridge; (6) the backfill from tracks (v26). Damage starts when the bridge is updated; weapons and places also come from tracks that exist.

## 7. Testing
*   **Backend, report:** a round with `weapon`, positions and `damage` is stored and read back; a round without them is accepted and stores `NULL`; an attacker who is not in the round, a weapon with other characters, a negative or fractional number, more than 1,024 entries and a non-array `damage` are each handled as section 4.2 says; a repeat of a round changes nothing; the migration adds the columns and table to an existing database and a second run changes nothing; deleting a round or a server deletes its damage; **the backfill** gives a track's weapon and place (times 8) to the death row of the same victim nearest in time, matches each row once, leaves a death more than 3 s away and a round whose track does not read alone, and a second run changes nothing.
*   **Weapon tables:** damage, share, hits, average, kills and users of a fixed set of rounds are the numbers worked out by hand; friendly damage is separate and not in the ranking; the world kills are "deaths by" only; bots follow the switch; the sort links order the table; the names are made readable; a round without data is counted as one the numbers do not rest on.
*   **Killzones:** deaths sum into the right squares; a death on a square's edge goes where the rule puts it; a death without a position is not drawn; the busiest-first limit; the weapon filter; the flattened mesh (a floor and a wall for each storey, the storeys, a file that is not a mesh); **who sees what: an anonymous visitor gets no map, a signed-in one gets the map and the storeys, also in the window, with the view logged; an admin with a plan and no mesh gets the plan, and a plain user does not; with the 3D views off nobody gets the map**; checked once on a real map's mesh and looked at; the SVG is well formed, escapes the map name and has no script, no external reference and no `<image>`; the page has no script tag for these sections (the in-game browser).
*   **Bridge (Fengari):** a hit on a player in the round adds to the entry for attacker, weapon and kind; overkill is capped; a hit on a prop, the world, a player not in the round and self-damage add nothing; a blocked hit (the hook says it was not taken) adds nothing; friendly is set for one team; more than 1,024 entries stop adding; the hook returns nothing; an error in the handler does not reach the game; the death carries weapon and position; with the stats switched off nothing is hooked.
*   **Cost:** the handler is timed with the parts probe's method on a real server before this RFC is called done; the budget is a few microseconds for a hit.
*   **Looked at:** the pages rendered in headless Chromium, on the website and as the window; and in the game, once.

## 8. Alternatives considered
*   **Take the weapon and the positions from the track, only.** Only the kept tracks have them, one JSON for each round, and the whole track would be read for one picture. Rejected as the source; the tracks that exist are read once, to fill the death rows (4.2).
*   **Draw the picture in the browser (canvas, WebGL).** The in-game browser is the reason for this RFC; script and canvas are not reliable there. Rejected for the main picture; the replay stays the place for the 3D view.
*   **An `<img>` of an SVG.** Works in the in-game browser, but a second request, a second cache and a second access check. Inline is one response.
*   **Record every hit.** Thousands of rows for each round. The sum over attacker, weapon and kind answers the questions asked.
*   **Record the damage to each victim.** A matrix for each round for a question nobody asked; it can be added to the same table as a column later.
*   **Draw the floor plan (RFC 10) under the deaths for everyone.** RFC 10 decided plans are for admins. The first version of this RFC drew only the plan, and only for admins, and the picture was a blank grid of squares for everybody else. The mesh is better data and the game's players are signed-in users anyway (RFC 16), so the plan is now only the fallback for an admin.
*   **Draw the mesh in 3D for the window.** Needs WebGL and script, which the in-game browser may not run; the replay is the place for it.
*   **Weapon names from a table of the game's weapons.** The bridge sends class names; a table of names can be added to the backend without a protocol change. Until then, the rule of 4.3 makes a good name from most.

## 9. Open questions
*   What counts as a weapon for the tables: the class that did the damage (a thrown grenade, a C4, a barrel) or only the held weapons? This RFC takes the class that did it, so a barrel shows as a barrel.
*   Is 128 units the right square? About two player widths; the first real data will show whether a coarser or finer one reads better.
*   Should the damage a role's ability does (a Necromancer's zombies, a Jackal's sidekick) be put under the role's weapon? Not attempted here.
