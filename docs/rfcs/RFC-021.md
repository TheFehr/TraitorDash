# RFC 21: Weapon Statistics and Killzones

> **Status: built on the backend and in the bridge (2026-11-04); not yet run on a real server.** Follows RFC 4 (the round report), RFC 14 (what the report holds), RFC 13 (the pages) and RFC 10 (the floor plan). Adds data to the round report, two views of the statistics page and one picture on a map's page, all drawn on the server so they work in the in-game browser.

## 1. Abstract
The statistics say who won, who killed whom and when. They do not say with what, how much damage a weapon did, or where on a map people die. This RFC adds: **damage and kills by weapon** (for the server, and for each player), and **killzones**: where on each map the kills happen, as a picture. Everything is drawn into the page as HTML tables and inline SVG by the backend, with no script, because the in-game browser runs old, limited web code (RFC 16).

## 2. Motivation
*   "Which weapon does the most damage on this server?" and "what does this player kill with?" are the first things a group argues about.
*   A map's hot spots (the corridor everyone dies in, the spawn that is always camped) show a map's balance. A round's kills are already in the replay one at a time; nothing adds them up.
*   The data is not there to be summed: a death in the round report has a victim, a killer and a second, nothing more. A track has the weapon and the position of each death, but only for the rounds whose track is still kept (older ones are removed to make room), and it is one JSON document for each round, not something to query. Damage is in neither.

## 3. What is there
*   `deaths` (migration v7): victim, attacker, `at_seconds`. The report is `POST /api/v1/round`; `deaths` is validated against the round's players. The bridge's `Death` hook (`stats.lua`) writes them.
*   `track.lua` already finds the weapon of a death (`Weapon(inflictor, attacker)`) and the positions; they stay in the track.
*   The statistics page has the tabs Overview, Leaderboard and Rounds; a player has a page; a map has a public rounds page and an admin page with the floor plan (RFC 10, admins only, drawn by `renderPlanSvg`).

## 4. Design

### 4.1 What the bridge sends (all optional; an old bridge sends none)
*   **Each death** gets `weapon` (the class name of the weapon or other thing that did it: `weapon_ttt_m16`, `prop_physics`, `worldspawn`; letters, digits, `_ . -`, at most 64) and `x`, `y`, `z`: where the victim died, in whole world units.
*   **`damage`**: one entry for each attacker, weapon and kind (`friendly` or not), summed over the round: `{ attacker, weapon, damage, hits, friendly }`. Only damage that a player did to another player counts; the world, props and self-damage are left out. `damage` is the points of damage the game reported, each hit capped at the victim's maximum health (a shotgun at point-blank range does not count 8 pellets of overkill), as a whole number. `hits` is how many hits (a pellet is a hit). `friendly` is true when victim and attacker were on one team and are not the same player. At most 1,024 entries; a round has a few dozen.
*   The bridge hooks `PostEntityTakeDamage`, which runs after a hit has been applied (and says whether it was). The handler is wrapped like the others, adds to a table keyed by the three values and returns nothing. It does no work for a hit on anything but a player in the round. The cost is a table lookup and two additions for each hit; it is measured by the parts probe's method (section 7).
*   The bridge never sends the positions of the **attacker**; killzones are where people die.

### 4.2 Storage (migration v25)
*   `deaths` gets nullable `weapon TEXT`, `x INTEGER`, `y INTEGER`, `z INTEGER`. A death with no position is not drawn; a death with no weapon is "unknown weapon" in the weapon tables. Old rounds keep `NULL`, and the pages say how many rounds the numbers rest on.
*   New table `round_damage (server_id, round_id, steam_id, weapon, friendly, damage, hits)`, primary key `(server_id, round_id, steam_id, weapon, friendly)`, removed with its round and server like `deaths` and `round_revives`.
*   **The tracks that exist are used.** A track already holds the weapon and the place of each death (RFC 12; places in steps of 8 units). Migration v26 gives them to the death rows of every round that still has a track, matching a track's death to the death row of the same victim nearest in time (within 3 seconds, one row for each). A track that does not read is skipped. So the weapon kills and the killzones have history from the first day, for the rounds that kept a track; the damage columns start when the bridge is updated. Rounds without a track, and rounds whose track was removed, stay unknown.
*   Validation as for deaths: an attacker who is not a player of the round, and a weapon or a number that does not fit, are dropped or stored as unknown, not refused; more than 1,024 entries, or a body that is not an array, is a `400` that stores nothing. A server's 50,000-round cap holds the table in check.

### 4.3 The weapon tables
*   **The statistics page gets a tab "Weapons"** (`view=weapons`): one row for each weapon: damage (not friendly), share of all damage, hits, average damage for a hit, kills, how many people died to it, how many players used it and in how many rounds. Sorted by damage by default; `sort=kills|hits|damage` is a link in the heading, so it works with no script. Friendly damage is in its own column and never counts toward the ranking. Weapon names are made readable (`weapon_ttt_m16` is "M16", `weapon_zm_shotgun` is "Shotgun"); the class name stays as a tooltip.
*   **A player's page gets "Weapons"**: the same columns for that player (damage done, hits, kills with it, and how often they died to it).
*   Counting rules: a **kill** is a death with a killer who is not the victim; a death by the world is "deaths by" only. Bots follow the same switch as everywhere else (RFC 14). Rounds before this RFC are not in the damage columns; the tab says "from N rounds" next to the total of rounds.

### 4.4 Killzones
*   **What it is.** All deaths of one map with a position, summed into squares (first 128 units; the squares now follow the data, section 11), shown as a picture from above: the more deaths in a square, the brighter and warmer it is. North is up, like the floor plan. The picture is an SVG made by the backend and placed in the page (not an image to load and not drawn by script), a rectangle for each square with a count in its tooltip, so the in-game browser shows it (an SVG with circles, rectangles and gradients).
*   **Where it is.** The map's public page (`/servers/<id>/maps/<map>/rounds`, which the in-game window also shows) gets a "Killzones" section: the picture, the number of deaths and rounds it rests on, the five squares with the most deaths as a list (with their coordinates, for the server's console and for the map's author), and links to filter by weapon. Filters are links with a query string (`?weapon=weapon_ttt_m16`), not controls that need script.
*   **The map underneath.** The picture is drawn on the map itself, flattened. The stored mesh (RFC 11, 18) has a node for each storey with its floors and walls; the backend reads it once and turns each storey into a grid of cells (16 units or more, at most about 360 across), each a floor, a wall or nothing, and keeps the result for the few maps in use. The grid is drawn as runs of rectangles: floors dark, walls lighter, so it is an outline of the map as the 3D view shows it, with no height. Who sees it follows RFC 13 and RFC 16: **a signed-in visitor sees it, and so does a player in the game, whom the game server vouches for with a user session (never an admin)**; opening it is logged like a view of the 3D map (once an hour for each person and map). An **anonymous** visitor on the web sees the deaths on a blank background. An admin whose map has no mesh but has a floor plan (RFC 10, admin-only) sees the plan's floor instead, in the same way. The choice of storey (a link to `?level=n`, the mesh's own storeys, a death belongs to the storey that has a floor under it closest to its height: each storey keeps the height of its highest floor in every cell, and the square the death is in is looked at; only where no storey has a floor there does the highest storey whose floors start at or below the height decide. The first rule, by that start height alone, was wrong on Cape Town, where storeys overlap in height (storey 3 starts at -60 and holds low ground too) and it put all 31 deaths on storey 3; by the floor under them, 18, 11 and 2 deaths fall on storeys 1, 2 and 3, and all 31 stand on a floor cell of the map, which also confirms the orientation and the scale of the stored positions) is there when a mesh or a plan is drawn. With the 3D views switched off (`MAP_GEOMETRY`) no mesh is drawn for anybody.
*   **The deaths.** Each square (section 11) is a soft blob (a radial gradient from yellow to red, larger where more died), the busiest on top, so a corridor reads as a stripe and a spawn as a spot; its tooltip gives the count. Five colour steps, one gradient for each.
*   **Limits.** At most 10,000 squares drawn; a map with more is drawn with the 10,000 busiest. Only deaths since this RFC are in it, and the section says so.
*   **Privacy.** Positions are where a player died, with no name and no time. The picture and its list name nobody.

### 4.5 What it does not do
*   No accuracy (a shot that missed is not a hit the game tells us about), no damage taken, no per-round weapon timeline. Damage taken, kill lines (killer to victim) and a heat map of where killers stood are later options (section 8).
*   No change to the replay, the track or any existing statistic.

## 5. Security considerations
*   The new body fields are numbers and names checked as the existing ones are; nothing is stored that is not a whole number or a class-name string. The SVG is built from numbers; the only text in it is a count and a map name, both escaped. Filter values (`weapon`, `level`) are matched against the stored values and a pattern before they are used in a query or in the page.
*   The queries are limited (50 weapons, 10,000 squares) and use the indexes on the round and the player; the pages are answered as the other statistics pages are, for a read-only visitor, like them.

## 6. Compatibility and rollout
*   Old bridges send no `weapon`, positions or `damage` and are accepted as before; the new tab and section say there is nothing yet. A new bridge on an old backend sends fields the backend ignores (as with every earlier addition).
*   Order: (1) migration and validation, with tests; (2) the queries and the tab; (3) the player's weapons; (4) the killzone picture and the section; (5) the bridge; (6) the backfill from tracks (v26). Damage starts when the bridge is updated; weapons and places also come from tracks that exist.

## 7. Testing
*   **Backend, report:** a round with `weapon`, positions and `damage` is stored and read back; a round without them is accepted and stores `NULL`; an attacker who is not in the round, a weapon with other characters, a negative or fractional number, more than 1,024 entries and a non-array `damage` are each handled as section 4.2 says; a repeat of a round changes nothing; the migration adds the columns and table to an existing database and a second run changes nothing; deleting a round or a server deletes its damage; **the backfill** gives a track's weapon and place (times 8) to the death row of the same victim nearest in time, matches each row once, leaves a death more than 3 s away and a round whose track does not read alone, and a second run changes nothing.
*   **Weapon tables:** damage, share, hits, average, kills and users of a fixed set of rounds are the numbers worked out by hand; friendly damage is separate and not in the ranking; the world kills are "deaths by" only; bots follow the switch; the sort links order the table; the names are made readable; a round without data is counted as one the numbers do not rest on.
*   **Killzones:** deaths sum into the right squares; a death on a square's edge goes where the rule puts it; a death without a position is not drawn; the busiest-first limit; the weapon filter; the flattened mesh (a floor and a wall for each storey, the storeys, a file that is not a mesh); **who sees what: an anonymous visitor gets no map, a signed-in one gets the map and the storeys, also in the window, with the view logged; an admin with a plan and no mesh gets the plan, and a plain user does not; with the 3D views off nobody gets the map**; checked once on a real map's mesh and looked at; the SVG is well formed, escapes the map name and has no script, no external reference and no `<image>`; the page has no script tag for these sections (the in-game browser).
*   **Bridge (Fengari):** a hit on a player in the round adds to the entry for attacker, weapon and kind; overkill is capped; a hit on a prop, the world, a player not in the round and self-damage add nothing; a blocked hit (the hook says it was not taken) adds nothing; friendly is set for one team; more than 1,024 entries stop adding; the hook returns nothing; an error in the handler does not reach the game; the death carries weapon and position; with the stats switched off nothing is hooked.
*   **Cost:** the handler is timed with the parts probe's method on a real server before this RFC is called done; the budget is a few microseconds for a hit.
*   **Looked at:** the pages rendered in headless Chromium, on the website and as the window; and in the game, once.

## 8. Alternatives considered
*   **Take the weapon and the positions from the track, only.** Only the kept tracks have them, one JSON for each round, and the whole track would be read for one picture. Rejected as the source; the tracks that exist are read once, to fill the death rows (4.2).
*   **Draw the picture in the browser (canvas, WebGL).** The in-game browser is the reason for this RFC; script and canvas are not reliable there. Rejected for the main picture; the replay stays the place for the 3D view.
*   **An `<img>` of an SVG.** Works in the in-game browser, but a second request, a second cache and a second access check. Inline is one response.
*   **Record every hit.** Thousands of rows for each round. The sum over attacker, weapon and kind answers the questions asked.
*   **Record the damage to each victim.** A matrix for each round for a question nobody asked; it can be added to the same table as a column later.
*   **Draw the floor plan (RFC 10) under the deaths for everyone.** RFC 10 decided plans are for admins. The first version of this RFC drew only the plan, and only for admins, and the picture was a blank grid of squares for everybody else. The mesh is better data and the game's players are signed-in users anyway (RFC 16), so the plan is now only the fallback for an admin.
*   **Draw the mesh in 3D for the window.** Needs WebGL and script, which the in-game browser may not run; the replay is the place for it.
*   **Weapon names from a table of the game's weapons.** The bridge sends class names; a table of names can be added to the backend without a protocol change. Until then, the rule of 4.3 makes a good name from most.

## 9. Open questions
*   What counts as a weapon for the tables: the class that did the damage (a thrown grenade, a C4, a barrel) or only the held weapons? This RFC takes the class that did it, so a barrel shows as a barrel.
*   ~~Is 128 units the right square?~~ No single size is right: see section 11 (squares that follow the data).
*   Should the damage a role's ability does (a Necromancer's zombies, a Jackal's sidekick) be put under the role's weapon? Not attempted here.

## 10. The height of a death (2026-11-04)
The position of a death was taken in `TTT2PostPlayerDeath`, after the victim had become a spectator, whose position is at the height of their eyes. On the live server 278 of 322 deaths were 64 units above the victim's feet (standing eyes) and 14 were 24 to 32 above (ducking eyes). The bridge now takes the position in `DoPlayerDeath` (RFC 22, 4b). Rounds reported before that still carry the high values: for the storey of a death the rule of section 4.2 (the layer with a floor closest in height) is not much affected, since the next storey is usually more than 64 units away, but the 32-unit bands of a death's height are two bands too high. The old values are not corrected in the database.

## 11. Squares that follow the data (2026-11-05)
**Why.** The first real data (all there was on 2026-11-05: Monday and Tuesday, 66 rounds of about 7 players, 352 deaths of which 322 have a position, on 11 maps) showed that a fixed square of 128 units suits no map. A map has 30 to 55 deaths, and at 128 units about nine in ten occupied squares hold a single death (Yucatan: 24 of 27), so the picture is a scatter and no hot spot shows. The maps differ a hundredfold in size: the walkable area seen from above is about 500 m² for the plane and the train and 30,000 m² for the outdoor maps (median 5,900 m², measured from the 27 stored meshes; the one real reachability scan, Diescraper, agrees with its mesh within 8%). A square of 128 units is 6 m². The same square is a room on one map and a speck on another, and it is far too fine for the few deaths there are now and probably too coarse for the many there may be later. **The size has to follow the data: start rough, and the more deaths there are, the finer the grouping.**

**4.4 is replaced by this for the size of the squares** (everything else in 4.4 stays: the picture, the page, who sees the map underneath, the filters, the privacy).

### 11.1 A quadtree of squares
*   The deaths of a map (with the weapon and storey the page asked for) are put into one set of squares that do not overlap and cover every death exactly once. The squares are **aligned powers of two**: the finest is 32 units (a player's width), then 64, 128, 256, 512 and 1,024, each four times as large as the one before, so a large square is exactly four of the next size.
*   The map starts in **root squares** (the coarsest size, 11.2). A square that holds **more than 3 deaths** is split into its four halves, and each of those is split again by the same rule, down to 32 units; a square with 3 deaths or fewer is left as it is. So a corridor where many died is drawn in fine squares, and a corner where one player died is one large soft blob, on the same picture.
*   Because the rule is the number of deaths and not a size chosen in advance, nothing has to be set by hand and the picture gets finer by itself as rounds are played: today's few deaths give a rough picture, a few hundred give a corridor-sized one.
*   The count in a square never changes with the size: the deaths of the four children add up to the parent's. A test checks that the squares always hold exactly the deaths.

### 11.2 The coarsest size follows the map's area
The root size is chosen so that a map starts as about 64 squares (eight by eight) whatever its size: the walkable area seen from above (the count of cells with a floor in any storey of the flattened mesh, RFC 18; else the floor cells of the floor plan; else 512 units) divided by 64, the side of that square rounded to the nearest power of two, and kept between 128 and 1,024 units. On the stored maps that is 128 units for the plane and the train (500 m²), 512 for the median map and 1,024 for the large outdoor ones. The area is only used for the size and reveals nothing; it is taken from the mesh for every visitor, also those who are not shown the mesh underneath.

### 11.3 What is drawn
*   One soft blob for each square (as in 4.4), as large as the square, so a coarse square is a wide soft spot and a fine one a small sharp one. The colour follows the **density**, the deaths in the square divided by its area, on a logarithmic scale from the thinnest to the densest square on the picture; a large square with 2 deaths is cooler than a small one with 3. The tooltip says "n deaths in a square of S units".
*   The legend says what the colours and sizes mean: "squares of 128 to 512 units, finer where more players died", and the colour bar is labelled "fewer" and "more" for the deaths in an area (not a count of deaths, which no longer means the same thing from square to square).
*   The list beside the picture is the five **densest** squares, with the size of each ("3 deaths in a square of 64 units around x 100, y 200").
*   At most 10,000 squares are drawn (the densest), as before.

### 11.4 Storage
Nothing changes in the database: the deaths keep their exact positions (RFC 21 4.2), and the squares are made when the page is asked for, from deaths counted in cells of 32 units and 32-unit height bands (the finest square), in one query. The page is public and cheap: the number of rows is at most the number of deaths of the map. The storey and weapon filters work on those cells as before, so each filter gets its own squares.

### 11.5 What it does not do
*   It does not smooth between squares or draw a continuous heat field; the squares are the unit and stay inspectable.
*   It does not use the time of a death or the round, or weight a death by anything (a Traitor kill and an Innocent's death count the same).
*   The numbers (3 deaths to split, 64 root squares, 32 to 1,024 units) are a first guess made on a data set that is small; they are constants in one place and are changed when the pictures from many rounds say so.

### 11.6 Testing
The squares add up to the deaths for any set of positions (random sets, negative positions, a death on an edge); a square splits at exactly 4 deaths and not at 3; a single death stays in the root square; many deaths in one place go down to 32 units and no further; the four children of a square are the aligned halves of it; the root size follows the area (500 m² gives 128, 6,000 m² gives 512, 30,000 m² gives 1,024, no area 512, an area of nothing 128); the colour follows density and not count; the legend and the tooltips say the size; the picture is well formed and has no script or reference; the page; a re-run of the earlier killzone tests with the new squares.

### 11.7 As built (2026-11-05)
*   `services/killzones.ts`: `squaresOf(cells, root, pick)` builds the quadtree from the finest cells, `rootSizeOf(area)` the coarsest size, `footprintOfMap` and `footprintOfPlan` the area; `KILLZONE_CELL` in `stats.ts` is now 32 (the finest square) and the query is the same `GROUP BY` as before. `Square` is `{ x, y, size, n }` (the lower corner and side in units); `hottest` lists the densest with their size. The picture draws a blob as large as its square (radius 0.75 of the side, at least 7 px) coloured by log density; the page says "squares are 64 to 512 units wide, finer where more players died".
*   The area is read from the mesh for every visitor (also an anonymous one, who is not shown the map), else from the floor plan, else 512 is used.
*   **Looked at** on the real data (the stored mesh and the 322 deaths with a position of 2026-11-05): with the same deaths, 67th Way (55 deaths, root 512) is 35 squares of 64 to 512 units, pale wide spots for the lone deaths and small red ones in the busy middle; the Airbus (30 deaths, root 128) is 20 squares of 128; Lego (31 deaths, root 512) 21 squares of 256 and 512; against 20 to 41 squares of a fixed 128 that were almost all single deaths. The numbers (3, 64, 32 to 1,024) are a first guess; they should be looked at again when the maps have a few hundred deaths each.
*   Tests: `killzone_squares.test.ts` (12) and the adapted killzone tests in `weapons.test.ts`.

## 12. Storeys a player can get to (2026-11-05)
**Why.** The maintainer asked why the killzone page of Minecraft B7 Summer offers level 2, which no player can reach. The storeys come from the stored mesh (RFC 18), cut by the heights of the floors, and nothing says that anyone can stand on one: level 2 of that map is one flat plate of 3,187 cells at exactly -800 units, 544 below the map. (Real data, the same day: it had no death on it, and level 1, a plate at -1280, has one.)

**What is listed.** A storey is listed when it has a floor a player can get to. Reaching is worked out on the flattened map (`maps/topdown.ts`, `reachableCells`) as a walk over the grid of floors:
*   **Seeds:** the map's spawn points (the mesh carries them, `spawns` in its root extras) and **every place a death was recorded on the map**. A spawn or a death lands on the highest floor at or under it in its cell (else a neighbouring cell's); one more than 600 units over any floor is in the air over a pit and is no seed. A death is proof that a player stood there, so a storey with a death on it is always listed, and what it leads to.
*   **Moves:** from a floor cell to each of the eight neighbours, on any storey, whose floor is at most `56 + cell` units higher (a jump, and a slope of 45 degrees climbs a cell's width in a cell; the cell holds the highest point of its floor) or at most `260 + cell` units lower (a fall that is walked off; more is the end of a player). Stairs and ramps between storeys are neighbouring floors at nearly the same height, so they are followed; a cliff is not climbed, a plate far under or over the map is not reached.
*   **Generous on purpose:** walls are not looked at (a thin wall in a cell would have cut a corridor and hidden a real storey); a place behind a wall counts as reachable. What the mesh cannot say, ladders, lifts and teleports, is covered by the deaths as seeds, so a storey that only those lead to appears as soon as someone dies on it; until then it shows no deaths and its picture would be empty anyway.
*   **Cost:** a pass over the grid, 0 to 50 ms for each of the 27 stored maps; the list is kept for each flattened map until a death is added.

**Where it is used.** The "Level" bar of the killzone page offers only these storeys (a level asked for in the address that is not one of them is ignored: the whole map is drawn), and the outline drawn under "All" leaves the others out, so a plate does not stretch the picture. The **replay's** "Up to level" bar offers them too (`background.listed` in the payload, which is also in its ETag); the heights of all storeys stay in the payload, because a player's level is told from them. A map without a mesh (a floor plan for an admin) lists every storey of the plan as before.

**Checked against the only real walking scan** (Diescraper, RFC 10, which found floors from -200 to about 700 units): the storeys above that (1056 to 1312 units and most of 641 to 960) come out as not reached from the spawns, and the lower four as reached. On the 27 stored maps the spawns alone reach 90 to 100% of the floors of most storeys; on a few (Kakariko, the pool, the White House basement) the spawns sit on a plateau the walk does not leave, and the storeys with deaths on them are listed through those.

**Not done.** The floors of an admin's floor plan are not used as seeds (they would be exact, since the bridge walked them); nor the positions of the replays' tracks (every place anyone walked); both would make the list better and are cheap to add.

**Tested** (`reachable.test.ts` and the map page and replay payload tests): a plate far under the map is not reached, stairs between storeys are, a cliff is not climbed, a fall of 200 is walked off and one of 500 is not, a place a death was recorded is a seed and a storey with one is listed, a seed in the air or outside the map is no seed, the spawns come with the mesh, the list is kept and follows a new death, the page offers only reachable storeys, a level asked for that is not one is the whole map, and the replay's payload names the list.

### 11.8 The filters above the picture (2026-11-05)
The Level and Weapon bars were under the picture, which is as tall as the map is long, so they moved with every map and with every change of level. They are now above it, in the same place on every map; the text of the page says "above". (The maintainer's suggestion.)
