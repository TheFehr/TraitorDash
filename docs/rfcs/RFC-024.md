# RFC 24: Where the Bodies Are

> **Status: proposed (2026-11-05); scope widened on 2026-11-06 (section 5); stages 1 and 2 built the same day (backend, bridge, both replay drawings, tests), not yet run on a real server. Stages 3 (pose) and 4 (props, weapons) are not built.** Follows RFC 12 (position tracking), RFC 14 (revivals and role changes) and RFC 22 (the lying body in the replay).

## 1. Abstract
The track records where living players are and the one place where each died. It never records where a body is afterwards. The replay therefore draws every body at its death position for the rest of the round. A body that is carried, dragged or pushed, or that slides after death, is shown in the wrong place. A revived player then appears at the place they were revived at, which looks like a teleport. This RFC adds a sparse record of body positions to the track and makes the replay draw the body where it was.

## 2. Motivation
*   A necromancer or a detective who moves a body to a safe place before reviving it is a normal play. In the replay the body stays where the player died, and the revived player appears somewhere else (noticed 2026-11-05).
*   Bodies also move without anyone's doing: a ragdoll settles for a moment after the death and can slide down a slope, and players push and shoot them. Today none of that shows.
*   Where a body lay, and whether it was moved, is part of what a round replay is for (hiding a traitor's kill, a body found by someone else than the one who killed).

## 3. What is there
*   **The track (RFC 12):** per player and segment, `x`, `y`, `z`, the view yaw and the state bits, sampled by one repeating timer for the players that are alive (`SampleAll`, `track.lua`). One extra sample is taken at the moment of death, at the position of the victim before they became a spectator (RFC 22).
*   **`deaths` and `events`** in the track body: the first carries the death positions, the second revivals and role changes (RFC 14). Both are optional and capped (`MAX_TRACK_DEATHS` 512, `MAX_TRACK_EVENTS` 1024); the backend validates them whole and refuses a track with an unknown shape.
*   **The replay (`replay.ts`):** a body is shown at `death.at` from the death until a revival (`currentDeath`), lying (RFC 22) and turned by the yaw of the last sample.
*   **The game:** TTT2 creates a `prop_ragdoll` for each death and fires `TTTOnCorpseCreated(ragdoll, player)`. `CORPSE.GetPlayer(ragdoll)` gives the player back (the stats already use it for defibrillator attempts).

## 4. Proposal

### 4.1 The bridge keeps the body
`TTTOnCorpseCreated` stores the ragdoll with the tracked player (`p.rag`). The reference is weak in practice: it is checked with `IsValid` at each use and dropped when the entity is gone or the player lives again (a revival removes the body in the game). Nothing else is kept.

### 4.2 It samples the bodies on the existing tick
The timer that samples the living players also asks each player who has a valid body for its position (`GetPos` of the ragdoll). A point is recorded only when the body is at least 16 units from the last point recorded for it; the first point is recorded when the body is first seen (this fixes the position the replay starts from, which can differ from the death position after the settling). A body that lies still therefore costs one distance check per tick and no data. A carried body is recorded at the rate of the tick, which is what the sampling interval is for. Every point is taken inside the existing time budget (RFC 12): a tick that is skipped for cost skips the bodies too, a gap and never an invented point.

### 4.3 The track carries them
A new optional list `bodies` beside `deaths` and `events`, in time order, each entry `{ p, t, x, y, z }` with the player's id, the time in whole milliseconds as in `events`, and the position in units / 8 as for the players (absolute, not differences: the list is sparse, and a plain list is easy to validate). It has its own cap (`MAX_TRACK_BODIES`, 4096), so a body being thrown around cannot use up the room of the positions, and the size estimate of the bridge counts them (about 40 bytes each); a track that reaches a cap is sent truncated as before. Nothing about the shape of the rest changes, and the field is additive: an older bridge sends none, an older backend ignores it.

### 4.4 The backend validates and decodes
Same rules as for `events`: a player who was in the round, an integer time in range and in order, integer coordinates in range, at most the cap, an unknown field refused. `decodeTrack` turns the list into a path per player (sorted by time). A pure function `bodyAt(bodies, deaths, player, t)` returns the position of the body at a time: the last point at or before `t` that is not before the death, else the death position. It is used by every consumer, so they agree.

### 4.5 The replay draws the body where it is
*   The body is placed with `bodyAt`; its height uses the same floor correction as the deaths (RFC 22: the points are the ragdoll's, not a spectator's, so no eye-height correction applies to them; the lift by the measured depth stays).
*   While a body moves it is drawn at the interpolated position between two points no more than 1.5 seconds apart, and otherwise jumps (a gap is not interpolated through).
*   A revival ring and the line from the reviver (RFC 14) are drawn at the revived player's first living position as before, and the body disappears at the revival. Because the body now stands where the revival happened, the two agree.
*   The flat drawing (RFC 19) places its cross markers the same way.
*   A track without `bodies` is drawn exactly as today.

## 5. Scope: as much as can be recorded (decided by the maintainer, 2026-11-06)
The first draft left out the ragdoll's pose, untracked bodies, props and weapons, and what was done to a body. The maintainer wants as much as can be recorded, so the RFC covers all of it, in stages of rising cost, each behind the track's time budget and its own cap:

*   **Stage 1, position and turn.** `bodies` points carry the position and the turn of the ragdoll (yaw, pitch, roll, as the entity has them), so a body lies the way it fell and is drawn that way instead of by the player's last yaw.
*   **Stage 2, what was done to a body.** New `events` kinds: `id` (identified, with who), `search` (searched, with who and whether a credit was taken), `pickup` and `drop` (carried with the magneto stick or by hand, with who), `burn` or `shot` when the game says so. These go in the same `events` list as revivals, with their own cap, and the replay writes them in the feed.
*   **Stage 3, pose.** A few bones of the ragdoll (pelvis, head, both hands, both feet) as positions relative to the root, recorded only while the body moves and only for a body that moved. The replay poses the lying model from them. Cost and size are measured before this goes on; if they are too high it stays at stage 1.
*   **Stage 4, other things that lie around.** Dropped weapons and other entities a player carries, throws or pushes (props, `prop_physics`), as a list of entities with their own keyframes, the way RFC 20 records the map's moving parts. Capped by count and by keyframes, and only entities that actually move are kept.
*   **Bodies of players who are not tracked** (bots, players who joined late) are recorded the same way, with an id made by this site (RFC 25) so no SteamID is needed.

Still not recorded: the contents of a player's inventory on the body (the game's own search screen shows it), and anything that is not an entity.

### 5.1 What stages 1 and 2 are, as built (2026-11-06)
*   **Points** are `{ p, t, x, y, z, pt, w, r }` (position in units / 8, pitch, yaw and roll in quarter degrees). The bridge records a point at the first sighting and after that when the body is 16 units or more from its last point, or has turned 20 degrees or more about any axis. The cap is `MAX_TRACK_BODIES` 4096.
*   **Hooks:** `TTTOnCorpseCreated(ragdoll, player)` for the body. `OnPlayerPhysicsPickup` and `OnPlayerPhysicsDrop` (GMod's own hooks) for `pickup` and `drop` events: only a ragdoll counts, and `of` is the dead player when `CORPSE.GetPlayer` knows. Both are unverified on a real server: whether the magneto stick of TTT2 goes through these hooks is not known. If it does not, carrying still shows as the body moving.
*   **Identified and searched** are the same act in TTT: the `found` event of RFC 26 (`TTTBodyFound`). A covert search that does not identify is not recorded.
*   **Drawing:** the replay puts the body at `bodyAt` (the last point, or between two points less than 1.5 s apart, else the death position), in both drawings. A ragdoll's yaw has no known relation to the drawn figure's, so the figure is turned by how far the ragdoll has turned since its first point; pitch and roll are recorded and not drawn until stage 3.
*   **Untracked bodies:** the backend takes only players of the round, so a body of someone who was not in the round is not recorded. Bots are in the round and are.
*   **Events:** the cap of `events` is 4352 (256 more for each of `pickup` and `drop`).

## 6. Security and privacy
*   The new field is validated in full by the same rules as the others; no name or text enters it other than the player's id, which must be one of the round.
*   It adds no information that the players do not already see in the game: a body's position is visible to everyone in it.
*   The size cap and the byte cap stay as they are; the new cap is on top of them.

## 7. Compatibility and rollout
*   Backend first (accepts and ignores nothing it does not know yet), then the bridge, then the replay: each is useful alone and none breaks an older partner. A track recorded before this has no bodies, and the replay behaves as today.
*   The bridge sends `bodies` only when there is at least one point.

## 8. Testing
*   **Bridge harness:** a body is stored on `TTTOnCorpseCreated`; a body that moves 16 units or more gives a point per tick, one that does not gives none (apart from the first); an invalid or removed ragdoll stops the sampling and does not raise; a revival drops it; the list is capped and sent in time order; a skipped tick records nothing.
*   **Backend:** `bodies` is optional; a valid list is stored and decoded; a player outside the round, a time out of order, a non-integer, a missing field and a list over the cap are refused; `bodyAt` returns the death position without points, the latest point at or before the time, and never a point from before the death.
*   **Replay:** a moved body is drawn at its last point; a gap is not interpolated; the revive ring and the body agree; a track without `bodies` is drawn as before.

## 9. Alternatives considered
*   **Put the body positions in `events`** as a new kind. The list is capped at 1024 and a carried body would use it up, and the events have a different meaning (RFC 14). A list of its own keeps the caps apart.
*   **Record the body in the player's own segment while they are dead.** That changes the meaning of a segment (the player is not there), and the revive logic depends on segments ending at the death.
*   **Sample at a fixed rate whether the body moves or not.** Simpler, but most bodies lie still for most of the round, and the cost would be paid for nothing.
*   **Derive the move from the revival** (put the body at the position of the first living sample). Cheap, but it moves the body at the end instead of along the way, and says nothing for a body that was moved and not revived.

## 10. Open questions
*   **Threshold:** 16 units is a guess. The first real rounds will show whether a body that is carried is followed smoothly enough or whether 8 is better.
*   **Which position of a ragdoll:** `GetPos` is the position of the entity's root, which for a ragdoll is close to the pelvis. If the first real rounds show an offset from the drawn body, the physics object of the pelvis is the alternative.
*   **Carried bodies and the magneto stick:** whether a body held by the player is at rest for the physics (and so reported at the right place) needs a look on a real server.

## 11. Stages
Each of the stages of section 5 is built the same way, backend first, then bridge, then replay, each with tests:
1.  **Position and turn:** the `bodies` field, validation, `decodeTrack`, `bodyAt`, the hook, the sampling, the replay in both drawings; the cost measured with `traitordash_frametime round` on a real round.
2.  **What was done to a body:** the new event kinds, the hooks, the feed.
3.  **Pose:** after the cost of stage 1 is known.
4.  **Other things that lie around:** after RFC 20's parts are proven on a real server.
5.  **Documentation:** RFC 12 and RFC 14 point here.
